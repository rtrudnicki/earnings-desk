Day ahead

Higher · low confidence
My read: US equities open sharply higher and keep part of the gap, because seven straight losses, a VIX close of 35.89 and a 10% Nasdaq week leave sellers exhausted and shorts exposed into a holiday week. I am anchoring on the futures gap in the snapshot and on the absence of any fresh negative headline over the weekend beyond what Friday already priced. The bounce has no catalyst behind it, so I would not treat a green close as evidence the unwind is over.
• Positioning: seven-day losing streak and VIX near 36 set up short covering; Nvidia, Anthropic, Micron and CoreWeave are all bid premarket.
• Rates relief: the 10Y fell 23 bp last week to about 4.92% on the haven bid, and Bessent warned Sunday against overtightening.

If he endorses Waller's half-point hike for Dec 8-9, the last hour gives back the gap; if he leans toward a quarter point with patience, the bounce holds into the close. What mattered

Tech
Nvidia falls 14% on a reported SEC accounting probe; S&P 500 enters correction
Nvidia closed Friday at $125.53, down 14.0% on the day and 22.9% on the week, its seventh straight weekly loss, after reports that the SEC is investigating its accounting. Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Nov 12. The PHLX semiconductor index fell 10.7%, Micron 13.9%, AMD 13.2%, Broadcom 12.2%; the S&P 500 closed at 6,910.39 and the Nasdaq Composite is negative for 2026. Nvidia's Nov 17 print beat estimates; I did not find the revenue or guide figures.
So what: Read: an accounting probe attacks the one number the whole AI capex chain is valued on, so a beat cannot repair the stock until the scope of the probe is known. The damage runs downstream to memory, optical and AI power names that trade as derivatives of Nvidia orders, and to IBM and INFQ only through multiple compression, not through their own fundamentals. A bounce today in the same names that fell most is mechanical; watch whether Nvidia files an 8-K on the probe.

Fed
Waller wants a half-point hike on Dec 8-9; Bessent pushes back
Governor Waller said Nov 18 that a half-point increase is probably needed in December because inflation is "headed in the wrong direction, and it's spreading out," and that the Fed may need to "shock people." His alternative is a quarter point with a clear signal for January. The Fed hiked in September for the first time in over three years and held in October. Treasury Secretary Bessent warned Sunday that overtightening could imperil the economy.
So what: Read: pricing is unsettled. CME FedWatch showed about 95% odds of a quarter-point hike after Waller spoke, while Investing.com reported this morning that traders no longer fully price one after the equity rout; I could not reconcile the two. If the selloff talks the Fed down, long-duration equities get relief; if Wednesday's core PCE (consensus +0.3% m/m) runs hot, the Fed hikes into a correction and the 10Y rally of 23 bp last week reverses. Higher-for-longer is a direct headwind to RH's deleveraging math and housing-linked demand.

Policy
Trump threatens a "tremendous tax" on US companies building AI data centers abroad
At a White House briefing Friday, Trump said the US would penalize American companies that build AI data centers overseas. He gave no rate, no list of covered companies, no start date and no legal mechanism. He paired it with a push for states to cut local deals: tax breaks, utility-bill relief or direct payments to residents.
So what: Read: this is a verbal threat, not a rule, but it hit on the worst day of the selloff and raises the cost of the Gulf and Southeast Asia capacity the hyperscalers had planned. If it becomes policy, demand shifts toward US sites that already hold power and permits, at the same moment Loudoun County has frozen new applications for 12 months. Winners would be owners of entitled, powered US land and domestic gas turbine suppliers; losers are operators with large overseas pipelines.

Liquidation
Gold fell 9% and bitcoin broke $60,000 in the same week as stocks
Gold lost about 4% Friday to roughly $3,539 and 8.9% on the week, its lowest since September 2025. Bitcoin fell below $60,000 for the first time since early July, down 7.9% on the week. Treasuries were the only haven that worked: the 2Y yield fell 25 bp on the week to 4.63%. Gold is back up about 3% this morning.
So what: Read: havens falling alongside equities is the signature of forced deleveraging, where funds sell what they can to meet margin, not a judgment on gold. One automated source cites margin-call liquidations; I found no reporting of fund gates or credit-spread stress, so treat the forced-selling story as inferred from price action. If that is right, the selling pressure is finite and the bounce has room; if gold fails to hold today's rebound, deleveraging is still running.

Energy

Take: Crude fell a third straight week on a surplus view that is now consensus, and it is lower again this morning despite a Houthi claim of a strike on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh refinery. The market is pricing oversupply over war risk until barrels are lost; the OPEC+ ministerial on Nov 29 is the next supply event.
Crude
Houthis claim a hit on the 400,000 b/d Rabigh refinery; crude slips anyway
The Houthis said on Nov 22 that they struck the Rabigh refining and petrochemical complex on Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast. Saudi authorities have not confirmed damage; the coalition said it intercepted a drone over the western region. Trump said he is inclined to order strikes on Iran next year.
So what: Read: Rabigh sits on the Red Sea coast that Saudi Arabia uses, via the East-West pipeline, to bypass Hormuz, so a confirmed outage would threaten the bypass and hit product markets harder than crude. Brent trading lower on the news says the market discounts unverified Houthi claims. Confirmation of damage is the trigger for a repricing; diesel and jet cracks would move first.
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/trump-iran-war-hormuz-saudi-.html
Crude
Third straight weekly decline: WTI down 5.2%, Brent down 3.4%
WTI settled Friday at $83.98 and Brent at $92.57, down $4.58 and $3.27 on the week. The IEA sees Q4 supply exceeding demand by more than 1.7 mb/d, China's October refinery runs fell 8.1% y/y, tanker rates are down 64% in five weeks, and US crude stocks rose 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million against an expected flat print. Single-source figures from a weekly oil newsletter.
So what: Read: the Brent-WTI spread widening to about $8.59 from $7.28 says the glut is showing up in US barrels first, which helps Gulf Coast refiners with export access and hurts inland producers. Cheaper crude is the one disinflation channel working in the Fed's favor right now.

Natural gas

Take: Nothing new over the weekend. Storage is comfortable at 80 Bcf above the five-year average after a mild November, and the snapshot's Henry Hub price sits well above the EIA's $2.87 winter forecast, so the front of the curve is carrying a weather and LNG premium the balance does not yet support.
Storage
Late-season injection: +20 Bcf to 3,882 Bcf
Working gas rose 20 Bcf in the week ending Nov 13 to 3,882 Bcf, which is 66 Bcf (1.7%) below last year and 80 Bcf (2.1%) above the five-year average. The EIA's November outlook raised its end-of-November storage forecast by 67 Bcf to 3,840 Bcf on mild weather. This week's report likely moves to Wednesday for the holiday; I did not confirm the schedule.
So what: Read: an injection in mid-November means withdrawal season has not started, which caps the upside until a cold snap arrives. LNG terminals running near capacity with international prices at their highest since December 2022 is what holds the floor.
https://natgaschart.com/storage-reports
Curve
EIA cuts its winter Henry Hub forecast to $2.87
The EIA projects a $2.87/MMBtu average Henry Hub spot price for November through March, 16% below last month's forecast and 36% below last winter.
So what: Read: a gap of roughly $0.60 between the futures price and the agency forecast is a bet on cold weather. A warm December closes the gap from the futures side, which hurts unhedged gas producers and helps gas-fired power margins.

Data centers

Take: Projects keep getting announced while the politics tighten on three fronts in one week: a threatened federal tax on overseas builds, a 12-month Loudoun County moratorium, and bills to make data centers pay more for power. No hyperscaler has cut capex guidance since the selloff; that is the thing to watch, because the equity market just withdrew its endorsement of the spend.
Trend
Loudoun County adopts a 12-month moratorium on data center applications
Loudoun County, Virginia, the largest data center market in the US, formally adopted a 12-month pause on new data center development applications. A separate federal permitting bill would force data centers to pay more for power, and Rep. Dexter of Oregon introduced a bill banning builds on federal land.
So what: Read: a freeze in the core market raises the value of sites already entitled there and pushes new demand to adjacent Virginia counties, Texas and the Midwest. Combined with the overseas tax threat, entitled and powered US land gets scarcer on both sides.

Power deal
Google signs a 50 MW geothermal PPA with Quaise in Oregon
Google and Quaise signed a 50 MW geothermal power purchase agreement supporting Google's data centers in The Dalles, Oregon. Separately, Siemens Energy said high gas turbine demand could persist through 2035.
