Data centers

Take: The constraint shifted this week from power and chips to politics: 40 of 41 local ballot measures favored limits, Texas has a governor-elect talking moratorium on top of Abbott's approval pause, and the equity market took the second-derivative names down 17% in a day. Project announcements kept coming regardless, which is the gap to watch.
Trend
Ballot box turns against the build-out; Texas becomes the swing jurisdiction
27 restrictive or oppositional measures passed Nov 3 across ten states, five outright bans, three Maine moratoriums; Grove City, Ohio set approval thresholds at 50 acres, 20 MW or 500,000 gallons a day. In Texas, Abbott halted new grid approvals in August pending an ERCOT and PUC audit, ERCOT planned to notify 'Batch Zero' large loads of their allocations by spring 2027, and governor-elect Hinojosa has pledged a moratorium until the legislature writes rules. A September poll had 56% of Texans opposed to data centers in their community, 29% in favor.
So what: Read: the binding constraint on 2028 capacity is now local consent, and consent is cheaper in jurisdictions that did not vote this week. Expect capital to route toward federal land (the DOE Paducah site), tribal land, and states with pre-emption statutes, and expect a scramble for already-entitled sites.
https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/11/06/voters-favored-limiting-data-center-development-in

Google confirmed its first Wyoming data center near Cheyenne (Project Tembo) as a $17B investment on Nov 5 and said it pays 100% of its power costs. Black Hills separately builds 564 MW of new gas and manages a 2.1 GW microgrid for the site; county filings reference 2.7 GW scaling to 10 GW, which Google has not confirmed.
So what: Read: this is what the post-ballot template looks like. Behind-the-meter gas and fuel cells, the hyperscaler paying the full power bill, a state with no measures on the ballot. The utility (AEP) gets a 20-year contracted asset; the gas producer gets a 2.1 GW load that does not touch a contested grid.

Opportunity radar

Entitled, grid-approved data-center sites in jurisdictions that just voted for limits
Buy or option already-permitted parcels with executed interconnect or will-serve agreements and approved site plans in counties and towns that passed restrictions or moratoriums on Nov 3, then sell the entitlement to an operator who now cannot get a new one. The buyer is a hyperscaler, neocloud or developer with a 2028 delivery commitment; the seller is a landowner or small developer who does not understand that the grandfathered approval is now the asset, not the land.
Why now: 27 restrictive measures passed this week, Maine and Ohio towns imposed moratoriums and 20 MW thresholds, Texas has an approval pause plus a governor-elect promising a moratorium, and analysts say projects approved through 2027 are exempt. Scarcity of consent was created overnight; the market has not repriced the grandfathered inventory yet.
Upside: Entitlement premiums in constrained power markets have run from low single-digit millions per site to well above that for 100 MW-plus approvals; a handful of positions could return multiples on a few hundred thousand dollars of option money. Realistic for a small operator because the work is county records and utility queues, not capital.
First step: Pull Ballotpedia's list of the 41 jurisdictions, then pull each county's approved-but-unbuilt data-center site plans and conditional use permits from the planning department, and cross-check against the ERCOT large-load queue and the relevant utility's will-serve letters. Rank by approval date (pre-measure), MW approved, and whether the approval survives the new ordinance's grandfather clause.
