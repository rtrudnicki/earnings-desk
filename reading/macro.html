Trade
USTR set to clear Section 301 tariffs on China, the EU and 14 other economies
Bloomberg reported on Nov 24 that USTR is about to conclude its excess-capacity investigation under Section 301 and announce plans for higher tariffs on 16 economies. They would replace the IEEPA levies the Supreme Court struck down in February 2026. Other outlets name Mexico and Japan among the targets. No rates, product lists or effective dates are published.
So what: Read: Section 301 is slower but legally sturdier than IEEPA, so this rebuilds the tariff wall on firmer ground and it will be harder to litigate away. That adds goods inflation while the Fed is hiking. NKE is the holding to watch: its sourcing is Asia-heavy and margin normalization is the thesis, so check the country list for Vietnam and Indonesia when it publishes.
https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/us-readies-tariff-hike-on-china-eu-good

Energy

Take: Crude sits near three-month lows as the Iran war premium unwinds and supply builds; Brent settled Monday at $89.37, its lowest close since Aug 31. The next catalyst is the OPEC+ meeting on Sunday, Nov 29.
OPEC
OPEC+ meets Nov 29 on 2027 policy with an IEA-projected 3.8 mb/d surplus ahead
Ministers decide whether to extend about 2 mb/d of cuts or raise quotas. The IEA says global output rose 2.3 mb/d in October to 105.4 mb/d and sees a surplus of about 2 mb/d this quarter and 3.8 mb/d in 2027. Libya's El Sharara restored about 350 kb/d. Brent is down about 22% in a month after topping $100 in October.
So what: Read: the group has to extend cuts to hold the high $80s, and Iraq's dispute over its capacity assessment makes a clean extension less certain. Goldman sees Brent at $78 by December 2027 and JPMorgan the high $70s in 1H27, so the sell side already leans lower. The risk to that view is Hormuz: Kpler has shipments at 9.5 mb/d over the 10 days to Nov 19, down 22% from October, and DBS says the talks have shown little progress.

For the week ended Nov 20, a Reuters poll expected a 4 Bcf build, Investing.com's consensus is a 19 Bcf withdrawal, and NGI models a 25 Bcf pull. Release is listed for 12:00 ET, though sources differ on the time.
