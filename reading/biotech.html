FDA approves phentolamine 0.75% (Ryzumvi) for presbyopia; Opus books a Viatris milestone and a second royalty stream
On October 16 the FDA approved the sNDA for phentolamine ophthalmic solution 0.75% in adults with presbyopia, one day ahead of the October 17 goal date. Basis: Phase 3 VEGA-2 and VEGA-3, both of which met the primary and all key secondary endpoints with no treatment-related SAEs; most common ocular reactions were conjunctival hyperemia (21%) and instillation-site irritation (16%). Viatris holds exclusive US rights under the 2022 license ($35M upfront, up to $130M in milestones, royalties) and says it is seeking a commercial partner for the presbyopia indication; no launch date was given. Opus says the approval triggers a milestone payment but did not disclose the amount.
Why it matters: This is the first label expansion of an approved product and the second indication Opus earns royalties on, after the 2023 reversal-of-mydriasis label. Read: the fact that Viatris wants a partner rather than launching itself says the presbyopia opportunity (Vuity has been on market since 2021 and under-delivered) is a slow-build consumer-pay market, not a near-term royalty engine. One level deeper

How to read a dose-finding radioligand abstract once a Phase 3 comparator exists (CATX at ESMO after BMS's ACTION-1)
Until last Monday every [212Pb]VMT-alpha-NET update was graded against Lutathera's NETTER-1 history. BMS's actinium-225 RYZ101 meeting PFS in ACTION-1 changes the yardstick: there is now an alpha emitter with a randomized PFS win in the post-Lu-177 setting, so Perspective's ORR in a single-arm dose-finding study will be read as a proxy for a PFS trial it has not run. Three traps when you open the 2396RO abstract. First, denominator drift: prior cuts quoted ORR on the first half of cohort 2 (10/23, 43%) or on the subset with SSTR2 expression in every lesion (8/16, 50%); check whether the new number is on all 46 cohort-2 patients regardless of SSTR2 status, because a drop from 43% to the high 30s on a larger, less selected denominator is regression to the mean, not a signal failure. Second, investigator-assessed versus blinded central review: ACTION-1's PFS is BICR; every VMT-alpha-NET response so far is investigator-assessed RECIST 1.1, which historically runs several points higher. Third, the dose question is really a half-life question. Lead-212 has a 10.6-hour half-life, so most of the alpha dose is delivered in the first day and renal and marrow exposure is front-loaded; actinium-225's 9.9-day half-life delivers dose over weeks and tolerates slower tumor retention. That is why 'no renal injury, no clinically significant myelosuppression at 6.0 mCi' is as important as ORR: it is the argument that Pb-212 can be given four times eight weeks apart without the cumulative kidney dose that limits Ac-225. The read-through to valuation is that the Phase 3 design due around year-end now almost has to be randomized against standard of care in a post-Lu-177 population with a PFS primary, mirroring ACTION-1, and that pushes the first registrational readout past 2028. Price the abstract on durability and tolerability, not on whether ORR ticks up or down a few points.
