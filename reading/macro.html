What mattered

Tech
Nvidia reinstates Huang; one stock moved the index 4.7%
Nvidia's board voted unanimously on Nov 23 to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and CEO, effective immediately. He resigned Nov 12 after reports of extramarital affairs, one with an alleged Chinese spy; the chairman said on Nov 17 the board would not bring him back. NVDA closed +31.5%, its best day since 2003, adding about $950B in value ($3.03T to $3.98T); Micron +19%, CoreWeave +22%. Trump said he gave the board assurances Huang broke no national security laws.
So what: Read: the rally priced out key-man risk and nothing else. The overhangs stay open: an independent investigation into the foreign-intelligence angle, a new export-control committee, and Commerce adding Singapore's Megaspeed plus six Southeast Asian firms to the entity list over chip diversion. That last item is a direct hit to NVDA's grey-channel Asia demand. A market where one governance headline swings the S&P 4.7% is a concentrated market; the same leverage works on the way down. Tuesday's premarket giveback is the first test.

Fed

NY Fed's Williams said Monday inflation is too high and he would not exclude hiking at consecutive meetings; he opposed a 50 bp move. Tomorrow's October core PCE (consensus +0.3% m/m, 3.2% y/y) decides whether the 5% tail for 50 bp grows. A 0.4% print would hit the Nasdaq harder than the Dow.

Geopolitics
Iran claims three tankers hit mines in Hormuz; crude falls anyway
The IRGC said three tankers under US escort exploded after striking naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz; Anadolu, citing Iran's navy, reported two. CENTCOM has not responded and nothing is independently verified. Treasury widened Iran sanctions the same day. Brent still traded near $89 and WTI near $80, close to three-month lows; Brent closed above $117 on Oct 30.
So what: Read: when confirmed-sounding attack headlines cannot lift crude, positioning has flipped from fear to surplus. Winners are refiners, airlines and the inflation prints two months out; losers are anyone long the war premium. The risk is asymmetric from here: a verified mining of an escorted convoy, or insurers pulling cover, reprices fast because the Dec-Jan Brent spread has collapsed from $3.67 to $0.16 and nobody is paying for prompt barrels.

Energy

Take: Crude sits near three-month lows (Brent about $89, WTI about $80) and is down roughly 24% from the Oct 30 close above $117. Supply is the driver: Libya's Sharara pipeline restarted, Americas exports are rising, and Gulf flows kept moving through attacks.
OPEC
OPEC+ meets Nov 29 on 2027 output with backwardation gone
Ministers meet Nov 29 to set 2027 policy. The Brent Dec-Jan spread fell from $3.67 in October to $0.16. Kpler data put Hormuz crude flows near 10 mb/d by Nov 10, down from 13.4 mb/d at the start of the month, after 11.1 mb/d in October.
So what: Read: a flat curve tells OPEC+ the market no longer needs prompt barrels, which argues against adding supply for 2027. Flows through Hormuz are still falling, so the price drop rests on non-Gulf supply and weak sentiment. That is a thinner cushion than the tape implies.

Data centers

Take: Demand signals are intact (HPE Cloud and AI orders more than doubled, Dell carries a $95B AI server backlog), but the constraints are shifting from chips to financing cost, power and local politics. With the 30-year near 5.3% and a Fed hike priced, debt-funded builds are the part of the story the market stopped trusting last week.
New build
SpaceX's $74B Arizona and Texas AI campuses hit local pushback
SpaceX plans about $50B in Cochise County, Arizona (6,000 MW at full build on 747 acres, $3.6B of grid upgrades, Apache Generating Station growing from about 1 GW to more than 6 GW) and more than $24B at Starbase, Texas (TCEQ air permit issued Nov 16 for 35 gas turbines, 750 MW). Cameron County voted 3-2 on Nov 17 against talks on a 10-year, 100% tax abatement; Arizona's AG asked Cochise County to postpone its Dec 9 vote.
So what: Read: the gas-first, bring-your-own-power model is now standard for anyone who cannot wait on a grid queue, and the fight has moved to who pays for water, grid upgrades and tax breaks. County votes are the new gating item. Dec 3 (AG town hall) and Dec 9 (both votes) are the dates.

Power deal

GridAI Technologies closed its all-stock purchase of Pronghorn Resources on Nov 23: about 100 MMcf/d of gross Powder River Basin gas production and more than 450 miles of pipeline, with a target of 300 MW of power delivery in 2027. No offtaker is named and the deal value was not disclosed.
