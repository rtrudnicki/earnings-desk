One level deeper

Three event-driven OS trials in one day: what a first-interim win, a futility stop and an '80th event' each tell you
Overall survival trials do not read on a calendar; they read when a pre-specified number of deaths has occurred, and the statistics at each look are set in advance. Genmab's result is the rare case: at the first interim, the OS boundary is deliberately hard to cross (an O'Brien-Fleming-type rule spends almost no alpha early), so a 'statistically significant' hit there means the observed hazard ratio was far better than the trial was powered on, which is why a 50% risk reduction is credible before the hazard ratio is even published. Geron's result is the mirror image: a DMC futility recommendation is not a failed p-value, it is a conditional-power calculation that says even if the drug performed at the assumed effect from here on, the trial would still probably miss, and in an open-label 2:1 trial against best available therapy that often reflects the control arm outliving the model (crossover, better supportive care) as much as the drug underperforming. SELLAS's announcement is neither: reaching the 80th death only fixes the information time; the fact that the last eight events took about ten months tells you survival in at least one arm is running longer than planned, which can be the drug working or the control arm being healthier than assumed, and the data cannot say which until unblinded. The analyst questions that follow are mechanical: what hazard ratio was each trial powered on and at what event count; how much alpha was spent at prior looks (REGAL's IDMC 'continue' recommendations spent some); and how wide the confidence interval will be at the final count (at 80 events a true HR of 0.70 can easily print anywhere from about 0.45 to 1.10). The trading rule that falls out is simple: never price an interim 'continue' as efficacy, treat a futility stop as information about the control arm as well as the drug, and treat an early OS win as evidence of a large effect, which is exactly why Bicara repriced before any Genmab number was shown.
