KARD
KATALYST-AV fully enrolled, but the primary read moves from Week 24 to Week 48 and topline from 1H27 to 2H27
Kardigan enrolled 156 patients in the Phase 2b of ataciguat (once-daily oral sGC activator) versus placebo in moderate calcific aortic stenosis. A blinded protocol amendment moves the primary endpoint, CT aortic valve calcium (AVC), to Week 48; Week 48 topline is now 'expected in the second half of 2027' (the August release guided 'Phase 2b topline data from the interim 24-week analysis in the first half of 2027'), with a Phase 3 plan update in 1H27 and an internal Week-24 interim retained for 'directional mechanistic trends'. Baseline AVC in the first 139 patients ranged 320 to 1,983 Agatston units. A retrospective analysis of ~45,000 US patients found baseline aortic valve area was the strongest predictor of progression (27% lost at least 0.1 cm2/year), cardiac damage on echo raised the odds, and AVC did not distinguish rapid from normal progressors. Investor webcast from AHA today, Saturday Nov 7, 8:00 am ET. The 8-K cover text says topline '2H 2026'; the press release says 2H 2027; the release is the fuller document.
Why it matters: This is a readout delay of roughly a year on the company's lead asset, dressed as an endpoint refinement. The company's own words ('more time is needed to assess a robust reduction in calcium') say the 24-week signal-to-noise was judged inadequate before unblinding, which is a legitimate reason but tells you the effect size they are powered for is small relative to scan variability. The bigger issue is the observational finding that AVC, the primary endpoint, does not track who progresses; FDA and cardiologists will want AVA, LV mass index and eventually time to valve replacement, so a Week-48 AVC win is necessary but not sufficient for the 1H27 Phase 3 design. No cash update in the release. Q3 results date is still unannounced (his estimate is Nov 10); this 8-K was not it.

When a company moves a surrogate endpoint's timing: Kardigan's Week 24 to Week 48 amendment, read as an analyst
Kardigan enrolled 156 moderate aortic stenosis patients to test whether ataciguat slows valve calcification, measured as change in CT aortic valve calcium (AVC, Agatston units). Baseline AVC in the first 139 patients ranged from 320 to 1,983, a six-fold spread, and annual AVC progression in moderate disease runs roughly 10 to 20%, so at 24 weeks the expected placebo change is small relative to scan-to-scan noise; the CEO's phrase 'more time is needed to assess a robust reduction' is a statement about variance, not about the drug. Amending the primary read to Week 48 under blinded conditions is legitimate (no unblinded efficacy was reviewed and a Week-24 internal interim survives), but it carries three costs: the topline moves from a 1H27 interim to 2H27, the trial now measures a different exposure than the one it was powered on, and any later 'directional' Week-24 disclosure is an unpowered look that management controls. The sharper problem sits in Kardigan's own ~45,000-patient observational analysis: baseline aortic valve area predicted rapid progression (27% lost at least 0.1 cm2 per year), echo evidence of cardiac damage raised the odds, and AVC did not separate rapid from normal progressors. If the surrogate does not track the variable that drives valve replacement and heart failure, a Week-48 AVC win is a mechanism result, and FDA plus cardiologists will ask for AVA, LV mass index and eventually event rates in the Phase 3 design due 1H27. The general test when any company changes surrogate timing: was the change prompted by variance or by signal, does the surrogate have a validated link to the outcome, and who holds the unblinded interim. Compare Immunocore's TEBE-AM, an event-driven overall-survival trial that slid from 'as early as end-2026' to 2Q27: that slip is mechanically the opposite case, where patients living longer delays the readout and is mildly informative about the control arm, whereas a surrogate-timing amendment is informative about measurement noise. Price the first as a calendar shift and the second as a modest cut to probability of a clinically meaningful win.
