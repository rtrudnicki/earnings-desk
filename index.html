The read

The swing. Warsh's press conference at 13:30 CT: an explicit nod to December (priced 76.5%) sends the 2-year through 4.75% and stocks lower; a data-dependent dodge gives the relief bid.

  • Goods deficit widened to $134.9B (largest since March 2025) on a record 7.5% jump in capital goods imports, a drag on tomorrow's Q3 GDP advance but a sign AI capex is still running hot.
  • Inventories built again (wholesale +0.6%, retail +0.3%), cushioning the GDP math and keeping the GDPNow update near the mid-3s.
  • Bank of Canada hiked 25 bp to 2.50%, the first in three years, citing oil and inflation near 3% through 2027: a G7 peer tightening on the same shock the Fed faces.
  • UK budget passed quietly (£15B headroom, 10-year gilt 5.34%, down 1.4 bp), removing a feared global long-end shock for today.
  • EIA crude drew 1.89M bbl against a flat consensus with Brent near $111; the oil-driven inflation story is not fading into the Fed.
  • CME FedWatch: 90.6% hold today, 76.5% hike on December 9; 10-year 5.01%, 2-year 4.75% pre-decision.

Pay attention to

  1. The GDPNow update (due after the morning data, not posted at 09:50 CT): how much the $134.9B deficit cuts from 3.4% sets the bar for tomorrow's GDP advance at 3.1-3.2% consensus.
  2. The FOMC statement at 13:00 CT for the inflation sentence and the vote count; any dissent in favour of a hike is the hawkish tell on December.
  3. Warsh at 13:30 CT on whether December is pre-committed or data dependent; the 2-year at 4.75% is the gauge, a move above it means the market took it as a signal.
  4. Brent near $111 and WTI after the draw; a push higher through the presser makes any 'transitory oil' language from Warsh harder to hold.
  5. Microsoft and Alphabet report after the close: the capital goods import record says AI capex is still accelerating, and their capex guides either confirm it or mark the top.
