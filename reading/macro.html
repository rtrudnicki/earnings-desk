Fed
Waller backs a December hike and opens the door to 50 bp, hours before CPI
At the Bank of England Watchers Conference in London on Nov 10, Governor Waller said he leans toward a 25 bp hike at the Dec 8-9 meeting even if October inflation improves, and that 50 bp "should be considered" if core CPI is "around 0.3 percent or even higher". CME FedWatch odds of a December hike were 76.8% on Nov 6 after +165,000 October payrolls; post-speech readings range from 84% to 96% by source. So what: Read: a board governor pre-committing before the data removes the dovish CPI outcome. A cool print no longer cancels December, it only caps it at 25 bp. The 2Y at 4.56% against a 3.75%-4.00% funds rate already prices two-plus hikes, so the repricing risk sits in the 50 bp tail and in equities with long-dated cash flows, not in the front end. Data centers

Take: The build-out's binding constraint moved from power to politics in one week: Texas approvals are frozen, 40 of 41 state ballot measures restricted development, and capital is repricing toward capacity that is already energized or carries its own generation. No named project broke ground or completed in the Nov 6-10 window that I could find.
Trend
Texas freeze looks set to run into 2027 under Governor-elect Hinojosa
Governor Abbott paused data center approvals at the grid operator and then at TCEQ starting in August pending an audit expected to finish by December. Hinojosa, who takes office Jan 19, 2027, pledged a moratorium on all approvals until the Legislature (convening Jan 12, 2027) passes cost, water and labor rules. Wells Fargo puts Texas at about 27% of planned US capacity; Goldman expects conditional moratoria in Texas, Georgia and Michigan.
So what: Read: the audit ending in December no longer reopens the queue, because the incoming governor has promised to extend it. Projects with Texas permits in hand gain scarcity value; projects in the queue face a delay of at least one legislative session (regular sessions run to late May). No statewide pause is law yet, so the lame-duck window between now and Jan 19 is when Abbott's agencies could still clear approvals.

Opportunity radar

Texas data center sites that already hold permits or energization just became scarce assets
Find and option Texas sites (or stalled projects) that cleared TCEQ air permits or ERCOT large-load approval before the August pause, then resell or joint-venture them to operators locked out of the queue. Buyers are neoclouds and developers with signed tenant demand and no deliverable Texas capacity before 2027-28.
Why now: Abbott's pause was supposed to end with a December audit. Hinojosa's Nov 3 win plus her pledge to freeze approvals until the Legislature acts turns a four-month pause into one that likely runs past May 2027, in a state with roughly a quarter of planned US capacity. Bill filing for the session opened Nov 9, so the terms of any grandfathering get written in the next 10 weeks.
Upside: Unknown until grandfathering is confirmed; that is the whole question. As a marker, Constellation paid about $1.17M per MW for existing gas capacity this week, and a permitted 100 MW site that saves a tenant 12 or more months is worth a multi-million-dollar premium over raw land. If approvals are not grandfathered, the angle is dead.
First step: Pull TCEQ's permit database and ERCOT's large-load interconnection status reports for approvals dated before August 2026, list the holders, and read the first filed session bills for grandfather language. Small or distressed holders (crypto miners, single-site developers) are the ones who would sell.
