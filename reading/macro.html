Data centers

Take: Two brakes tightened this week: capital (Meta's capex sold off; Nvidia noted financing strain) and local consent (Amazon walked away from a 122-acre Ashburn site as Loudoun moves toward a year-long approval pause). New builds keep coming, but they are shifting to stranded power (Texas wind, federal land, hydro) and smaller first phases.
Trend
Amazon withdraws its Ashburn campus; Loudoun pause vote next month
Announced Oct 20 as 'Project Potomac Promise': Amazon withdraws its application for a four-building, 800,000 sq ft campus on the former GWU site it bought for about $427M, forgoes legal action, donates about 25 acres (valued near $65M) for an arts center and indoor sports facility and pays $5M into the housing trust fund. The rest is rezoned mixed-use, housing predominant. The Board is installing a year-long legislative pause on data center approvals with a final vote expected in November.
So what: A hyperscaler converting entitled data center land in the largest data center market on earth to housing is the first visible retreat from Loudoun. Read: it moves the marginal NoVA megawatt to Prince William, Culpeper and Frederick MD, and it raises the value of anything already by-right with a power letter outside Loudoun.

Opportunity radar

Entitled data center land outside Loudoun just got scarcer
Parcels in Prince William, Culpeper, Fauquier or Frederick MD that already carry by-right data center zoning and a utility will-serve or capacity letter. The buyer is a hyperscaler or a developer (QTS, Vantage, Stack type) that has to place NoVA-adjacent megawatts and can no longer count on Loudoun approvals. The play is control of the entitlement, not construction.
Why now: Amazon withdrew a 122-acre Ashburn campus on Oct 20 and conceded it will not litigate; Loudoun's year-long pause on data center approvals goes to a final vote in November. Demand did not fall (Meta narrowed 2026 capex up to $135B to $145B), only the permitted supply in the core county did.
Upside: I found no current per-acre comp for entitled versus raw land in the adjacent counties, so size it yourself. Reference point: Amazon paid about $427M for 122 acres in Ashburn (about $3.5M an acre) and is now giving 25 acres away.
First step: Pull Prince William and Culpeper zoning maps for parcels inside their data center overlay districts, cross against Dominion's published transmission capacity areas, and list owners of 20+ acre parcels with no recorded data center option. That list is the asset.
