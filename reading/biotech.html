One level deeper

Two priority vouchers, one indication class: why 'first to file' does not settle 'first to market' for psilocybin
The Commissioner's National Priority Voucher compresses FDA review to a target of 1-2 months, but the clock starts only when the application is complete and accepted. A filing that needs an information request, a missing module, or a late manufacturing inspection loses its head start fast. For a Schedule I compound, approval is not launch: DEA has to reschedule. Compass's release cites an Executive Order directing DEA to a 'timely review' after positive Phase 3 data, but timely is not a defined number of days. Then comes the REMS: certified treatment sites, in-clinic monitoring for hours, and payer coding. That infrastructure, modeled on Spravato, sets uptake more than the approval date does. Label matters more than a few weeks of timing. Usona's MDD label would cover a much larger pool than Compass's TRD label, but TRD is where payers already reimburse an in-clinic, monitored product (esketamine). Practice: when a competitor's filing moves a stock 27% in a day, rebuild the model on four inputs: approval order, label population, site capacity, and price. Then check whether the move matches the change in those inputs or just the headline.
