Overnight: Oil set the tone again. What mattered

Geopolitics
Houthi strikes reach Saudi Red Sea export hub at Yanbu; tanker attacks spread past Hormuz
The Houthis claimed Monday's attack on an Aramco site in Yanbu plus the Shaybah oilfield and Riyadh and Abha airports. Kpler counts at least 18 vessel attacks in two weeks; LSEG says Iran is now targeting tankers in the Gulf of Oman. Brent rose ~1.5% to $110.38 by 03:50 GMT Wednesday; reports of Qatari and Pakistani mediation are unconfirmed, and Iran told Pakistan no talks until its demands are met (Nournews, via Reuters).
So what: Read: Yanbu is Saudi Arabia's bypass around Hormuz, so hitting it removes the market's main fallback route and justifies a higher risk premium than a Gulf-only threat. Winners: US shale, tanker owners on rates, US refiners exporting into a diesel shortage. Losers: airlines (UAL guided Q4 weak last night), chemicals, and rate-sensitive equities via inflation pricing.

Rates
Housing data breaks; long end rallies while the 2Y rises
September housing starts fell 5.2% to a 1.221 million annual rate vs 1.3 million expected; pending home sales fell 7.3% to 66.0, the lowest since the series began in 2001. The 10Y fell 3 bp to 5.12% and the 30Y fell 6 bp to 5.42%, while the 2Y rose 2 bp to 4.79%.
So what: Read: a bull flattener is the market saying the Fed (hiked to 3.75-4.00% in September after 3.7% headline CPI) keeps tightening into a slowing economy. Bad for homebuilders and building products into Thursday's PulteGroup print, and for regional bank loan growth. RH and other housing-linked retail lose the volume tailwind they need; a lower 30Y helps mortgage rates only at the margin.

Energy

Take: Crude is pricing physical supply risk, not demand: Brent sits at its highest since May on attacks against Saudi and Gulf infrastructure, and the US Energy Department says it is working with refiners globally to raise output because of a diesel shortage.
Crude
API shows another large US crude draw before today's EIA
API reported a 4.9 mb US crude draw for the week to Oct 16, with product stocks also drawing, after an 11.12 mb draw the prior week. EIA data comes at 09:30 CT today.
So what: Read: two straight large draws plus a diesel shortage mean US barrels are being pulled abroad to replace disrupted Gulf supply. That supports WTI-Brent tightening and US refining margins; a confirmed EIA distillate draw keeps diesel, and freight costs, climbing.

Natural gas

Take: Henry Hub is stuck near $3 ($3.01 on Oct 20, range $3.00-3.30) on mild weather while global LNG spikes; the US price cannot follow because export capacity is the bottleneck. Storage is above the 5-year average (3,567 Bcf after a 67 Bcf injection for the week ended Oct 9); Thursday's print is expected at 75-78 Bcf.
LNG
Asian spot LNG hits $30.4/MMBtu, highest since Dec 2022, on tanker strikes
Asian spot LNG reached $30.4/MMBtu Monday per traders; European TTF was about €86.65/MWh on Oct 20 with storage 74.47% full, below normal for the date. US LNG feedgas averaged 17.6 bcfd in October vs 17.9 in September.
So what: Read: a ~$27 spread between Henry Hub and Asia is a windfall for US exporters with uncommitted cargoes (Cheniere, Venture Global spot volumes) and makes any US LNG outage costly. US producers get little of it since feedgas is capacity-capped. Europe entering winter under-stored is the next pressure point.

Announced Oct 19: 12 X-energy Xe-100 reactors (80 MW each) at the former Breed Generating Station, Sullivan County, Indiana, operated by Indiana Michigan Power. Amazon funds development and buys first power. No cost, construction start or in-service date.
