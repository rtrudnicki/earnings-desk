Acumen's oligomer antibody sabirnetug fails ALTITUDE-AD; program ended
The 542-patient Phase 2 missed the primary iADRS endpoint at 18 months at both 35 mg/kg and 50 mg/kg, and missed CDR-SB and other key secondaries with no meaningful separation from placebo (October 30). Amyloid PET SUVr fell significantly at weeks 36 and 76 at both doses; ARIA-E was about 10%. Read-through: This is the cleanest test yet of the soluble-oligomer hypothesis as a differentiated mechanism, and it says the clinical signal in Alzheimer's tracks how much plaque you remove, not what species you bind. One level deeper

Why a biomarker win and a clinical miss is the default outcome, not a surprise: reading ALTITUDE-AD
Acumen's release leads with a statistically significant drop in amyloid PET at weeks 36 and 76 and a 10% ARIA-E rate, then reports that 542 patients showed no separation on iADRS or CDR-SB at 18 months. The trap is treating the biomarker as proof the mechanism worked. In anti-amyloid Alzheimer's the clinical effect has tracked the magnitude and speed of plaque removal: lecanemab (CLARITY AD, n=1,795) and donanemab (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2, n=1,736) both pushed most patients below roughly 25 centiloids within 12 to 18 months and bought 22% to 29% slowing on their primary scales, and both paid for it with ARIA-E of about 13% and 24%. A 10% ARIA-E rate is itself a tell that sabirnetug removed less plaque, because ARIA scales with clearance. The oligomer hypothesis predicted the opposite: a bigger clinical effect from binding soluble species with less plaque disruption. So the question to ask when a company says the biomarker moved is whether that biomarker is the causal lever in the comparators' data or a bystander the drug happens to touch. Second, check power against the effect the mechanism implies: 542 patients over 18 months can only detect an effect at least as large as the approved drugs produced in trials three times the size, so a modest true benefit would read as a miss anyway. Third, when ARIA, PET and the clinical scale all point to a smaller plaque effect than the comparators, a miss is the base case and the stock should be priced on cash before the readout, not after. The same test applies to any SMID that reports target engagement before efficacy: write down what magnitude of biomarker change the winners needed, then ask whether the trial could even see it.
