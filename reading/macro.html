Day ahead

Higher · low confidence
A modest bounce is the base case: futures were up 0.3% to 0.9% premarket, crude is down 4% on the Doha talks, and the Nasdaq just lost 5% in a month, so there is oversold tech to be bought. Size stays light into Apple and Palantir after the close and the midterms tomorrow, so the bounce is more likely to fade into the afternoon than extend. I am anchoring on the oil move and the overnight futures, not on any change in the AI capex debate, which Friday did not settle.
• Oil relief: WTI back under $100 on the Qatar two-day Hormuz plan, which cuts the inflation tail the Fed hawks are leaning on.
• Mean reversion after a 1.82% S&P / 2.86% Nasdaq day and a 5.2% Nasdaq October; the capex-punished names (AMZN, GOOGL, INTC) are where the bounce lives.
• Event risk caps it: ISM manufacturing at 09:00 CT with a 54.7 consensus, Apple and Palantir after the close, midterms Tuesday.
Swing factor: An Iran headline. If the Doha plan stalls or Trump signals post-midterm strikes, crude retraces its 4% drop and equities give back the bounce; a confirmed reopening timeline pushes crude toward $95 and extends the rally. Second swing is the 09:00 CT ISM: a hot headline or prices-paid print hardens the December hike (already 75% to 85% priced) and turns the day lower.

What mattered

Tech
Friday's rout was about who pays for 2027 capex, not about AI demand
Amazon reported Q3 cash capex of $75.9B (GAAP PP&E purchases $77.3B vs $58.7B FactSet consensus), EPS $1.89 vs $1.96, held 2026 capex at about $220B and said 2027 will be meaningfully higher; AWS grew 39% to about $46B. Alphabet fell 10% after holding 2026 capex at $195B to $205B and guiding a significant increase in 2027. Intel fell 20% to 23% after guiding Q4 revenue to $15B to $16B vs $17B expected and its CFO said it is probably not going to have positive free cash flow next year. S&P 500 -1.82% to 7,580.07, Nasdaq -2.86% to 25,437, Nasdaq -5.2% for October.
So what: Read: the market has stopped paying for capex ambition and started paying for capex discipline. Microsoft, which cut its calendar-2026 capex to $170B from $175B on GPU supplier delays, was punished least. The next casualty is the funding chain: hyperscaler free cash flow is now the binding constraint, so 2027 spend leans harder on bonds and off-balance-sheet JVs, which is where spreads start to matter. Neoclouds and second-tier GPU buyers lose first; names with cash and no capex story get the rotation.

Geopolitics
Qatar's two-day Hormuz plan is a proposal, and shipping is still being hit
Iran's foreign minister Araghchi said Qatar-mediated talks in Doha produced a plan in which the US acts on day one and Iran reopens Hormuz on day two; Iran's conditions include sanctions relief, restored oil waivers and a smaller US presence. Trump said Saturday the US still has one or two targets to take care of in Iran, naming the Pickaxe Mountain site, while saying he is in no hurry to resume strikes; Axios reports Netanyahu wants strikes immediately after Tuesday's midterms. At least 10 shipping incidents since October 31, including Nakilat's Q-Max LNG carrier Al Mayeda hit by unidentified objects on October 30. OPEC+ held December targets on Sunday; most Gulf members are already below quota.
So what: Read: crude's 4% drop prices the deal, not the attacks. The binary resolves Wednesday morning, after the vote, and the asymmetry is to the upside in oil because the plan needs US concessions Washington has not signaled. Refiners and tanker owners keep the war premium either way; airlines and chemicals are the trade if the reopening is confirmed. OPEC+ is irrelevant until Gulf barrels can actually leave the Gulf.

Fed
December is a hike, not a cut, and the 10Y is sitting near 5%
The FOMC held at 3.75% to 4.00% on October 28 by 10-2, with Cook and Hammack dissenting for a 25bp hike. CME FedWatch had December hike odds at 85.7% on October 28 and about 76% on October 30; Trading Economics shows roughly 75% today with the 10Y at 4.93%. Today's ISM at 54.7 consensus is the first data point that can move the hike odds; a soft print is the only path to a bid in duration this week before Friday's 55k payroll consensus.

Crude
WTI below $100 on Doha, Brent-WTI spread near $8.80
WTI fell more than 4% to about $99.60 and Brent about 3% toward $110 after Araghchi said the Qatar plan could reopen Hormuz on day two of implementation. OPEC+ kept December targets unchanged on November 1. Brent-WTI spread about $8.84. Goldman is reported by AP to have raised its December Brent forecast to $105 from $90; earlier Goldman notes in the search carried $90, so treat the $105 figure as single-sourced.
So what: Read: an $8.80 Brent-WTI spread says the shortage is seaborne, not North American, which keeps US Gulf Coast refiners and exporters earning the arbitrage until the strait is actually open. A confirmed reopening compresses that spread fast; US crude exporters lose first, Asian refiners win.

Natural gas

Take: Henry Hub December sits near $2.90 after an 80 Bcf injection took storage to 3,724 Bcf, 38 Bcf above the five-year average, and the mild forecast is doing the rest; the next EIA print is Thursday November 5. The global LNG market is the opposite tape: Qatar lost 17% of its export capacity to the March strikes and its carriers are now being hit, which supports US export netbacks without lifting Henry Hub.
Storage
First above-average build in 11 weeks
EIA reported an 80 Bcf injection for the week ended October 23, above expectations, taking inventories to 3,724 Bcf, 38 Bcf above the five-year average and ending a 10-week run of below-average builds. December futures settled $2.898 Friday, down 5.4 cents; LNG feedgas is running 19 to 21 Bcf/d depending on the source. The March 2027 over April 2027 premium hit a record low of about 5 cents.
So what: Read: a 5-cent March-April spread says the curve sees no winter scarcity at all. The only bull case left is weather, and the first cold forecast of November will move the front more than any storage print. Producers who curtailed in Q3 have no reason to bring volumes back at $2.90.

LNG
Qatari LNG carrier hit while Ras Laffan runs at 83% of pre-war capacity
Nakilat's Q-Max carrier Al Mayeda was struck by unidentified objects on the evening of October 30 in Gulf waters; no injuries, ship reported operational. Iranian strikes in March damaged Ras Laffan trains 4 and 6, removing about 12.8 million tonnes a year, roughly 17% of pre-war output, with repairs first estimated at three to five years. Al Jazeera counts at least 29 ships hit in October. Iran International reports Qatar is signing long-term US LNG supply contracts to cover its shortfalls.
So what: Read: Qatar buying US LNG is the clearest signal yet that the Gulf export gap is multi-year, not a headline. That is a bid for US Gulf Coast liquefaction FIDs and brownfield trains, and it widens the gap between the Henry Hub price and the value of US export capacity. The loser is Europe and Asia on the spot curve, where TotalEnergies already guided higher prices through year-end.

Data centers

Take: The build-out is still accelerating on paper and slowing in the ground: all four hyperscalers guided 2027 capex higher, yet Microsoft and Alphabet both said GPU supplier delays pushed usable capacity into 2027, and the market punished the spenders by 7% to 10%. No gigawatt-scale US groundbreaking landed in the window; the only confirmed start is a 60MW Samsung SDS site in Korea.
Capex
Four hyperscalers now guide roughly $720B to $740B for 2026 and more in 2027, with supply the constraint
Alphabet Q3 capex $51.4B, 2026 held at $195B to $205B, 2027 up significantly; CFO said external capacity came online only at the very end of Q3. Microsoft Q3 capex and finance leases $45.2B (+30% y/y), calendar-2026 cut to $170B from $175B on outside GPU supplier delays deferring capacity into 1H 2027. Meta Q3 $34.91B, 2026 narrowed to $135B to $145B, no 2027 number. Amazon Q3 cash capex $75.9B, 2026 about $220B, 2027 meaningfully higher.
So what: Read: the bottleneck has moved from power and permits to GPU delivery, which means 2026 dollars slip into 2027 and stack on top of the increases. That is bullish for the construction and power chain over a longer horizon and bearish for anyone whose 2026 revenue depended on those racks landing on time (neoclouds, colocation leases with delivery milestones). The funding gap also grows: Amazon's quarterly outflow was about $42B.
