One level deeper

What a priority voucher does and does not do to COMP360's clock
This is analysis, not reported fact. Three things get conflated: breakthrough designation, priority review, and the commissioner's voucher. Breakthrough buys meetings and a rolling submission; it does not shorten the review. Priority review is a PDUFA commitment, and for a new molecular entity the six-month goal runs from the 60-day filing date, not from submission, so an NDA completed Nov 9 has a filing date near Jan 8, 2027 and a goal date near July 8, 2027. The voucher was a pilot run from the commissioner's office that promised one to two months; it has no statutory clock, so when the sponsor of the program leaves and Reuters reports career staff are back in charge of timelines, the fallback is the PDUFA goal. For a Schedule I drug there is a second clock: by statute DEA must issue an interim scheduling rule within 90 days of approval, and states then conform their own schedules (management says 93% of the population sits in states committed to act within 30 days). Stack those and an on-time ordinary review means first commercial dosing in roughly September to November 2027, against company guidance of 1H 2027. The lesson for judgment: when management quotes a timeline, ask which legal clock backs it. A date backed by a PDUFA goal is a commitment FDA meets about 90% of the time; a date backed by a pilot program is a hope. Price the first and treat the second as upside.
