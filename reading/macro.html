Geopolitics
BIS chip rule takes effect Thursday: licenses for five countries and for rented compute
Commerce issued an interim final rule on November 16, effective November 19, requiring licenses to ship advanced AI chips to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, and covering data centers that offer more than 12 million total processing performance to China-headquartered tenants; facilities already serving large Chinese customers get 90 days to apply. It follows the Megaspeed case, about $4.8B of Nvidia hardware bought over three years. China said it will take necessary measures; the Stoxx 600 fell 1.8% on Tuesday.
So what: Read: this closes the cloud-rental loophole, so the hit lands on Southeast Asian colocation operators and neoclouds with Chinese tenants more than on Nvidia, whose guide already assumes zero China compute. US-operated data centers in Singapore and Malaysia are reported as exempt, which tilts regional share toward US hyperscalers. The open risk is Chinese retaliation while Trump is in Shenzhen for APEC talks with Xi; rare earths and Nvidia's antitrust file are the obvious levers.

Energy

Take: Crude is falling on inventories and a returning surplus even as the Gulf conflict escalates: Brent settled up 0.76% at $93.14 on Tuesday, then gave it all back overnight. Both benchmarks are on track for a 20% drop this month, from above $100 Brent a month ago.
Crude
API shows a 5.28M barrel crude build and a 5.12M gasoline build
API data for the week to November 13 showed crude +5.28M barrels (after +9M the prior week), gasoline +5.12M and distillate +351k. At 4:20 a.m. ET Brent was down 1.2% at $91.99 and WTI down 2% at $84.34; the Brent/WTI spread hit a two-week-plus high, and Sparta Commodities says the WTI export arbitrage to Asia and Northwest Europe is shut.
So what: Read: barrels are backing up on the US Gulf Coast, so WTI has to cheapen relative to Brent until exports clear. That is negative for US producer realizations and supportive for Gulf Coast refiner margins. EIA's official numbers today either confirm or cancel this.

Geopolitics
Hormuz: ADNOC vessel attacked, US strikes Qeshm Island, Houthis target Saudi bypass pipeline
On Tuesday an ADNOC L&S vessel was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz (one crew missing, 11 injured), Iranian drones hit a US radar site in Bahrain (third strike in five days, no damage per CENTCOM) and the US struck a missile launch site on Qeshm Island. The Houthis claimed five drones at two pumping stations on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline; the IRGC says about six tankers struck mines on Sunday and Monday. Oman and Qatar continue mediating.
So what: Read: the market is treating each strike as noise because exports from the region are rising and freight rates are falling. The one item that would change that is real damage to the East-West line, since it is the Saudi route around Hormuz. Crude selling off on bad news is how a market behaves when it is long barrels, and it sets up a sharp reversal if a pumping station is confirmed down.

Natural gas

Take: December gas has given back most of last week's squeeze, from a $3.34 settle on November 12 to about $3.10 on Tuesday and $3.19 this morning, as forecasts turned warmer for November 18 to 22. Record LNG feedgas is the floor; a storage surplus and 114.7 Bcf/d of production are the ceiling.
Storage
Thursday's EIA print: early models point to a build of about 24 Bcf
Last week's report showed +61 Bcf vs a 40 Bcf five-year average and a 67 Bcf consensus, taking stocks to 3,862 Bcf, 75 Bcf (2.0%) above the five-year average and 92 Bcf below last year. One early estimate for the week ended November 13 (which included the November 12 to 13 nor'easter) is +24 Bcf; that is a single source, not a survey.
So what: Read: a build near 24 Bcf would be the smallest of the season and roughly holds the surplus; anything above 30 Bcf widens it past 80 Bcf with warm weather ahead and the December contract expiring November 25. Speculators were net short 227,227 contracts as of November 10, so a low number can still squeeze.

LNG
Feedgas record of 21.8 Bcf/d; Qatar force majeure extended to at least end of December
LNG feedgas hit a record 21.8 Bcf/d on November 14 (+12% y/y), about 19% of Lower 48 output of 114.7 Bcf/d, with a pipeline-delivery record at Golden Pass. QatarEnergy extended its force majeure to at least the end of December; TTF was about EUR 91/MWh on November 13 (roughly $29/MMBtu, about 9x Henry Hub) and EU storage is 71% full vs an 89% five-year average.
So what: Read: the global gas shortage is not reaching Henry Hub because US export capacity is already full; the spread accrues to whoever holds liquefaction capacity and uncontracted cargoes, not to US producers. EIA's November STEO cut its November to March Henry Hub forecast to $2.87. April 2027 at $2.33 shows the curve expects the surplus to return after winter.

Data centers

Take: Demand signals are intact: Nvidia's data center revenue grew 21% in one quarter and its Q4 guide implies more. The new development is policy, with the BIS rule effective Thursday putting Southeast Asian builds that depend on Chinese tenants under a license regime. No hyperscaler capex revision or large named US project broke overnight.
Trend
Chip rule puts Southeast Asia capacity for Chinese tenants under license
From November 19, data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil offering more than 12 million total processing performance to China-headquartered customers need a BIS license, with 90 days to apply for existing facilities. CNBC reports the rule puts the region's build-out at risk; US-operated facilities in Singapore and Malaysia are reported as not covered.
So what: Read: Johor and Batam projects underwritten on Chinese AI demand face financing questions first, since lenders will not fund a campus whose anchor tenant needs a license it may not get. Capacity demand does not vanish; it shifts to US-operated sites and to the Gulf and India.
