Data centers

Take: The constraint moved from megawatts to permits this week: voters went against data centers in 40 of 41 local measures, Texas's governor-elect wants a moratorium on approvals, and OpenAI, Google and Microsoft are now publicly pledging to pay their own grid costs. Capex has not turned down, but the mix is shifting to behind-the-meter power (fuel cells, on-site gas) and already-entitled sites, and the first hyperscaler walk-aways from leased capacity are being reported.
Trend
40 of 41 local data-center ballot measures went against the industry; Texas governor-elect Hinojosa wants an approvals moratorium
Across 10 states on Nov 3, voters favored restrictions or rejected pro-development proposals in 40 of 41 local measures (CNBC/Ballotpedia). The lone exception, Harwood ND, rejected a review-and-hearing ordinance 76-24. Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa takes office Jan 19 and wants a moratorium on data center approvals (24/7 Wall St). Morgan Stanley estimates 4.9 GW of US capacity faces permitting risk by 2028, rising to 12 GW by 2030 (secondary citation).
So what: Read: entitled, powered, grandfathered sites just became scarcer than GPUs. A Texas moratorium needs the legislature, which a Democratic governor does not control, so treat it as a stated intent, not a rule. Still, every month of uncertainty raises the value of approvals already granted and pushes operators toward on-site generation that skips the interconnection fight.
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/data-center-ballot-measures-2026-midterm.html
Power deal
OpenAI pledges to pay for power and grid upgrades at campuses it owns; Effingham GA contract is 3.2 GW with a 1,000 MW curtailment right
On Nov 5 OpenAI published a National Community Compact and Compute Campus Policy Blueprint: for owned-and-operated campuses it will pay for electricity and grid infrastructure and post financial guarantees before major spending; no dollar figure, no legal force. Its Effingham County, GA campus has a Georgia Power contract for 3.2 GW delivered 2028-2032, with the utility able to cut up to 1,000 MW at peak. The pledge is silent on leased sites (SB Energy's Ohio campus, Oracle's Abilene). Google pays 100% of power at its $17B Wyoming campus with 702 MW of on-site fuel cells; Amazon pledged $1B over five years on Oct 2.
So what: Read: ratepayer politics is now a line item in hyperscaler capex, and curtailment clauses mean nameplate GW overstate firm compute. Utilities with regulated rate bases (Georgia Power, Black Hills) win; the risk shifts to the tenant. Watch whether the leased-campus gap gets closed, because that is where Oracle and the neoclouds carry the exposure.

Opportunity radar

Entitled, grid-approved data-center sites become the scarce asset if the permit backlash hardens; Texas is the test case
Acquire or option already-permitted, large-load-approved parcels (county approvals plus an ERCOT or utility large-load interconnection position, or an on-site gas generation permit) in the Texas and inland corridors before any moratorium or review-ordinance wave is written, then sell the entitled position to a hyperscaler, neocloud or developer who can no longer get a fresh approval. The buyer pays for the time and certainty, not the land.
Why now: Voters went against data centers in 40 of 41 local measures on Nov 3; Texas governor-elect Hinojosa wants a moratorium on approvals from Jan 19; OpenAI and Google now pledge to pay 100% of power and grid costs, which makes a site with its own generation permit worth more; Microsoft is declining incremental leased MW, so capital is moving to fewer, better-entitled sites. Morgan Stanley puts 4.9 GW of US capacity at permitting risk by 2028.
Upside: Read, not a number found: entitled-land premia in prior scarcity regimes (cell towers, 2010s-era wind interconnection queue positions) ran to multiples of raw-land value, and ING's $500M sale of 12 data-center loans with bids due Nov 19 says some current holders are forced sellers. Realistic prize for a small operator is a single-site flip measured in low millions on an option costing tens of thousands, if the moratorium talk becomes an ordinance and the site sits inside an existing approval.
First step: Pull the list of Texas county conditional-use and site-plan approvals for data centers granted in 2025-26 (Ellis, Navarro, Taylor, Williamson counties as a start) and cross it with the ERCOT large-load interconnection queue; identify owners of approved-but-unbuilt parcels and the expiry dates on their approvals. Separately, track Hinojosa's transition agenda and the 2027 legislative session filings for any data-center moratorium or review bill, because that language, not the ballot measures, decides whether this trade exists.
