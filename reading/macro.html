What mattered

Tech
The market stopped paying for AI capex and started charging for it
Alphabet posted Q3 free cash flow of -$9.07B (consensus +$2.7B) on $51.4B capex, missed EPS ($2.67 vs $3.02) and fell 13% Thursday, its worst day ever. Amazon reported FCF of -$42.4B, EPS $1.89 vs $1.96, held 2026 capex at $220B and said spending rises again in 2027; Intel guided Q4 revenue to $15.0B to $16.0B vs $17.38B consensus with gross margin falling to 38% from 44%, and closed down 28.6% Friday, its worst day since 1974. The S&P fell 1.8% for its worst day since June.
So what: Read: negative FCF at two of the four hyperscalers means 2027 capex gets funded with debt and leases, not operating cash, so credit spreads on hyperscaler paper and their power counterparties become the tell. Nvidia (-4.2%) and the chip-equipment names in Tokyo (Advantest, Tokyo Electron) are now the derivative of capex guidance, and the next data point is AMD Tuesday. Intel's PC-weakness guide is a consumer read, not an AI read; the market sold it as both.

Geopolitics
Hormuz tanker fires and Houthi claims on Saudi sites put Brent at a six-month high as Trump says the war ends soon
Iran's IRGC said three to four tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz Friday, including a supertanker carrying over 2 million barrels that it says hit a mine; UKMTO independently confirmed one crude tanker struck by a projectile with crew safe. Houthis claimed strikes on Ras Tanura, Abqaiq and Rabigh (no Saudi confirmation of damage; coalition reported interceptions). December Brent settled +$2.62 at $117.73. Trump said the war will end soon one way or another; Qatari mediators held talks in Tehran, Iran is due to answer a draft this weekend, and one report says Washington is readying a possible strike after Tuesday's midterms.
So what: Read: this is the single largest two-sided gap risk into Monday. A deal takes $10 to $15 out of Brent on the headline alone given a six-month high built on transit risk; a rejection plus a post-election strike keeps refining margins at records and keeps the Fed hawkish. Refiners and integrateds (Exxon now earns more from refining than upstream) are the only equities with a positive carry into either outcome. The tanker-count and Saudi damage claims are Iranian and Houthi sourcing; treat the specifics as unverified.

Energy

Take: Crude is trading the Hormuz headline premium (six-month high, covered above) while refining captures the margin: the majors just reported refining out-earning upstream. OPEC+ meets Sunday November 1 with the group already pumping below quota, so a hold changes nothing for supply.
OPEC
OPEC+ eight-country group meets Sunday with output already short of quota
The October 4 meeting kept November targets unchanged for a second month; the seven core members pumped about 25 million b/d in August, well below prewar levels and below quota. Analysts expect no quota changes before 2027 because the war delayed the capacity review that sets 2027 baselines.
So what: Read: quotas are not the binding constraint, transit and damaged infrastructure are, so Sunday's decision is noise. The price-relevant OPEC variable is whether Gulf producers can physically ship, which depends on the Iran talks, not the communique.
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/04/opec-agrees-to-keep-november-oil-output-targets-steady.htm
Products
War-driven refining surge shows up in major-oil earnings
Exxon reported refining earnings above upstream for the first time in at least six years; Reuters attributes the surge to attacks on refineries in Russia and the Middle East pushing up diesel prices. Chevron revenue rose 36% to $67.8B.
So what: Read: product cracks, not crude, are the inflation transmission into the Fed's numbers. If a deal lands, cracks fall slower than crude because the damaged refining capacity does not come back on the headline.

Natural gas

Take: The December contract fell on its front-month debut Thursday and again Friday on milder forecasts, with storage 38 Bcf above the five-year average after an 80 Bcf build. Nothing structural changed; the market is waiting on November weather and the LNG pull.
Storage
80 Bcf build puts working gas 38 Bcf over the five-year average
EIA reported an 80 Bcf injection for the week ended October 23, taking working gas to 3,724 Bcf, 38 Bcf above the five-year average of 3,686 Bcf and 147 Bcf below last year. The surplus grew from 35 Bcf the week before.
So what: Read: a growing surplus into November with mild forecasts caps the front; the year-on-year deficit only matters if cold arrives before December. The curve is pricing weather, not supply risk.

Data centers

Take: The build-out is still accelerating on the numbers (Alphabet guiding above $300B for 2027, Amazon above $220B) while the equity market has flipped to treating that spend as a liability. The binding constraint remains hardware and power supply, not budgets: Microsoft is the only hyperscaler cutting 2026 capex and did so because GPU suppliers are late.
Capex
Alphabet signals 2027 capex above $300B after a $51.4B quarter
Q3 capex was $51.36B against $42.29B operating cash flow; 2026 guidance held at $195B to $205B, with management signaling annual capex topping $200B and then exceeding $300B in 2027. Google Cloud revenue roughly doubled to about $30.7B (verify against the filing).
So what: Read: a $300B year with negative FCF means the data center pipeline gets financed with debt and leases, which shifts counterparty risk onto developers, utilities and lenders. Power procurement commitments will not slow; who funds them changes.
