One level deeper

Reading eGFR slope in IgAN: why every drug shows a 5-point win and what separates them
FDA accepts total eGFR slope over two years as a surrogate for kidney failure, and the Kidney Week prints show why the number flatters everyone: telitacicept -0.6 versus -5.7, atacicept -0.6 versus -5.6, sibeprenlimab +0.3 versus -4.2 mL/min/1.73m2 per year. The size of the win is set mostly by the placebo arm, so a trial that enrolls high-risk patients (UPCR above 1 g/g, falling eGFR at entry, limited SGLT2 inhibitor use, as in a China-only cohort) manufactures a steeper control decline and a bigger difference; compare baseline UPCR, baseline eGFR and background therapy before comparing slopes. Mechanism matters for the shape of the curve: endothelin and RAS agents (sparsentan, atrasentan) drop eGFR 3 to 5 points in the first weeks for hemodynamic reasons, so their total slope absorbs an acute dip while the chronic slope from month three is what reflects structural protection; anti-APRIL and complement agents have no acute dip, so total and chronic slope coincide and the headline number looks cleaner. Event endpoints (confirmed 30% or 40% decline, dialysis) are more convincing than slope but rest on handfuls of events: TELIGAN's 2.5% versus 16.4% is roughly 4 versus 26 patients, which is why the hazard ratio of 0.11 carries a 0.04 to 0.30 interval. Proteinuria at 9 months earns accelerated approval; it does not predict which agent holds eGFR best, which is where Roche's sefaxersen sits today with a 43% UPCR cut and a blinded week-105 slope still to come. When two drugs post the same slope, the differentiators are dosing cadence, injection-site burden (56% with telitacicept), infection rate and price, not efficacy. Analyst habit to build: never quote a slope difference without the placebo slope and the baseline UPCR beside it.
