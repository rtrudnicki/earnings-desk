What mattered

Geopolitics
Gulf escalates on three fronts: Houthi strikes on Saudi oil, IRGC tanker claims, NYT reports post-midterm US-Israel strike plans
Drone debris near a processing plant at Saudi Arabia's Shaybah field killed two and injured two on Wednesday; the Houthis also struck a Saudi airport. The IRGC Navy claimed four tankers were hit by Iranian forces in the northern Gulf and three vessels struck mines west of Hormuz (unverified, Reuters-relayed). The US ordered non-essential government personnel out of Saudi Arabia. The NYT reported US and Israeli militaries have drawn up a short, intense campaign against Iranian nuclear, missile, drone and energy targets after the Nov 3 midterms, with assets in place by early November and no decision taken; the White House said it does not comment on plans that 'may or may not exist.' Brent traded $111 to $112, WTI near $99.
So what: This is a security premium, not a barrel shortage: Kuwait says output is back above 2 mb/d and Saudi exports via Hormuz and the Red Sea are 'largely restored.' The transmission channel is freight and insurance, then inflation expectations into an FOMC that already carries hike odds. Iran's stated retaliation list per the reports includes Saudi refineries, data centers and undersea cables, which puts Gulf cloud regions and Red Sea cable routes on the risk map. Refiners with crude access outside the Gulf (US Gulf Coast) and tanker owners win; Asian importers (Korea, Japan, India) and airlines lose. Read: the market will price a post-midterm strike as a scheduled binary, which keeps the oil curve bid through November regardless of flows.

Data centers

Take: Capex is still rising (Meta's 2026 floor went up $5B to $135B, Tesla is adding 170 MW of training compute by December) but power is now the binding constraint, and this week showed both ends of it: Microsoft walked away from an Essex campus the UK grid cannot feed until the 2030s, while the deals getting signed are 20-year utility PPAs and behind-the-meter gas.
Power deal
Microsoft exits Nscale's Loughton, Essex site after the grid connection slipped from end-2026 to the 2030s
Per the Telegraph, Reuters and the Guardian (Oct 18-19), Microsoft terminated its anchor-tenant agreement for Nscale's Loughton campus, where it was due to rent 23,040 Nvidia GB300 GPUs from Q1 2027. UK Power Networks reportedly cannot connect the site before the early-to-mid 2030s. Microsoft and Anthropic account for ~85% of Nscale's contracts per its IPO filing; Nscale is preparing a US listing of up to $3B at a reported valuation of up to $35B. Nscale says it is 'exploring alternative power sources' and that its Microsoft relationship remains strong.
So what: This is the first named case of a hyperscaler cancelling a signed tenancy over grid timing alone. The read-across is that a site without secured power is worth close to nothing to a tenant with a 2027 deployment date, and that neocloud IPO revenue backlogs need to be haircut for power risk. Expect Nscale and peers to pivot to on-site gas, which is where Enerflex's >1 GW behind-the-meter award (Oct 13) already points.
https://www.businessday.co.za/world/international-companies/2026-10-18-microsoft-pulls-out
Power deal
Microsoft signs a 20-year, 1,100 MW supply agreement with NiSource for an Indiana campus, starting 2028
NiSource announced the agreement on Oct 16: about 1,100 MW added to its generation pool, 20 years from 2028, serving an Indiana data-center campus, with Microsoft paying for all generation and transmission needed for the site. Generation type and dollar value not disclosed.
So what: The template is now utility-built, tenant-paid generation under long contracts, which shifts rate-base growth to regulated utilities in states with land and gas (Indiana, Texas, Ohio) and away from merchant power in constrained grids. For a utility, a 1.1 GW single-customer load is a decade of rate-base growth in one signature; the regulatory fight will be over who pays for the stranded risk if the tenant leaves, which is exactly what Loughton just demonstrated can happen.
