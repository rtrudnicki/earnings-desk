Credit
Brazil repriced in one session after Lula won the runoff 51.79% to 48.21%
The Bovespa closed down 13.88% at 176,608 on Monday, the worst day since March 2020, after a circuit breaker halted trading for 30 minutes at an intraday low near 169,516. The dollar closed up 8.79% at 5.4154 reais. Bradesco fell 30%, Itau 21%, Nu Holdings 18%, Petrobras more than 10%, and XP roughly halved. So what: Local banks took the hit because the market is pricing looser fiscal policy and tighter state control, which means higher Selic for longer and a weaker real. Read-through is to EM FX funding and to US-listed Brazil exposure (EWZ, NU, ITUB, BBD); a contested result keeps the risk premium open rather than letting it mean-revert. Commodity exporters priced in dollars (Vale, Petrobras) get a currency cushion the banks do not.

Anthropic filed its S-1: $16.3B of first-half revenue, Nasdaq ticker ANT, listing as soon as November 16
Revenue for the six months to June was $16.3B versus $4.6B for all of 2025. Net loss through June was $80.7B, of which $72.4B was a non-cash fair-value charge on convertibles and SAFEs. Tech
Meta upsized to $25B of common at $625 plus $12.5B of mandatory convertible preferred, first leg of a $95B equity plan
The common is sold under forward sale agreements, so Meta receives no cash until settlement (one source says by November 19, 2027). The preferred is 250 million depositary shares at $50 each. Underwriters hold 30-day options on another $3.75B of common and 37.5 million depositary shares. A $60B at-the-market program follows. Nvidia is taking $3B of the deal and Meta says it will expand deployment of Vera Rubin and Vera CPUs. Both legs close October 29. The stock fell about 9.4% last week on the announcement.
So what: Hyperscaler capex is now being funded with equity rather than free cash flow or bonds, and a forward sale means Meta is locking a price today for shares it issues over the next year. Read: this sets the bar for Microsoft and Alphabet on Wednesday. Any capex raise without a funding plan gets sold; any hint of equity funding gets sold harder. Nvidia buying stock in its largest customers is vendor financing by another name.

Energy

Take: Crude held above $100 WTI and $110 Brent on Hormuz and Houthi risk while peace delegations sat in Tehran; the Brent drop in the table is the January roll, not a sell-off. OPEC+'s seven voluntary-cut countries meet November 1.
OPEC
Saudi output rebounded to 8.372 million bpd in September, up more than 2 million bpd from August
OPEC+ data show Saudi production at 8.372 million bpd in September as Aramco ramped loadings from Gulf terminals after the attack on the East-West pipeline; September exports rose from a 28-month low. OPEC crude output overall rose about 920,000 bpd in the month.
So what: Saudi barrels are coming back but through Gulf terminals that sit inside the Hormuz risk, so the recovery is reversible by one tanker strike. Read: the Brent-WTI spread above $10 is the price of that routing risk, and it widens, not narrows, on the next incident.
https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Saudi-Arabias-Oil-Exports-Rose-From-a-2
Crude
US SPR at 282.6 million barrels, lowest since 1982
DOE data put the reserve at 282.6 million barrels last week. Drawdowns are part of a 172 million barrel release structured as loans, with borrowers returning barrels plus a premium later. Some analysts call the level the operational minimum.
So what: The federal buffer is spent. Read: the next supply shock gets priced in the futures curve rather than offset by releases, and the loan structure means refiners owe barrels back into a market that is already short, which supports the back of the curve.

Data centers

Take: The build-out news this week is on the funding side, with Meta's $95B equity plan and Anthropic's IPO filing, while the permitting side keeps tightening in Texas and New York. No named project broke ground or completed on October 26 or 27 in the sweep.
Trend
Over 4,250 planned or in-progress US and Canada projects, a $4.75T pipeline, and more than 500 moratoria across 42 states
Data Center Knowledge (October 27) cites Industrial Info Resources: US operational data center load 53 GW versus 23 GW in 2023, 42 GW approved or under construction, more than 400 GW of new load in planning, and almost 100 US sites designed for at least 1 GW. $130B of projects were rejected in Q1 2026. Large gas turbines from GE Vernova, Mitsubishi Power and Siemens Energy are sold out through 2029 with orders stretching into 2031. New Jersey has 41 local moratoria and Michigan 36.
So what: Announcements still outrun blockages, but the binding constraints are turbines and permits, not capital. Read: the 300 MW threshold where self-supplied gas pencils is the dividing line; above it, projects compete for a turbine queue that ends in 2031, which favors reciprocating engines, fuel cells and anyone holding delivery slots.
https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/the-power-challenge-behind-hypersc
Power deal
Texas energization hold on 75 MW+ loads runs since August 3, with audit reports due December 10
Governor Abbott's August 3 directive ordered PUCT and ERCOT to verify and audit every data center in the interconnection queue; ERCOT paused energization of new large loads of 75 MW or more and the Batch Zero study process. About 200 GW of large-load requests sit in the ERCOT queue. An October 15 analysis says the hold runs pending reports due December 10, and reports a separate TCEQ permit pause from September 21 that only one source confirms.
So what: Texas was the default location for gas-fed AI campuses; the queue is now frozen until at least mid-December. Read: that pushes near-term megawatts toward Louisiana, Pennsylvania and Oklahoma and raises the value of sites under 75 MW or already energized.
