What mattered

Rates
December hike is priced; the open question is 25 or 50
October PPI rose 0.3% m/m (0.4% expected) and 6.5% y/y, the largest annual gain since November 2022. The 10-year traded near 5.14% on Friday. FXStreet today cites 92% odds of a 25 bp hike on December 9 and 8% for 50 bp; CNBC's Friday FedWatch read was 87% and 13%.
So what: Read: the hike itself is no longer the risk, the size is. High-yield spreads narrowed to 320 bp from 342 bp over the week, so credit is not confirming stress yet, but all-in junk yields still rose because the base rate climbed. Dollar strength (DXY at its highest since April 2025) hurts multinationals' translated revenue, NKE and IBM included. Rate-sensitive housing names such as RH face a 5%+ long end into the December print window.
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html
Tech
Asia dumps chips ahead of Nvidia's first print without Huang
Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on November 12 amid reports of affairs, one with a woman US intelligence reportedly believes is a Chinese spy; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. Nvidia fell about 13% last week, its worst week since April 2025, and HSBC cut it to hold with a $170 target (from $365). It reports Tuesday after the close against consensus of about $2.47 EPS on $108.8B revenue. Monday Asia: Kospi -3.59%, SK Hynix -5.6%, Advantest almost -10%, TSMC about -3%. RUMOR: Huang is 'reportedly mulling a comeback' (CNBC live blog, no named source).
So what: Read: the numbers on Tuesday are likely fine; the risk is governance and deal-making. Goldman flagged that Nvidia's multi-party financing deals may be harder to negotiate without Huang, and those circular investments fund a large share of neocloud and lab demand. CoreWeave and Dell sold off Friday while AMD and Broadcom were bid, so the market is already rotating to second sources. Apple at a record $360.80 shows money hiding in mega-caps with no AI capex exposure.
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/15/stock-market-today-live-updates.html
Japan
Japan Q3 GDP contracts; yen past 160
Japan's Q3 GDP shrank at a 0.1% annualized rate against a Reuters poll of +0.3% and +1.4% the prior quarter; net exports subtracted 0.6 points. The Nikkei fell 2.42%. On Friday the yen weakened past 160 per dollar and Japan's finance minister warned of possible action.
So what: Read: a contraction makes it harder for the BoJ to tighten while the Fed hikes, which widens the rate gap and keeps pressure on the yen. Intervention risk is live above 160; a sudden yen spike would force carry unwinds and hit US tech positioning funded in yen, the August 2024 pattern. China's October industrial output, retail sales and investment also all missed, so Asia demand is soft on two fronts.

Energy

Take: Crude is holding in the high $80s WTI after last week's 13% to 16% drop, pulled between a new IEA surplus call and fresh tanker incidents in the Strait of Hormuz. Read: the market is treating the Hormuz reports as noise so far, because flows have largely recovered to pre-war levels.
Crude
IRGC says two tankers hit mines in Hormuz
Iran's Revolutionary Guards said Monday that two oil tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz and exploded; six tankers caught fire in similar incidents Sunday and Monday. Reuters could not verify the claim. Brent was $95.89 (+0.1%) and WTI $88.65 (+0.1%) at 07:57 GMT.
So what: Read: a +0.1% response to six burning tankers says the market doubts the source or sees flows as resilient. About 15M b/d transited Hormuz in 2025, so a verified mining campaign would reprice crude fast and lift tanker rates and war-risk insurance first. Treat the sourcing as weak until a third party confirms.
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/oil-prices-brent-wti-latest.html
Supply
IEA flips Q4 from deficit to a 1.7M b/d surplus
The IEA said Friday that supply will average 105M b/d in Q4, 1.7M b/d above forecast demand, reversing its earlier deficit call as Middle East exports recover. China's October refinery throughput fell 8.1% y/y and crude stocks built by an estimated 492,000 b/d.
So what: Read: surplus plus weak Chinese runs is why crude gave back 13% to 16% last week. Lower crude helps the inflation prints that are driving the Fed, with a lag, and takes margin away from US producers that hedged little. BMI still calls OPEC+ export recovery fragile, so the surplus depends on Hormuz staying open.
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/oil-prices-brent-wti-latest.html

Natural gas

Take: Henry Hub gave back part of last week's rally: December settled about $3.23 on Friday, down 3.2%, on warmer forecasts and recovering output, and trades near $3.15 this morning. Storage is comfortable; LNG feedgas is the support.
Storage
Late-season build of 61 Bcf beat the 5-year average
EIA reported a 61 Bcf injection for the week ended November 6 against a 5-year average build of about 40 Bcf, taking Lower 48 stocks to 3,862 Bcf. EIA's November outlook sees Henry Hub spot averaging $2.87 from November through March. Next storage print is Thursday.
So what: Read: a larger-than-normal build this late widens the surplus heading into withdrawal season and caps rallies unless cold shows up. EIA's $2.87 winter average sits below the current strip, so the agency is leaning bearish against the futures.
https://www.eia.gov/pressroom/releases/press594.php
LNG
Feedgas hits a 2026 high near 21 Bcf/d
NGI estimated US LNG feedgas at 21.2 Bcf/d on Friday, the highest this year; LSEG data put the November average at a record 19.3 Bcf/d. Lower 48 output averaged 112.1 Bcf/d so far in November, below October. Figures come from trade-press summaries and vary by source.
So what: Read: export demand near 19% of output is what keeps a $3 floor under a market with surplus storage. Any Gulf Coast plant outage now moves price more than a storage miss does.
https://naturalgasintel.com/news/surging-lng-winter-risk-send-natural-gas-futures-soaring/

Data centers

Take: Nothing new broke over the weekend, but last week's flow says the build-out is still accelerating and is shifting to on-site gas power in Texas. Read: the Huang exit is the first real threat to the financing chain, since Nvidia's own investments back several of these tenants.
New build
PowerBridge breaks ground on Alpha Digital in Reeves County, Texas
PowerBridge announced groundbreaking in mid-November on a roughly 3,400-acre powered-land campus near Pecos, leased from LandBridge, designed for more than 2 GW of co-located gas generation. Phase one is 300 MW with first power targeted for Q4 2027; Liberty Power Innovations builds and runs the modular gas units. No customer is announced.
So what: Read: this is speculative powered land, built ahead of a tenant. It works only if hyperscalers keep paying for speed-to-power over grid interconnection. Winners if it fills: LandBridge (royalty-style land income), Liberty (a second business beyond frac), Permian gas producers with stranded Waha supply.
https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/powerbridge-breaks-ground-on-gigawatt-scale-pow
Power deal
Fermi signs 600 MW letter of intent with an unnamed hyperscaler
Fermi America said on November 12 that an investment-grade hyperscaler committed to an initial 600 MW at Project Matador in Texas starting in 2027, with a $200M prepayment and a $15M early site agreement. It is not yet a definitive lease, and Fermi's August lease with TensorWave was terminated.
So what: Read: the swap from a neocloud tenant to an investment-grade one is the trend to watch. Developers are upgrading counterparty credit as financing gets pickier, which leaves smaller GPU clouds with fewer sites. Skepticism is warranted until the lease is signed.
https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/fermi-does-600mw-power-deal-with-hyperscaler-bu
Power deal
Anthropic buys $3.4B of gas gen-sets; Riot signs 910 MW with an AI lab
Per the November 12 Mission Critical roundup (covering November 9 to 11): Anthropic took over a OnePWR order of Caterpillar generator sets worth $3.4B to support 1.7 GW, and Riot Platforms signed a 910 MW agreement with an unnamed frontier AI lab at its Corsicana, Texas site. Google also committed $17B to its first Wyoming data center with a planned 702 MW fuel-cell plant.
So what: Read: labs are now buying generation equipment directly, about $2M per MW for gen-sets, because turbine and grid queues are the binding constraint. That favors Caterpillar and reciprocating-engine suppliers, and bitcoin miners with energized sites, over anyone waiting on a utility interconnect.
