One level deeper

Two kinds of FDA voucher: the one BioNTech just bought for $205M and the one Compass holds
A priority review voucher (PRV) is a transferable coupon Congress created to reward approvals in rare pediatric diseases, tropical diseases and medical countermeasures. It converts a standard review (10-month goal after the 60-day filing period, so about 12 months from submission for a new molecule) into a priority review (6-month goal, about 8 months). It does not change the approval standard, the holder must give FDA 90 days' notice before using it, and the user pays an extra fee on top of the application fee. Because it is transferable it has a market price: $67.5M for the first sale in 2014, a $350M peak in 2015, roughly $100M from 2019 to 2024, and now $205M after the rare pediatric program's December 2024 lapse cut new supply. Value it the way a buyer does: four months of earlier revenue on a specific product, times the probability of approval, plus any first-to-market position gained on a rival. That only clears $205M for a large product. My read on BioNTech: most novel cancer drugs earn priority review on their own merits, so paying for a voucher implies the intended filing would otherwise get a standard clock, for example an entrant into a setting with existing therapies where being months ahead of a competitor matters. The Commissioner's National Priority Voucher, launched in 2025, is a different instrument: FDA awards it at its discretion, it cannot be sold, and it targets a review of one to two months. Compass's voucher is this kind, so it has no resale value and its whole worth is timing. The trap in both cases is treating speed as probability. A voucher moves the decision date forward; it does nothing to the odds, and a compressed review leaves less time to resolve a manufacturing or labeling question before the goal date, which is how fast reviews end in CRLs. For a seller like Scholar Rock the voucher is non-dilutive cash equal to half its balance sheet; when you model a rare pediatric name ahead of approval, carry the voucher as a separate line at a haircut to the last print, not inside the drug's NPV.
