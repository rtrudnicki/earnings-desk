What mattered

Tech
Huang resigns from Nvidia effective immediately; CFO Kress is interim CEO
Nvidia's Nov 12 release says Jensen Huang resigned as President, CEO and director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while staying CFO, Harvey Jones becomes the first board chair, and the board is searching inside and outside. The stock fell as much as 14% intraday and closed -4.34%; Q3 FY27 results stay on Tuesday Nov 17 at about 1:20 pm PT. Press reports tie the exit to extramarital relationships, one with a woman US officials suspect is a Chinese agent; Senate Banking has asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick to testify in December.
So what: Read: the governance discount is the small part. The bigger risk is Washington. A CEO linked by reports to a suspected Chinese agent, plus probes into chip smuggling to China, gives export-control hawks cover to tighten rules on Nvidia and on cloud operators, which hits China-exposed semis and Hong Kong tech before it hits Nvidia's US order book. Customer demand does not change with the CEO, so the Tuesday guide matters more than the search. The release did not reaffirm guidance; it only said operations continue without interruption. Bernstein cut its target to $200 from $400. Retail bought the dip ($111M net in the first 30 minutes Thursday per Vanda), so the bounce is not institutional conviction.

Rates
10Y above 5.1%, December hike fully priced, 50 bp on the table
October CPI (Nov 10) ran +0.4% m/m vs +0.3% expected and core +0.3% vs +0.2%, the third straight 0.3% core. The 2Y jumped about 17 bp to 4.73% that day, its largest one-day rise since April 2025; the 10Y reached 5.10% Thursday and 5.127% early Friday. CME FedWatch shows 100% odds of a hike on Dec 9, about 87% for 25 bp and 13% for 50 bp. Waller said Nov 10 that 50 bp should be considered if core CPI prints 0.3% or higher; Logan sees two more quarter-point hikes.
So what: Read: this is the driver behind your biotech tape. XBI -3.27% and Russell -1.66% on a day the S&P lost 0.35% is a discount-rate move, and I found no sector-specific catalyst for the XBI drop. Cash-burning small caps reprice on the 2Y, so financing windows narrow and follow-on discounts widen while the 50 bp tail is live. The Waller condition (core at 0.3%) was already met by the CPI print, so the 13% looks low to me. Thursday's 30Y auction cleared at 5.441% and the October deficit was $404B vs $298.6B expected, so supply is adding to the pressure.

Feedgas at a record 19.3 bcfd in November while output dips
