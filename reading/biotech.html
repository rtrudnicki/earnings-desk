One level deeper

Why a 'statistically significant' Phase 3 can be worthless: DYNASTY-Breast02 against DESTINY-Breast04
BioNTech and DualityBio met their primary endpoint on Monday and in the same release said the drug's benefit 'would be limited.' Read that pairing as a lesson in what a primary endpoint is for. A PFS p-value in a 541-patient open-label trial against investigator's-choice chemo answers one question: is the drug better than nothing much. It does not answer the question the market asks, which is whether it beats the incumbent, and here the incumbent is Enhertu, whose DESTINY-Breast04 posted a PFS hazard ratio of 0.51 (10.1 vs 5.4 months) and an OS hazard ratio of 0.64 in the same HR+/HER2-low population (NEJM, https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2203690). When a sponsor withholds the hazard ratio and the medians and calls the gain 'modest,' assume the HR is far enough from 0.51 that a cross-trial table would end the program, because disclosure is a choice and silence is informative. The second trap is the safety denominator. Interstitial lung disease from a HER2-directed TOP1 ADC is a class effect: Enhertu runs about 12% any-grade ILD with roughly 0.8% fatal, and the entire pitch for third-generation payloads and linkers was a lower number. DYNASTY-Breast02 reported ILD above the drug's own prior trials, with Grade 3-5 events including deaths, so the differentiation thesis failed on the one axis that mattered while the efficacy thesis merely underdelivered. For your own work the rule is: before a readout, write down the incumbent's HR and its fatal-toxicity rate, and decide in advance what 'met the primary endpoint' would need to show against both. If you cannot find those two numbers in the topline release, the release is telling you where they landed.
