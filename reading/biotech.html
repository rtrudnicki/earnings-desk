CMPS
COMP360 NDA is complete; the clock now runs on a voucher review
Compass said on Nov 9 that it completed the rolling NDA for COMP360 psilocybin in treatment-resistant depression, eight days ahead of the Nov 17 date in your table. The package rests on two positive Phase 3 trials (COMP005, COMP006) with more than 1,000 participants. The release gives no review duration and no launch date; BioSpace reports the company still expects a 1H 2027 launch and that the National Priority Voucher cuts review from 10 to 12 months down to 1 to 2 months. Stifel expects approval within 1Q27 or sooner. The release restates that psilocybin is Schedule I and must be rescheduled by DEA and the states before any sale.
The filing risk is gone and the next events are FDA acceptance and a decision that could land before your Feb 2 earnings estimate. Two things cap the upside. First, Usona filed its own psilocybin NDA (PSIL201, MDD, also with a voucher) the week of Nov 2, which took CMPS down more than 35% in a day; the stock was $9.01 on Nov 3. Second, approval is not launch: DEA scheduling and state-level action sit between an approval letter and revenue. Delix raised a $100M Series C on Nov 9 (J&J venture arm co-led) for a non-hallucinogenic, at-home 5-MeO-DMT analog entering Phase 2 in MDD in 1H 2027, which is the long-dated threat to an in-clinic dosing model.
Thesis read: Read: your thesis was a binary on the Phase 3 data plus NDA optionality. The data is in and the NDA is in, so the position is now a regulatory-timing trade at roughly your fair value ($10.43 rNPV vs $9.01 last quoted here on Nov 3; I did not find a Nov 10 close). The Usona drop is the thing to re-underwrite: your rNPV should now carry a second psilocybin entrant in MDD, a broader label than TRD. The $14 to $15 trim level is unchanged, but an approval inside 60 days is a fundamental change, so decide in advance whether an approval pop counts as 'no fundamental change'.

IRD
Q3: BEST1 Cohort 2 dosed at 11 patients, topline pulled into Q1 2027
Opus reported Q3 on Nov 10. It completed enrollment and dosing of 11 participants in the high-dose Cohort 2 of BIRD-1 (4.5e9 vg/eye) against an original target of five. Topline, covering 3-month Cohort 2 data and 6-month data for the three Cohort 1 BVMD participants, is now guided to Q1 2027 (your table had 2Q27). The runway claim needs care: a $46.5M quarterly loss against $89.0M of cash does not reach 2029 on its face, so the statement leans on the Oberland note facility and on the loss containing non-cash items. I did not see the split. LYNX-3 (phentolamine, dim-light disturbances) is absent from the release entirely, after the 1H 2026 topline never came.
Thesis read: Read: the timeline improved by one quarter and the cohort got bigger, which is the right direction. Before leaning on 'into 2029', pull the 10-Q and separate cash burn from non-cash charges, and check what Oberland draws are conditional on. Treat LYNX-3 as unguided until the company says otherwise.

Zura's tibulizumab misses in HS and the program is dropped; placebo HiSCR50 was 45.5%
Zura reported on Nov 9 that the Phase 2 TibuSHIELD trial (247 adults, 1:1:1, IL-17A plus BAFF dual antagonist) missed its primary and key secondary endpoints at Week 16. AN count fell 56.0% (300 mg) and 58.1% (150 mg) vs 52.5% on placebo. HiSCR50 was 48.0% and 52.7% vs 45.5%; HiSCR75 was 35.3% and 34.0% vs 28.1%. Serious adverse events ran 6.1% and 3.7% vs 1.2%. Zura is ending HS development; systemic sclerosis data (TibuSURE) are due 1H 2027.
Read-through: Read for MLTX: one fewer IL-17 competitor in HS, and another data point that placebo response in HS trials is running high (45.5% on HiSCR50). That second point cuts against sonelokimab too, because FDA reviewers will read the VELA package knowing how noisy the endpoint is. See the deeper section.

FDA investigates Zolgensma after nine tumor reports, two with vector integration
Novartis confirmed nine tumors in patients previously treated with Zolgensma out of more than 6,000 dosed; two show evidence of AAV vector integration into patient DNA, seven are not causally linked. FDA said on Nov 6 it is investigating the risk of neoplasia, is weighing regulatory action, and still sees benefit outweighing risk for approved uses. FDA
Class-wide NAION warning ordered for all 12 GLP-1 receptor agonists
FDA on Nov 9 required a Warnings and Precautions update for non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy across every marketed GLP-1 RA, including Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound, Mounjaro and oral Foundayo. The supporting meta-analysis covered 126 trials and 78,217 GLP-1 patients: 10 probable events on drug vs 5 on placebo, or 6.4 vs 3.8 per 100,000 patient-years.

One level deeper

Why HS trials keep failing on placebo, and how to read a HiSCR number
Zura's drug did about what IL-17 drugs do in hidradenitis suppurativa on the active arms (HiSCR50 of 48% to 53%); the trial failed because 45.5% of placebo patients also responded. HiSCR50 asks for a 50% drop in abscess and inflammatory nodule count with no new abscesses or draining tunnels. That is a count of lesions that flare and settle on their own, assessed by eye, in patients who enroll during a flare, so regression to the mean does much of the work. Concomitant antibiotics, lesion intervention rules and a large number of sites each add to it. From memory, to be checked against the labels: placebo HiSCR50 ran about 26% to 28% in adalimumab's PIONEER trials a decade ago and about 30% in the secukinumab and bimekizumab Phase 3s, so 45.5% in a 2026 Phase 2 shows the drift is getting worse as trials compete for the same biologic-naive patients. Sponsors respond by moving to HiSCR75, a higher bar that placebo clears less often, which is what MoonLake did in VELA; Zura's own HiSCR75 delta was only about 6 to 7 points against the mid-teens that bimekizumab showed. Two habits follow. First, never judge an HS result on the active-arm rate; write down the placebo-adjusted delta and the placebo rate next to it, and distrust any cross-trial comparison where the placebo rates differ by more than a few points. Second, check how the analysis treats rescue therapy and dropouts, because counting antibiotic users as non-responders versus using their observed data can move the delta by several points, and that choice is where FDA reviewers will press on the sonelokimab BLA. The same logic applies to any endpoint built on fluctuating lesion counts or patient-reported symptoms, including atopic dermatitis, depression and pain.
