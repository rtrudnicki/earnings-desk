BHVN
FDA accepts the troriluzole (Vyglxia) SCA resubmission; PDUFA set for 1Q27, PCNS advisory committee coming; BHV-1530 ADC poster lands at ESMO today
An 8-K furnished Oct 22 says the FDA accepted the NDA resubmission for troriluzole in spinocerebellar ataxia, treats it as a complete response to the Nov 4, 2025 action letter, and assigned a user fee goal date in the first quarter of 2027 (no day given). The agency plans to convene the Peripheral and Central Nervous System Advisory Committee; the date 'will be forthcoming in the near term.' The resubmission 'included new positive supportive analyses' with no detail. Shares rose roughly 25%; Baird moved its target from $42 to $48. Separately, abstract 1019P (Phase 1 BHV-1530, FGFR3 ADC, advanced solid tumors) posts at ESMO Madrid today 15:15 to 16:00 CEST.
Acceptance is procedural, not a read on approvability. The 2025 rejection turned on whether Study 206-RWE counted as an adequate and well-controlled study; the company has not said what the 'new supportive analyses' are, and an AdCom means the FDA wants external cover on exactly that question. Priced correctly this is a coin toss with a panel date as the next catalyst. The ADC poster is the first peer-forum efficacy look at an asset the market values near zero; the pre-announcement promised confirmed PRs in heavily pretreated patients and no DLTs, so the bar is response rate by FGFR3 status and whether wild-type overexpressors respond (the company's claim is roughly 50% of mUC). Open items unchanged: the Kv7 partial hold metabolite data and the $350M SK Biopharm closing (HSR still pending).
Thesis read: Catalyst table now carries the 1Q27 PDUFA (2027-03-31) and an undated AdCom. The AdCom date is the next thing to watch; a panel before year-end would compress the trade.

VOR
Telitacicept two-year TELIGAN data: eGFR slope -0.6 vs -5.7 mL/min/1.73m2/yr, zero kidney-failure events vs five on placebo
Oct 22 release plus an ASN Kidney Week oral (TH-OR111, high-impact trials plenary). TELIGAN (China, RemeGen) randomized 318 biopsy-confirmed IgAN adults 1:1 to telitacicept 240 mg SC weekly through week 39 then every other week to week 104, or placebo. Annualized eGFR slope through week 104: -0.6 (95% CI -1.7 to 0.4) vs -5.7 (-7.0 to -4.5), difference 5.1 (3.5 to 6.7), p<0.001. 24h UPCR -60.3% vs +0.9% (placebo-adjusted -60.7%); 44.0% vs 8.2% reached <0.5 g/g. Confirmed >=30% eGFR decline HR 0.11; >=40% decline HR 0.19. Composite kidney failure: 0 vs 5 (two placebo patients dialyzed). Gd-IgA1 -47.8% vs +19.1%. SAEs 8.2% vs 18.2%; serious infections 1.9% vs 4.4%; injection-site reactions 56.0% vs 18.9%.
Why it matters: A full two-year slope win in IgAN is the endpoint regulators use for traditional approval, and this is a hard-outcome sweep rather than a proteinuria surrogate. Caveats that matter for the read-across: the trial is China-only, the placebo slope of -5.7 is steep (high-risk population, and background RASi/SGLT2 use is not stated in the release), and the release says nothing about a US filing for IgAN; Vor's global Phase 3s are in gMG and Sjogren's with toplines 1H27. Competitive frame: Otsuka's sibeprenlimab and Vera's atacicept are the APRIL-axis comps, Celltrion paid up to $580M for an anti-APRIL IgAN asset the same day, and AZ's Ultomiris just got accelerated approval on proteinuria alone. Telitacicept hits both BLyS and APRIL; the Gd-IgA1 drop is the mechanistic tell.

One level deeper

IgA nephropathy endpoints: why a proteinuria approval and an eGFR-slope win landed on the same day, and what each one proves
Two IgAN events hit within 24 hours and they sit on different rungs of the evidence ladder. Ultomiris got accelerated approval on a 34-week urine protein-to-creatinine ratio change (43.4% placebo-adjusted); telitacicept reported a two-year annualized eGFR slope (-0.6 vs -5.7 mL/min/1.73m2 per year). The FDA treats proteinuria reduction at nine months as a surrogate 'reasonably likely' to predict benefit, which earns a conditional label with a confirmatory requirement; eGFR slope over two years is the accepted surrogate for traditional approval, because slope integrates the actual loss of filtration that ends in dialysis. That is why the Ultomiris label says kidney-function benefit is not established and points to week 106. Reading slopes across trials is where analysts get hurt. The treatment effect is a difference of slopes, so the placebo slope sets the ceiling: a -5.7 per year control arm is steep (typical IgAN placebo arms in Western trials with full RAS blockade run closer to -2 to -4), which means TELIGAN enrolled high-risk, fast-progressing patients and may reflect different background therapy in a China-only population. A 5.1 difference is therefore both real (CI 3.5 to 6.7, zero vs five kidney-failure events) and not directly comparable to the one-to-two-point slope differences that supported the sparsentan and iptacopan labels. Mechanism matters for the ordering too: telitacicept removes BLyS and APRIL upstream, and the 47.8% fall in galactose-deficient IgA1 is the pathogenic antigen falling, whereas C5 blockade acts downstream on the inflammatory effector after deposition. The market lesson is the valuation gap this creates: a surrogate label opens the door to many entrants (Celltrion just paid up to $580M for another APRIL antibody), while a hard slope win is what differentiates. When you model an IgAN asset, write down the placebo slope, the baseline proteinuria and the RASi/SGLT2 background before you write down the treatment effect.
