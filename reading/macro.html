What mattered

Tech
Nvidia loses its founder CEO five days before the print
Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, president and director on Nov 12; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and Harvey Jones became the first board chairman. The stock closed Thursday at $166.19 (down 4.3%, after a 14% intraday drop) and fell about 2% more Friday; HSBC cut it to hold and took its target to $170 from $365. Reports tie the exit to personal relationships and a US national-security inquiry; the board has stated no cause, and CNBC cites a source saying he was pushed out. The FT report that the board approached Arm CEO Rene Haas is a RUMOR (single outlet, unconfirmed); no search firm was hired as of Nov 13.
So what: My read: the market is treating this as a governance discount, not a demand problem, which is why AMD and Broadcom were bid Friday while CoreWeave and Dell sold. The real exposure is an export-control or security finding that touches China revenue or licensing, and that risk does not clear on Tuesday's numbers. A CFO running the company as interim means the call will be scripted and light on roadmap, so a beat may not be enough to re-rate the stock.

Geopolitics
Weekend escalation in the Gulf: Bahrain strike claim, five tankers mined
The IRGC said six drones struck a US radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base in Bahrain early Nov 15; Bahrain said it intercepted the attack, the first major one there since September. The inflation channel matters more than usual: PPI is already 6.5% year over year and the Fed is hiking, so an oil spike raises the odds of 50 bp in December.

Energy

Take: Crude fell for a second week (WTI $88.56, Brent $95.84, each down about 2.5%) on the IEA calling a Q4 surplus, but the weekend's mine strikes and the Bahrain attack claim put a bid under the reopen. The surplus call and the escalation cannot both be right for long.
Crude
IEA: first surplus since the war began, 1.7 million bpd in Q4
The Nov 13 IEA report puts Q4 supply near 105 million bpd, 1.7 million bpd above demand, as Gulf exports recover, and sees a 3.8 million bpd surplus in 2027 (3.94 million last month). OPEC+ pumped 41.23 million bpd in October, up 2.04 million bpd from September and about 97% of the pre-war February level. The agency still calls diesel tightness the overriding concern.
So what: My read: the crude surplus is conditional on Hormuz traffic continuing to normalize, and five tankers reported mined in two days is the opposite. The cleaner expression of the IEA view is the crude-to-diesel gap: crack spreads stay wide even if flat price falls, which favors refiners over producers.
https://www.cp24.com/news/money/2026/11/13/world-oil-market-is-returning-to-surplus-as-gul
Shipping
Five tankers reported mined in Hormuz across Nov 13-14
Iran said two tankers, one a supertanker, struck mines late Nov 13 and three more caught fire Nov 14. Iran requires its authorization for transit; the US maintains a naval blockade on Iranian ports and says the main mine routes are cleared. A UN official asked both sides to let fertilizer through; about a third of global fertilizer trade used the strait before the war.
So what: My read: the claims come from Iran and are not independently confirmed, so discount the count but not the direction. War-risk premiums and tanker day rates move before Brent does, and the fertilizer blockage feeds into 2027 crop input costs.

Natural gas

Take: December gas settled near $3.23 Friday, down about 3% on the day after Thursday's $3.337 but up roughly 8% on the week from $2.998, on colder forecasts and record LNG feedgas. Storage is the counterweight: a 75 Bcf surplus to the five-year average heading into withdrawal season.
Storage
EIA: 61 Bcf build to 3,862 Bcf, below the 65 to 67 Bcf consensus
For the week ended Nov 6 (released Friday Nov 13 on the holiday schedule) working gas rose 61 Bcf to 3,862 Bcf, 75 Bcf above the five-year average of 3,787 Bcf and 92 Bcf below a year ago. South Central added 27 Bcf to 1,235 Bcf and remains below its five-year average.
So what: My read: a build that missed consensus by about 5 Bcf still failed to lift futures, which says the week's rally was weather and short covering, not balance tightening. With the December contract expiring Nov 25, the weekend forecast runs decide whether $3.20 holds; a warmer revision gives back the squeeze.

Power deal
Fermi signs 600 MW with an unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler; TensorWave lease terminated
Fermi America (Project Matador, Texas) announced a 600 MW power agreement starting in 2027 with a $200M customer prepayment and a $15M early construction and site development agreement; a definitive long-term lease is still being negotiated. It also disclosed the termination of the TensorWave data center lease signed in August 2026. RUMOR: reports name Amazon as the customer (press reports, not confirmed by either company).
So what: My read: $200M up front on 600 MW is a deposit, not a lease, and Fermi's plan is 17 GW, so this validates the site more than the business model. The signal is the swap itself: developers are trading neocloud tenants for investment-grade ones, which tightens capacity and credit for the second-tier AI clouds.
