What mattered

Geopolitics
Brent dropped $8.66 in a day on talk progress, then recovered a fifth of it on a reported US strike on Qeshm Island
Brent settled down 7.63% at $104.77 and WTI down 7.4% at $96.46 Monday after Qatari mediators flew back to Tehran with a revised draft; the reported framework has Iran reopening the Strait of Hormuz for US sanctions relief with nuclear talks to follow, and Iran says nothing moves until the US naval blockade of its ports lifts. Overnight the WSJ, citing US officials, reported a US strike on Qeshm Island at the mouth of the strait, described as defensive, and Brent traded back to about $106. Trump said he did not intend to strike Iran after the midterms; Netanyahu is reported to be urging resumed attacks.
So what: Read: an $8 one-day drop is the market's estimate of the war premium that leaves on a deal, and the bounce recovering only a fifth says traders still lean toward a deal. A reopened Hormuz lets Saudi, Kuwait and Iraq, all producing below quota, lift actual output toward their targets and pulls the IEA's 2027 surplus forward; E&Ps and the refiners' crack spreads lose, airlines and the consumer gain, and the Fed's December hike case loses a few basis points of justification. The war is in its eighth month, so a false dawn is the base rate.

Energy

Take: Crude's one-day 7.6% drop and partial overnight bounce is the whole tape, covered above; underneath it OPEC+ is irrelevant for now because Saudi, Kuwait and Iraq are producing below quota on Hormuz constraints, so quota is not the binding variable, the strait is.
OPEC
Seven core OPEC+ members hold December targets at September levels; next ministerial November 29 sets 2027
At a virtual meeting November 1, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman kept December required production at the September 2026 level, about 31 mb/d combined, unchanged for the fourth month; the last voluntary cuts were unwound in September. Saudi, Kuwait and Iraq are still producing well below quota because of Strait of Hormuz constraints.
So what: Read: a Hormuz reopening is effectively an unannounced OPEC supply increase of whatever those three are short, and it lands into the IEA's 2027 surplus call; the November 29 meeting is the one where the group has to choose between defending price and defending share once that becomes visible.
https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/620-1-november-2026.html

Data centers

Take: The week's signal is on the financing side (Oracle's $20B raise, Amazon's negative free cash flow) rather than any slowdown in announcements; capital keeps moving to sites with existing grid connections. One named project today, in the UK.
New build
Drax, Brookfield and Radiant plan a 1 GW-plus AI campus at Drax Power Station, North Yorkshire; £20B lifetime figure, £2.5B committed to a 100 MW first phase
Announced November 3: phase one up to 100 MW targeted operational early 2028, a further 500 MW over 2028 to 2031, long-term capacity above 1 GW. Brookfield estimates total investment including semiconductors could exceed £20B ($26.8B), with £2.5B for phase one. Drax supplies land, power and its 4 GW grid connection; Radiant leads design and customers; Brookfield funds through a joint venture with Qatar's QIA. Phase one still needs planning, regulatory and network approvals and has no tenant. The UK government had just rejected a 1.2 GW AI Growth Zone bid for the same site. Drax shares rose about 8%.
So what: Read: the headline is a ceiling, not a commitment; £2.5B of 100 MW is the real number and it is being built on spec, which says developers still expect tenants to show up. The template is legacy generators monetizing grid connections, and Drax's 8% move says the market pays for the connection, not the campus.
