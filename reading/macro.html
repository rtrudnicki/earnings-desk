Tech
SEC enforcement staff are examining Nvidia's accounting; the stock fell 14% and the AI trade broke with it
Reuters, citing two people briefed on the matter, reported Friday that SEC enforcement staff are scrutinizing potential accounting issues at Nvidia. Reuters could not determine when the inquiry opened or which issues are in scope; the SEC declined to comment. NVDA closed down 14.01% at $125.53, its lowest close since May 2025, days after reporting fiscal Q3 revenue of about $115.8B and guiding Q4 to about $130B. Micron fell 13.9%, AMD 13.3%, Broadcom 12.2%, TSMC ADR 9.5%, CoreWeave 12.0%.
So what: Read: the market is no longer debating demand, it is debating whether the reported numbers and the financing behind them can be trusted. That is a harder fear to clear with a good quarter, which is why a beat-and-raise did not hold. The damage ran through the supply chain abroad: Taiwan's TAIEX lost a record 17.5% on the week, SoftBank fell 14.5% Friday, the Kospi 5.2%. Until scope is known, every company with Nvidia-linked vendor financing or circular revenue (neoclouds first) carries the same discount. Skeptic's note: an SEC inquiry is not a finding, and many close with no action. The stock had already lost about a third from its level a month earlier before this report, so some of Friday is capitulation.

OPEC
Full 21-member OPEC+ meets Nov 29 to set 2027 quotas against a projected surplus
Seven core members held December targets unchanged for a third month, with most producing below quota because of Hormuz disruption. A capacity review feeds the 2027 baselines. The IEA projects a 1.7 million barrel-per-day surplus for Q4 2026.
So what: Read: the baseline fight matters more than the headline quota. Members with spare capacity (UAE first) will press for higher baselines; if they get them while Gulf flows normalize in 2027, the surplus grows and Brent's $90 handle goes. A hold with no baseline changes is the status quo outcome and likely a non-event for price.

Data centers

Take: Projects keep getting announced and leased, especially around Dallas, while the equity market funding them reprices: the FT tallies $4.2tn lost in data-center construction stocks, and Cleanview reports construction slowed for the first time in more than a year. No hyperscaler has cut a capex guide; the pressure so far is on financing cost, politics and grid access, not on stated demand.
Trend
Trump threatens a 'tremendous' tax on US companies that build AI data centers abroad
At a White House news conference Friday, Trump said the US would not let companies move data centers overseas and would penalize those that try. AP reported he gave no details and said he had not yet told his own officials; no formal measure exists.
So what: Read: treat it as a statement of intent, not policy, until Treasury or Commerce writes something. If it becomes real, it raises the value of permitted, powered US sites and hurts Gulf and Southeast Asian campuses that depend on US tenants (the same week brought new US limits on AI chip access for Gulf data centers). It also collides with domestic opposition: Ohio voters are contesting Amazon's $17B project and Texas's governor campaigned on a moratorium, so forced onshoring meets a shrinking list of willing counties.

Power deal
Senate passes a bipartisan permitting bill that would make large AI data centers pay more of their grid upgrades
The bill speeds approvals for generation and transmission and proposes that large AI data centers cover more of the grid-upgrade costs tied to their load. It still needs House passage; vote count and bill name not found. Separately, Merlin paused two data centers near Bilbao, Spain, citing grid issues.
So what: Read: cost allocation is the piece that matters. Shifting upgrade costs onto the load raises all-in cost per MW for grid-connected campuses and improves the relative economics of on-site gas generation. Faster transmission permitting helps regulated utilities and transmission builders regardless of who the customer is.
