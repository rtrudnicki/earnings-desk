What mattered

Tech
Market stops paying for AI capex without operating income: Intel -30%, Amazon sold off on a light Q4 guide
Intel beat Q3 (revenue $16.5B, +21% YoY; data center and AI +78% to $7.3B) but guided Q4 revenue to $15.0-16.0B and non-GAAP EPS to $0.25 vs $17.05B and $0.43 expected, blaming a PC slowdown lasting at least through 1H27; shares fell roughly 28-31% Friday. Amazon grew AWS 39.3% to $46B with a $578B backlog, spent $75.9B of cash capex in the quarter, held 2026 capex at about $220B with 'meaningfully more' in 2027, and guided Q4 operating income to $28.5-33.5B; EPS of $1.89 missed $1.97.
So what: Read: the capex itself is not news, the penalty is. Alphabet kept $195-205B for 2026 and said 2027 rises 'significantly'; Microsoft spent $45.2B against a >$50B outlook. The tape is now rewarding whoever converts AI spend to operating income soonest and punishing the rest, which hurts suppliers whose order books depend on 2027 hyperscaler budgets (memory, networking, power gear) if the hyperscalers respond by pacing spend. Intel's PC call is also a consumer-hardware demand read ahead of Qualcomm Wednesday.

Fed
Fed held at 3.75-4.00% with two hike dissents; December hike priced near 80%, 10Y just under 5%
The Oct 28 FOMC held by a 10-2 vote, with Governor Cook and Cleveland's Hammack dissenting for a second quarter-point hike after September's. The statement upgraded growth to 'strong' and kept 'inflation remains elevated'. Market pricing for a December hike ran 75-85% depending on source; the 10Y traded 4.96-4.98% and the 2Y 4.59% into the weekend. October payrolls print Friday Nov 6 at 07:30 CT, consensus 55k vs 29k in September, unemployment 4.2%.
So what: Read: an equity market losing 1.8% on earnings with the 10Y at 5% and hikes priced is a multiple-compression story, not only an AI story, and it makes Friday's jobs number the week's real binary. A payroll print near 50k with unemployment flat keeps the hike live. Long-duration growth, unprofitable small caps and anything financed at floating rates stay the pressure points; banks' net interest income is the offset.

Geopolitics
Iran claims four tankers mined in Hormuz; US weighing strikes after Nov 3; Brent near four-year high
Iran's IRGC said Oct 30 that four tankers struck mines in the Strait using an 'unauthorized southern route', disputing US claims the lanes are clear (Iranian claim, not independently verified). CENTCOM says US forces have redirected 144 commercial vessels under the blockade of Iranian ports. Rubio said Saturday Trump 'hasn't made a decision' on resuming strikes; Trump said the US could hit '1 or 2 sites' in 'ten minutes'. Houthis claimed new attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities; Saudi defenses reported a drone intercept over Riyadh. Brent settled $113.43 Friday, WTI $104.12.
So what: Read: the oil premium is a war premium, not a demand story (see China PMI below), which cuts both ways. A reopening deal is the single biggest downside risk to crude and the biggest upside risk to equities this week. Until then, $104 WTI with a Fed leaning hawkish is a stagflation squeeze on the consumer, airlines, chemicals and anyone paying for diesel freight. Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure are the tail that turns a Hormuz problem into a Gulf-production problem.

Energy

Take: Crude sits near a four-year high on Hormuz mine claims and the open question of US strikes after Tuesday's midterms; OPEC+ meets online today and is expected to hold December targets, which changes nothing because Gulf members are already pumping below quota.
OPEC
Seven core OPEC+ members expected to hold December output targets at today's 12:00 GMT online meeting
Reuters (Nov 1, three sources) says Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman agreed in principle to leave December unchanged. The 1.65 mb/d cut from 2023 finished unwinding in September; about 2 mb/d of cuts covering most members remain through end-2026. Gulf members have been exporting 60-80% of normal levels in recent months because of the war. 2027 quotas wait on a Q4 capacity review.
So what: Read: paper targets are not the binding constraint, export capability through Hormuz is. The quota hold is a non-event for price; the market will trade the Iran headlines and nothing else from OPEC+ until the late-November ministerial sets 2027 baselines. A reopening would release Gulf barrels that are already allowed under quota, so the downside in crude on a deal is larger than the quota math suggests.
https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/opec-set-to-keep-oil-output-targets-steady

Natural gas

Take: Gas is going the other way from oil: a bearish 80 Bcf build (consensus 75) broke a 10-week run of below-average injections, pushed December Henry Hub under $2.90 and left storage 38 Bcf above the five-year average with LNG feedgas already at a record 20 Bcf/d.
Storage
EIA: +80 Bcf for the week ended Oct 23 vs 75 Bcf consensus; stocks 3,724 Bcf, +38 Bcf vs five-year average
The Oct 29 report printed an 80 Bcf injection against a 75 Bcf forecast and 77 Bcf prior, ending ten straight weeks of below-average builds. Inventories reached 3,724 Bcf, about 38 Bcf above the five-year average. The newly prompt December contract briefly traded below $2.900/MMBtu; NGI's weekly Henry Hub spot averaged about $3.045. Next report Nov 5.
So what: Read: with the five-year cushion rebuilt into November, the curve needs cold, not exports, to rally; Thursday's print and the first real cold forecast decide whether December holds $3. Producers that curtailed into the summer have room to keep volumes flat without a price response. For power buyers (data centers included) sub-$3 gas against $104 oil keeps gas-fired generation the cheapest firm option by a wide margin.
https://naturalgasintel.com/news/bearish-eia-storage-print-depresses-natural-gas-futures/
LNG
US LNG feedgas averaged a record 20 Bcf/d for the week ended Oct 25; Golden Pass still ramping
RBN puts feedgas at 20 Bcf/d, up ~0.4 Bcf/d week over week, with every terminal at full or peak operations. Golden Pass averaged 0.7 Bcf/d (record intake) and Plaquemines set a daily record of 4.359 Bcf/d on Oct 25. Rystad counts exports at a record 19.9 Bcf/d on Oct 18.
So what: Read: record exports and a bearish storage print at the same time mean supply growth is outrunning even 20 Bcf/d of LNG pull, which is the bear case for Henry Hub and the bull case for Gulf Coast gas-to-power economics. The next leg of demand is Golden Pass trains 2 and 3 and Corpus Christi Stage 3, not weather.
https://rbnenergy.com/daily-posts/analyst-insight/us-lng-feedgas-sticks-record-levels

Data centers

Take: The build-out is still accelerating on the companies' own numbers (Amazon ~$220B 2026 and more in 2027, Alphabet $195-205B with 2027 up 'significantly', Microsoft $45.2B in the quarter vs a >$50B outlook, ~$170B for calendar 2026), but Friday was the first session where the equity market penalized capex outright, so watch for hyperscaler language about pacing rather than cancelling.
Capex
Oracle reported to plan about 7 GW of new capacity, taking its portfolio to roughly 10 GW by end of FY2028
A MarketBeat report republished Oct 29 (secondary source, not an Oracle filing) says Oracle plans ~7 GW of additions over the next couple of years to reach ~10 GW by fiscal 2028, with a representative 1 GW project costed at about $37B of capex and vendor financing cutting Oracle's initial outlay for such a project by about half. Oracle's new funding was reportedly capped at $20B, below one quarter of capex.
So what: Read: Oracle is the most levered way the market expresses the capex-payback question, and vendor-financed gigawatts move the credit risk to the equipment suppliers and private credit lenders. If the Friday repricing persists, the 7 GW number gets phased, which hits the power developers and turbine queues that were sized to it before it hits Oracle.
