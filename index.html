The read

The swing. The Strait of Hormuz headlines: another tanker strike that pushes Brent back through $117 would turn an AI-led tech selloff into a broad risk-off close, while a calm afternoon lets the market bounce on the in-line wage data and the lower yields.

  • Employment Cost Index 0.8% in line, wages slowing to 0.7% and 3.1% on the year: wage costs are not the inflation problem, which is why the 10-year fell rather than rose on a day with a December hike still priced around 80%.
  • Intel down roughly 26% to 29% and Amazon down 4% at the open; Reddit, Roblox and Pinterest down 24%, 16% and 6%, so the AI-spending debate is now hitting the names that sell into it, not just the ones that spend.
  • Brent $113 to $117 after Iran claimed four tankers struck mines near Hormuz and a tanker caught fire off Khasab; crude up more than 2% on the day and more than 10% for October.
  • German inflation 2.8% versus 3.0% expected, core 2.1%, so the ECB December hike that the euro area GDP beat had cemented is now less certain, and the euro reversed to about 1.1170.
  • Gold down roughly 3% to about $3,910 and bitcoin near $77,000 after the Binance hack report, so the selloff is not a simple flight into every safe asset.

Pay attention to

  1. Watch whether the 10-year holds under 5% into the close; a yield that keeps falling while stocks fall says the bond market is pricing growth risk from the AI drawdown, not relief on wages.
  2. Watch the Nasdaq around the July lows that the morning note flagged, since a close below them turns an earnings reaction into a trend break for the AI complex.
  3. Watch Brent against $117, the Asian-session high, and any US Central Command response to Iran's four-tanker claim; oil is the only thing that can make today's benign wage data irrelevant for December.
  4. Check the Baker Hughes oil rig count at 12:00 CT against 460; a jump is the first supply response to $110-plus crude and a flat count says producers still do not believe it.
  5. China's October manufacturing PMI at 20:30 CT, consensus 49.9 after 50.1, sets Monday's open for commodities and the Asia tech names already down 2% to 3.5% overnight.
