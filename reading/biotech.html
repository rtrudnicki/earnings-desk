PCVX
Pfizer shows 25-valent infant PCV Phase 2 at IDWeek with serotype 3 titers 8.8x to 15x Prevnar 20, and commits to a 35-valent adult candidate by year-end
At IDWeek (Oct 24) Pfizer presented late-breaking Phase 2 infant data for PF-07872412 (25vPnC) versus Prevnar 20 on a 2, 4, 6, 12-15 month schedule: immune responses across all 25 serotypes, safety comparable to PCV20, no related SAEs. Serotype 3 geometric mean titers were 8.8-fold higher than PCV20 one month after dose 3 (4.22 vs 0.48) and about 15-fold after dose 4. Three pediatric Phase 3 studies started earlier in 2026 with up to 6,830 participants and a lot-consistency study is fully enrolled. Pfizer said it plans to start clinical development of a 35-valent adult candidate by end of 2026 pending regulatory alignment.
Why it matters: Vaxcyte's VAX-31 adult Phase 3 OPUS-1 (n=3,572 aged 50+) met its co-primary immunogenicity endpoints on Oct 5; OPUS-2 and OPUS-3 read 1H 2027 and the infant topline is guided to 1H 2027. Merck's 21-valent Capvaxive already owns the adult 'beyond PCV20' slot, and a Pfizer 35-valent adult shot would out-count VAX-31's 31 serotypes by the time Vaxcyte files. Serotype 3 is the serotype where every conjugate underperforms and the one Pfizer is now leading with; VAX-31's serotype 3 OPA non-inferiority margin in the OPUS-1 full dataset is the number to pull when it is presented. Read: the adult BLA timeline is intact, but the terminal share assumption in any VAX-31 model should carry a 2029-2030 Pfizer 35-valent entrant.

Why a 'positive' interim ORR falls as n grows, and how to read it before the tape does (CATX, Tango)
Two companies lost a fifth to a quarter of their value on Friday after reporting response rates that beat every historical comparator, and in both cases the number that mattered was the prior cut. Perspective's Cohort 2 went from 43% (10/23) in April to 33% (15/46) in October; Tango's pancreatic combo went from 92% (11/12) to 82% (18/22). Neither is evidence of a drug getting worse. A 10-of-23 response rate carries a 95% confidence interval of roughly 23% to 64%, so 33% was always inside the plausible range; early cohorts are also enriched for patients who responded quickly enough to be scanned before the cutoff, and 'including unconfirmed responses' means a second scan at least four weeks later has not yet verified the shrinkage, so 4 of Cohort 3's 10 responses can still revert. The analyst discipline is to write down the confidence interval at the first cut and treat the midpoint of the second cut as the working estimate, then compare against the controlled benchmark rather than the company's prior press release: for SSTR2+ NETs that is NETTER-1's 18% ORR and 65% PFS at 20 months for Lutathera, which VMT-alpha-NET still clears on a cross-trial basis. The second lesson is that the market reads safety footnotes faster than efficacy tables. Two grade 3 renal events in 68 patients is 2.9%, but alpha-emitting SSTR2 ligands concentrate in proximal tubules and kidney injury from radioligands shows up months to years later, so 'renal function is still being characterized' is a true statement with a long tail, and Tango's step-down to 200 mg after dose level 2 failed tolerability tells you the Phase 3 dose was chosen by toxicity, not by exposure-response. When you model these, the inputs that moved are not ORR; they are the Phase 3 start date (both slid into 2027), the dose, and the renal or hematologic monitoring burden that decides how a community oncologist uses the drug against an approved comparator.
