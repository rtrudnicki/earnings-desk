The read

The swing. October employment report at 07:30 CT Friday November 6; payrolls and average hourly earnings decide whether the December hike firms above 80% or slips back toward a coin flip.

  • Initial claims 189k versus 196k expected, matching the lowest level since 1969, with the 4-week average down to 194,750.
  • Q3 productivity +3.6% annualized versus +2.8% expected and unit labor costs -0.7% versus +0.6%, the first decline since Q2 2025.
  • Bank of England hiked to 4.00% on an 8-1 vote with the dissent for 50 bp and three members saying one hike is unlikely to be enough.
  • CME FedWatch has a December 25 bp hike near 79% to 82% after Wednesday's JOLTS, up from the low 70s a week ago.
  • The 10-year Treasury is holding near 4.90%, little changed on the day despite the two US prints.

Pay attention to

  1. The 2-year Treasury yield into the close: it was up about 3 bp early on the claims print, and whether the unit-labor-cost decline pulls it back says which print the rates market believes.
  2. December hike odds on CME FedWatch after the prints settle; the morning number near 79% to 82% is the line to beat or lose going into payrolls.
  3. Sterling and gilt yields after the 8-1 Bank of England vote; a hawkish hike that fails to lift the currency says the market thinks the Bank is behind the oil shock.
  4. Freddie Mac's 30-year rate when it posts this afternoon; a third weekly decline from 7.32% would be the first relief for housing since the summer.
  5. Tomorrow's October jobs report at 07:30 CT, with average hourly earnings mattering more than the payroll count after today's unit labor cost print.
