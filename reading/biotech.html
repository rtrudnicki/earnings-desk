Roivant/Priovant: brepocitinib clears both Phase 3 uveitis trials with a 48-week steroid taper
CLARITY-1 and CLARITY-2 in non-infectious uveitis met the primary endpoint and all key secondaries (October 21). Median time to treatment failure exceeded 48 weeks on brepocitinib versus about 12 weeks on placebo (p<0.0001 in both). Steroid-free quiescence in both eyes at week 48: 58.8% vs 19.8% (CLARITY-1) and 64.4% vs 19.4% (CLARITY-2). Macular edema resolved in 42% of eyes vs 4.8% pooled. Read-through: Adalimumab is the only approved systemic biologic in NIU, so an oral TYK2/JAK1 with these numbers is a real second option, boxed JAK warning included. The design point: the companies ran a faster steroid taper than precedent trials, which pushes placebo failure earlier and widens the gap. Check the taper schedule before accepting the 48-vs-12-week headline. For his ophtho names (IRD, SGP) this is a reminder that oral systemic agents are now competing in back-of-eye inflammation.

FDA
GSK/Ionis Hibsago (bepirovirsen) approved as the first HBV functional-cure label; GSK quietly ends a next-gen Phase 1 the next day
FDA approved bepirovirsen on October 20 (priority review, breakthrough) for non-cirrhotic adults on nucleos(t)ide therapy for at least six months with suppressed HBV DNA and HBsAg at or below 3,000 IU/mL. Six-month course, two injections per dose, at-home dosing after the first. Pooled B-Well 1/2: functional cure 19% (233/1,220) vs 0% (0/614) placebo; 26% (200/768) in the HBsAg ≤1,000 IU/mL subgroup. Boxed warning for on-treatment hepatic flares and thrombocytopenia; contraindicated in cirrhosis. Ionis gets 10-12% tiered royalties. On October 21 Fierce reported GSK terminated the Phase 1 of GSK3965193, a PAPD5/7 inhibitor, as a 'strategic decision'.
Read-through: This sets the bar for every HBV cure program on his list. ABUS (imdusiran siRNA, combo studies) and ASMB (capsid assembly modulator ABI-4334) now have to beat 19-26% functional cure in a NA-suppressed, HBsAg-enriched population, or widen the eligible population above 3,000 IU/mL. GSK dropping its own next-gen the day after approval reads as doubling down on one asset, not as a field-wide loss of interest. Arbutus after the $230M tender is mostly a royalty-and-litigation story, so the direct read is to ASMB's next-gen positioning.

One level deeper

How a biomarker-enriched label makes a cure rate look better than the drug: reading bepirovirsen's HBsAg cutoffs
Bepirovirsen's 19% functional cure rate is not a property of the molecule; it is a property of who was let into B-Well. The label copies the enrollment gate: non-cirrhotic, on a nucleos(t)ide analogue for six months with DNA suppressed, and HBsAg at or below 3,000 IU/mL. Inside that gate the rate climbs as baseline antigen falls, 26% at or below 1,000 IU/mL, which is the single most useful number in the release because it tells you the mechanism (antisense knockdown of HBsAg lets the immune system finish the job) only works when there is little antigen left to clear. Two analyst habits follow. First, size the market from the gate, not from the 250 million chronic HBV patients: the eligible pool is the NA-suppressed, non-cirrhotic, low-antigen slice, and I did not find a sourced share of treated patients who clear 3,000 IU/mL, so that is the first number to go find before modeling the launch. Second, when a competitor reports a functional cure rate, line up its baseline HBsAg distribution against this one before comparing; a 30% cure rate in an all-comers population would be a far bigger result than 26% in the enriched one. The boxed warning for on-treatment ALT flares is mechanism-linked (immune clearance causes hepatocyte injury), so a next-generation agent cannot claim a safety edge without also showing it still clears antigen. That is the frame for ABUS's imdusiran combinations and ASMB's capsid modulator: beat 19-26% in the same gate, or widen the gate above 3,000 IU/mL, or do neither and be a combination partner. GSK ending its own next-generation Phase 1 the day after approval says it has priced that decision too.
