The read

The swing. The 07:30 CT housing starts and permits print: a clear miss on top of the NAHB index at 29 would push 'housing recession' into the rate debate and pull yields lower, which helps stocks only if it does not read as a growth scare.

  • Treasury 10-year at about 5.10% this morning after 5.14% Monday, with Fed funds futures at 82% hold and 18% hike for October 28; rates, not data, set the equity tone this week.
  • Semiconductors ended an eight-session losing streak Monday with the Philadelphia index up 1.6% and the Nasdaq up 1.1%; futures point to modest tech gains.
  • GM beat on revenue ($50.0B vs about $48.8B) and narrowed full-year guidance upward with a $20B buyback; GE Aerospace, RTX, 3M and Northrop also print before the open, Netflix and Capital One after the close.
  • Oil: Brent near $110, a five-month high, on a Houthi attack on an LNG tanker and Iran tightening the Strait of Hormuz; European gas at about 90 euros per MWh is the highest since December 2022.
  • Europe is the soft spot: ZEW missed, the Stoxx 600 fell 0.8% Monday and the France-Germany spread is 148 bp; Asia was lower overnight with Hong Kong closed.

Pay attention to

  1. Housing starts and permits at 07:30 CT against 1.30M and 1.39M: anything under about 1.25M on starts with permits still falling confirms the NAHB signal and is the day's rates catalyst.
  2. The 10-year around 5.10%: a break back above Monday's 5.14% close into tomorrow's 20-year auction is the one thing that reverses the tech bounce.
  3. Brent near $110 and the 15:30 CT API crude number after last week's 11.1M barrel draw; energy is the swing factor for the November CPI path and the December hike pricing.
  4. GE Aerospace, RTX and Northrop before the open after GM's beat, then Netflix, Capital One and Intuitive Surgical after the close; Capital One's charge-offs are the cleanest consumer-credit read this week.
  5. Pending home sales and the GDPNow update at 09:00 and 10:00 CT: GDPNow at 3.6% versus a housing sector that is contracting is the gap the October 29 GDP print will have to reconcile.
