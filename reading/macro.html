Energy

Take: Nothing changed on Friday: Brent added 2 cents to $95.84 and WTI slipped 0.2% to $88.56, a second straight weekly decline. The tape is a standoff between an Iran headline premium and an IEA that now sees a surplus this quarter.
Crude
IEA calls the first quarterly surplus since the war began while ship attacks hit a monthly high
The IEA said the oil market will be in surplus this quarter for the first time since the war began and cited a significant hit to consumption this year. US crude and product inventories rose last week and refinery runs set a seasonal record. On the other side, Verisk Maplecroft counted 36 confirmed attacks on commercial ships in the Gulf, the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman in October, the highest monthly total of the war. Brent swung in a $2 range on Iran headlines and settled flat.
So what: Read: the barrels say lower and the headlines say higher, and Brent has now failed twice to hold above $96 on Iran news. If the surplus call is right, the path is the low $90s, which would take pressure off the headline inflation prints the Fed is hiking into. Record seasonal refinery runs also mean product cracks should ease, a negative for refiners that have been the winners. The risk to that read is one successful attack on a tanker.

Natural gas

Take: December Henry Hub fell 3.3% to $3.23 from Thursday's $3.337 settle after the storage print: a smaller build than expected, but the surplus to the five-year average still widened.
Storage
+61 Bcf vs about 65 expected; stocks at 3,862 Bcf, roughly 2% above the five-year average
EIA reported a 61 Bcf injection for the week ending Nov 6 against expectations of 65 to 67 Bcf, taking working gas to 3,862 Bcf. The surplus to the five-year average widened from 54 Bcf the prior week; sources put the new figure near 75 Bcf, which I did not confirm against the EIA table. EIA's November outlook, out Nov 10, cut its November to March Henry Hub forecast 16% to $2.87 and lifted end-November storage to 3,840 Bcf.
So what: Read: a bullish miss that sold off tells you positioning was long into the number after the run to a seven-week high. Any build in mid-November is late in the season, and EIA's own winter forecast sits 36 cents under the front month, so the market needs sustained cold to hold above $3.20. Record LNG feedgas near 19 to 20 bcfd is the floor. The next storage report is Thursday Nov 19 and the December contract expires Nov 25.

Data centers

Take: Announcements kept coming this week while the equity market sold the companies that finance them: IREN -6.7% and Applied Digital -5.8% on Friday. Direction of the build is still up, but customers are now paying developers in advance to reserve power, and local opposition is hitting the largest proposals.
Power deal
Fermi: investment-grade hyperscaler prepays $200M to reserve 600 MW at Project Matador
Fermi's Nov 12 release says an unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler will advance an initial 600 MW of capacity starting in 2027 at Project Matador in Texas, under a $200M prepayment agreement and a $15M early construction agreement; another $90M of funding is under negotiation. The lease is not signed. Management says 325 MW remains available for 2027. The stock had its best day ever on Friday. Press reports name Amazon as the likely customer; that is unconfirmed.
So what: Read: a hyperscaler paying $200M before a lease exists shows who holds pricing power: whoever can deliver power in 2027. It also moves funding risk from the developer to the customer, which matters with the 10Y above 5.1%. Stay skeptical on Fermi itself: prepayment is not a lease, revenue timing rests on construction milestones, and one customer is the whole 2027 story.

New build
Amazon breaks ground on its $7B campus in Wheatfield, Indiana
Amazon held the ceremonial groundbreaking on Nov 12 for a $7B campus on 304 acres in Jasper County, with more than 350 workers already on site and Ryan Companies as general contractor. Local commitments: $98M up front, $23M a year for ten years and $25M for water expansion in Wheatfield Township. MW, completion date and power supplier are not disclosed.
So what: Read: the community payments are the tell. About $353M in local money over a decade is now the price of a permit, and it sets the reference for every county negotiating the next one. The site sits near a NIPSCO plant per local coverage, consistent with the pattern of building next to existing generation.

Trend
Kraft Group's $16B, 900 MW Sutton, Massachusetts plan meets organized opposition
The proposal is ten buildings at 900 MW full build, the first two opening in 2029 and two more a year through 2033, for more than $16B. That load equals 7% of Massachusetts peak demand on last year's hottest day. No applications are filed; residents opposed it at the select board meeting and a local moratorium is under discussion. A state executive order requires local approval for any data center above 25 MW, and Kraft says it proceeds only if the town wants it.
So what: Read: New England has the least spare grid capacity in the country, so this is a test of whether a tax-hungry town (Sutton has a budget deficit) will trade land use for revenue. A local veto at 25 MW gives every Massachusetts town a block. Sites that already hold approvals and a power path gain value each time a proposal like this stalls, as in Ottawa, Kansas, which passed a 10-year moratorium this week.
