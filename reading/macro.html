Trade
USTR proposes Section 301 tariffs on 14 partners; Vietnam, Indonesia and Cambodia are on the list
On Nov 25 USTR proposed tariffs after an eight-month excess-capacity investigation covering China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, India, Taiwan, Vietnam, Indonesia, Cambodia, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Bangladesh and Switzerland. Rates mostly run 15% to 20%; China's 20% sits on top of about 25% in existing duties for roughly 45%. Greer said the rates work as a top-up, not an addition, and none takes effect for at least a couple of months; hearings are reported for January 2027.
So what: Read: this rebuilds the tariff wall the Supreme Court struck down in February on firmer legal ground, so it is more durable than the IEEPA version. For most partners the top-up design caps rates near last year's deal levels, which limits new inflation pass-through; China is the exception. NKE is the direct hit in your book: one source table shows Vietnam up to 35% and Indonesia and Cambodia up to 18%, and those three countries make most of Nike's footwear. That pressures the margin-normalization leg of your thesis until final rates and product lists are published. Japan called the proposal regrettable; Brazil did not rule out reciprocal measures.

Data centers

Take: The build-out continues, but two constraints tightened this week: local governments are pausing approvals in core markets, and neocloud financing costs are at records. Developers are answering the power side with behind-the-meter gas. Items below come from one project tracker; I did not open the underlying articles.
Power deal
Ohio approves 2,150 MW of behind-the-meter gas generation for data centers
The Ohio Power Siting Board approved 2,150 MW of on-site gas generation for Ohio data centers (listed Nov 20). Separately, Panattoni pitched a 300 MW campus near the Dayton airport in Union City (Nov 24). Dollar figures and timelines not found.
So what: Read: regulators are letting developers skip the grid queue by building their own gas plants, which shifts the bottleneck from interconnection to turbines and gas supply. That helps turbine makers and pipeline operators serving Ohio and shrinks the case for utility rate-base growth from these loads.

Loudoun County, Virginia paused data center applications for up to 12 months (Nov 19). Aurora, Colorado halted data center development until September 2027, and Vantage dropped its Trident site near Green Valley Ranch after protests (both Nov 24).

Opportunity radar

Small gas assets as the power anchor for data center sites
Buy producing or shut-in gas wells and gathering near fiber and developable land, then sell or lease the package to a data center developer as dedicated behind-the-meter fuel. The developer pays, because owned gas plus a siting approval skips the grid interconnection queue. The tracker lists Alpha Compute acquiring Pennsylvania gas assets for $5.5M to $6M to back a 430 MW campus.
Why now: Ohio's siting board approved 2,150 MW of behind-the-meter gas for data centers on Nov 20, while Loudoun County and Aurora paused new approvals. Demand is being pushed toward gas-rich states at the same time regulators are blessing on-site generation.
Upside: Read: a 430 MW campus burns roughly 70 to 80 MMcf/d at full load, about $90M to $100M of gas a year at $3.65. Assets that change hands for single-digit millions can underpin a site worth far more once power is secured. Realistic prize for a small operator is a low eight-figure sale of an assembled package, not ownership of the campus.
First step: Pull the Ohio Power Siting Board dockets for the 2,150 MW approvals to see who applied, what gas supply they showed, and what the board required. Then read the Alpha Compute deal terms. Both items come from a single tracker, so verify them first.
