Geopolitics
US remote-access chip rule hits Asia tech; Taiwan down about 3.9%, Hang Seng at a 4-month low
Commerce's BIS issued an interim final rule on November 16 requiring licenses for overseas data centers that serve China-headquartered customers with covered AI chips, and for chip exports to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. It also scraps the AI Diffusion Rule. On Thursday the Hang Seng fell about 1.9%, Tencent dropped as much as 6.8% after results showing slower revenue growth, and Taiwan's index fell about 3.9%.
So what: My read: the rule cuts off the offshore compute that Tencent, Alibaba and ByteDance rented in Southeast Asia, so their AI capex either moves to domestic chips or stalls. Losers are Malaysian and Singaporean data center operators with Chinese tenants and the server assemblers that ship there. Nvidia already guides China data center compute at zero, so the incremental hit to US suppliers is the Southeast Asia volume, which no one has quantified yet.

Crude
EIA: crude +3.4M bbl versus +58K expected; gasoline +5.2M, distillates +1.6M
For the week ended November 13, commercial crude rose 3.4M bbl to 417.8M against a Reuters poll of +58K. Gasoline rose 5.2M bbl and distillates 1.6M. Cushing fell 1.2M. Exports rose 1.03M b/d to 4.49M b/d. The prior week was a 4.7M bbl build against an expected draw.
So what: My read: builds across crude and both products in the same week point to soft demand, not a logistics quirk. With the IEA counting a 52M bbl global stock build in October, the market is trading surplus and treating Hormuz as a known quantity. Refiners lose crack support if product builds continue; US producers face a lower strip into 2027 budgets.

Supply
IEA annual outlook: Hormuz crisis has removed 2.8B bbl of oil and 60 bcm of gas, and is not over
The IEA's 2026 annual outlook, published today, calls the Hormuz disruption the largest energy supply shock on record, with cumulative Gulf losses of 2.8B bbl of oil and 60 bcm of gas as of November. Importers will spend about $1.6T on oil and gas in 2026, up 25%. In a prolonged-risk case, bypass pipelines and demand cuts shrink Hormuz flows from 20M b/d to 8.5M b/d by 2030.
So what: My read: the long-run message is bearish for Gulf pricing power. Bypass pipelines and substitution are permanent once built, and the IEA says a clean resolution would release a delayed wave of LNG into a buyer's market. That is a headwind for the 2028 to 2030 LNG price deck that US export projects are being financed on.

Trend
BIS rule puts licenses on chip shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil
The November 16 interim final rule requires a license for covered AI chips shipped to those five countries and for overseas data centers serving China-headquartered customers. Tencent, Alibaba and ByteDance have all signed offshore capacity deals in the region for access to top-end Nvidia chips.
So what: My read: Johor and Singapore campuses built around Chinese tenants now carry licensing risk on every GPU delivery, which slows lease-up and raises financing costs there. Demand that needs US chips migrates to US and allied sites, adding to an already tight US power queue.
