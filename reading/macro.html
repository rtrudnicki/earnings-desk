What mattered

Credit
AI capex is now priced as a credit problem, not a growth story
CoreWeave's 9.625% unsecured notes due 2032 traded around 79.5 cents (about a 15% yield) on Wednesday and the 2031 bond touched nearly 16%; 5-year CDS hit a record 1,194.5 bp vs 452 bp in June, implying more than 60% 5-year default probability. The company carries roughly $50bn of debt and Q2 interest expense was $640m, up about 140% YoY, against a $49m operating loss. Jensen Huang told Bloomberg Thursday that Nvidia does not need to backstop CoreWeave and that its cost of capital is 'actually decreasing'. CoreWeave and Nebius each lost more than 25% on the week; Oracle fell 4.6% Friday and is down 13% over 20 days.
So what: Read: the marginal dollar of GPU capacity is funded by junk debt and private credit, so a 5% 10-year does more damage to the build-out than any demand data point. Winners are hyperscalers that fund from cash flow (Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta); losers are neoclouds, Oracle's balance sheet and anyone lending against GPU collateral. Huang's 'cost of capital decreasing' claim is contradicted by the 15% print and should be treated as spin until a new facility prices.

Energy
Trump waives the Graham Act Russia sanctions for a diesel deal; Brent drops from a five-month high
Trump signed an order on Oct 16 waiving every operative Russia section of the Graham Act, including up to 500% tariffs on Russian goods and 100% tariffs on the top five buyers of Russian crude, two days before it took effect. He said Russia would make more than 300,000 tons of diesel available immediately; Rubio said the president will take up to 60 days to assess the impact. Brent fell 1.8% to about $107.90 and WTI 0.8% to $100.62 Monday after Brent crossed $110 Friday. Zelenskyy called the waiver wrong.
So what: Read: this is a diesel-price and midterms trade, not a supply fix. The waiver is revocable within 60 days and does nothing about Hormuz, where UKMTO counts at least 10 vessels attacked in a week and the IRGC reported another tanker on a mine this morning. Refiners and crack spreads lose on more Russian diesel; physical crude stays tight because insurance and shipping costs are the binding constraint, not sanctions.

Fed
Hot September CPI leaves an October hike live; Fed in blackout until Oct 29
September CPI rose 0.6% m/m and 3.7% y/y with gasoline up 9.1%, half the monthly increase; core rose 0.3% m/m to 2.6% y/y, both above consensus. The Fed hiked in September to 3.75-4.00%, the first hike in more than three years. CME odds of a second hike on Oct 27-28 ranged from 39% to 54% in the hours after the print and have since fallen after Chair Warsh declined to prejudge the meeting; sources this morning disagree on the level (13% to 52%), so I do not have a clean number. Odds of a hike by year-end exceeded 96%. 30-year mortgage rates are 7.4%.
So what: Read: the energy-driven headline gives Warsh cover to wait in October and hike in December, which is why the 2-year sits near 4.72% and the 10-year near 5.08% rather than higher. Treasury Secretary Bessent is already arguing the inflation is an energy shock. The risk is the opposite: core at 0.3% m/m is not an energy story, and a hike with Nasdaq already down 5.5% on the week would be the worst outcome for levered AI borrowers.

Geopolitics
Hormuz attacks at a wartime weekly high while Iran says the strait is closed
UKMTO reports at least 10 vessels attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz since last Sunday, the most in a week since the war began in February. Saturday the IRGC said a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels hit a mine and caught fire, with a second tanker explosion hours later; Sunday a tug was struck by two projectiles; Monday the IRGC reported another tanker on fire south of the strait. Houthi attacks on Saudi crude sites continue. Most mine reports rest on IRGC statements and are not independently confirmed.
So what: Read: physical crude trades at a premium to futures because of war-risk insurance, which is why the Russia waiver only moved Brent 2%. Tanker owners with uncommitted tonnage and US Gulf Coast exporters win; Asian refiners, India first, pay up. JKM LNG at $29.92 (+8.4% on the week) is the same trade in gas.
