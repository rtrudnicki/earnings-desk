One level deeper

Why "both trials met their primary endpoints" and a CRL can both be true: the statistical analysis plan is the evidence
Capricor says HOPE-3 met its primary endpoint; the FDA's July briefing documents said it missed, and the CRL (per Fierce) cites integrity violations that cannot be fixed after the fact. Both statements can be literally accurate because they refer to different analyses. A p-value only means something if the endpoint, the analysis population and the handling of missing data were locked before anyone saw unblinded results; that lock is the statistical analysis plan. If the plan changes after unblinding, or if blinding was compromised, the sponsor can pick the analysis that wins, and the nominal p-value no longer controls the false-positive rate. That is why the FDA treats a post hoc plan change as a defect in the trial, not in the paperwork: re-running the numbers cannot restore the pre-specification, so the only cure is new data. For you, the practical checks on any single-pivotal name are four: read the registry history for endpoint changes and their dates, compare the primary endpoint in the first protocol posting to the one in the topline release, look for the words "prespecified" and "per the original SAP" in the release, and read the FDA briefing book when there is an AdCom, because that is where the agency's own analysis first appears. A 9 to 3 vote against effectiveness plus a briefing book that disputes the primary is a stronger signal than any company statement, and the stock sat at $7.59 into the decision anyway. The second lesson is about resubmission classes: Capricor described the March class 2 designation as progress, but class 2 only sets a six-month review clock and says nothing about whether the deficiencies are resolved.
