Assembly dosed the first two participants in the Phase 2 study of ABI-6250, its oral once-daily NTCP inhibitor for chronic hepatitis delta (NCT07762027). FDA
Inovio's Voceryq wins accelerated approval in RRP on 7 of 26 surgery-free; INO +280% and Precigen -24% after hours
FDA granted accelerated approval Friday after the close to Voceryq (INO-3107, plasmid DNA immunotherapy with the CELLECTRA 5PSP electroporation device) for adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis. Basis: open-label single-arm RRP-001, 32 adults; in the 26 with at least three surgeries in the prior year (median 4.5), 7 (27%, 95% CI 12-48%) needed no surgery through 12 months. Nine-week regimen after surgical debulking; ordering in about 60 days, field launch Q1 2027; confirmatory protocol due to FDA by year-end. Inovio shares rose roughly 280-300% after hours; Precigen fell 24-27% from a $6.94 close.
Read-through: Second entrant, weaker single-arm data, accelerated rather than full approval, into a market where Precigen's Papzimeos did $74.7M in 1H 2026 with seven years of orphan exclusivity. Orphan exclusivity does not block a different active ingredient, so the Precigen risk is price, not share. Lesson for SMID: FDA accepted surgery frequency as an intermediate clinical endpoint on n=26, which is a permissive CBER read-through for other rare-disease single-arm filings.

Why a moving biomarker is not a derisked Phase 2: the Acumen ALTITUDE-AD lesson
Sabirnetug was built on the soluble amyloid-beta oligomer hypothesis: bind the toxic species, spare the plaque, and you should get the cognitive effect of lecanemab and donanemab with less ARIA. Friday's result inverted that: both doses significantly lowered amyloid PET at weeks 36 and 76, ARIA-E still hit about 10%, and iADRS at 18 months and CDR-SB showed no meaningful difference from placebo in 542 patients. The design trap is sample size against expected effect. Donanemab's TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 needed about 1,700 patients to show roughly 22% slowing on iADRS and about 29% on CDR-SB; a 542-patient Phase 2 is powered only to detect an effect materially larger than the approved antibodies deliver, so a miss was always the base case even if the drug worked like donanemab. That means a 'Phase 2 with clinical endpoints' in AD is a lottery ticket on a big effect, not a step that reduces the Phase 3 risk, and management describing it as 'registration-enabling if positive' is spin until you see the power calculation. Second lesson: a biomarker that moves while the clinic does not is the worst outcome for the hypothesis, because it removes 'we did not engage the target' as the excuse and leaves only 'the target is not the disease driver at this stage'. When you read any mechanism-first Phase 2 in your neuro names (DRUG's 5-HT2C agonist in epilepsy, BHVN's Kv7 opener), write down the effect size the approved comparator achieved, the n that took, and the n in front of you; if the trial can only win by beating the incumbent by two-fold, price it as a binary at the base rate for the mechanism, not at the probability the biomarker moves.
