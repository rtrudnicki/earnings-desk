Opportunity radar

Permitted behind-the-meter gas sites in ERCOT are the scarce asset, and the policy wind just shifted toward them
Assemble the package a developer cannot buy quickly: 300 to 1,000 acres on a 345 kV corridor and a high-pressure gas main, a TCEQ air permit for 300 to 750 MW of turbines or engines, water rights, and county tax abatement, then sell or option it to a developer or hyperscaler as a shovel-ready site. The buyers are the PowerHouse and Big Watt type developers and the neoclouds that need power in 2027, not 2030.
Why now: Trump's Oct 21 self-supply mandate, Microsoft cancelling a UK site over a 2030s grid date, the Taylor, Texas 1 GW / 750 MW gas approval, INNIO's 1.72 GW of follow-on engine orders from one customer, and PJM wholesale costs up 50% in H1 2026 all point the same way: power in hand is worth more than the building.
Upside: Raw land plus a permit application costs low single-digit millions over 12 to 18 months; sources above show developers paying for permitted power at campus scale but give no per-site price, so the prize is unquantified in the data I found. Read: the spread between an unpermitted parcel and a permitted one with a gas interconnect is the entire business, and it is widest right now in Texas counties outside the Austin and Dallas rings.
First step: Pull TCEQ air permit applications filed in 2026 for turbine or reciprocating-engine installations above 100 MW and cross them with the ERCOT large-load interconnection queue by county; the counties with gas and fiber but few applications are the targets, and the applicants who are landowners rather than operators are the people to call.
