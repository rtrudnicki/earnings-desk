CMPS
Reuters: FDA has shelved the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher program four days after Compass completed the COMP360 NDA
Reuters, citing six anonymous sources and internal documents, reported on Nov 12 that FDA is not moving forward with the CNPV program launched under former commissioner Makary, and that the political-appointee council that oversaw those reviews has gone dormant. Of 22 drugs with a voucher, 12 are approved, 5 of them since Makary left in May; no new vouchers have been awarded since. FDA's on-record statement says work under the pilot is ongoing and that career staff continue to evaluate applications granted a CNPV. BioSpace names Compass (NDA completed Nov 9) and Usona (PSIL201 in MDD) as holders with fresh submissions. RBC: this may add incremental risk to timelines, no added approval risk, PoS estimate held at 85%.
Sourcing quality: one wire report on anonymous sources, contradicted in part by FDA's statement, and nobody has said what happens to existing holders. The voucher was the basis for the 1 to 2 month review that Jefferies (approval as early as December or January) and Stifel (1Q27 or earlier) built into their dates, and for management's 30 to 60 day planning. Read: if the file falls back to a standard clock, COMP360 still has breakthrough designation and should get priority review, which is 6 months from the 60-day filing date, so about 8 months from Nov 9, landing near July 2027. DEA rescheduling then follows approval. That sequence does not fit a 1H27 launch. Cash is not the problem: $444.0M at Sept 30 against $51.0M debt, guided into 2028.
Thesis read: Your thesis is a data-and-approval binary at roughly fair value, and approval odds are unchanged by this. What changes is duration: the catalyst you were holding for could move from December-January to mid-2027, and the company's own 1H27 launch guide is now exposed. The new undated PDUFA row in your table is correct to stay undated. The thing to watch is the filing acceptance letter and whether Compass states a goal date; a stated date inside 1Q27 means career staff are honoring the voucher clock. I did not find a Nov 12 close for CMPS, so check the tape before deciding whether the timing risk is already in the price.

Kardigan pushes ataciguat and tonlamarsen toplines from 1H27 to 2H27; the 24-week ataciguat interim is gone
Q3 release, Nov 12. Ataciguat Phase 2b KATALYST-AV is fully enrolled (n=156); the primary endpoint is aortic valve calcium at Week 48 and topline is now expected in 2H27, with a Phase 3 update in 1H27. The release no longer mentions the 24-week interim that the August release had guided for 1H27. Tonlamarsen Phase 2 KARDINAL-ASH is still enrolling and topline is now expected in 2H27. Danicamtiv Phase 2b KINSHIP-DCM Cohort 2 is fully enrolled with topline in 2Q27, unchanged, and Phase 3 is enrolling both cohorts. Cash and investments $591.4M at Sept 30; Q3 R&D $64.4M, net loss $58.6M ($0.64 per share). No call.
The CEO's stated reason for the ataciguat change is that baseline valve calcium varies as much as it does in the company's observational dataset of about 45,000 mild-to-moderate aortic stenosis patients, so more time is needed to show a reduction versus placebo. Read: that is the company conceding a 24-week calcium read was underpowered against measurement noise, and choosing to wait for one clean Week 48 look over a noisy early one. It protects the trial and costs you six months. Tonlamarsen slipping while still enrolling is a plain enrollment delay. At $58.6M a quarter, $591.4M covers about 10 quarters (my arithmetic; the company gives no runway date), so all three readouts are funded. There is no approved drug that slows aortic stenosis, so the delay does not hand a competitor anything.
Thesis read: No stated thesis. Your table moved 13 KARD rows to 2027-12-31; those are duplicates of two events, and 2027-12-31 is a placeholder for 2H27, not a date. Danicamtiv in 2Q27 is now the first KARD readout and the only one inside the next 8 months.

Amgen's full dazodalibep Phase 3 data in Sjogren's: significant, and small
Amgen released full OASIZ-301 results around Nov 11 ahead of an ACR late-breaker. FDA
Class-wide GLP-1 label change for optic nerve stroke risk
On Nov 9 FDA required all GLP-1 receptor agonists to add non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy to Warnings and Precautions. The basis is a meta-analysis of 126 randomized trials: 6.4 cases per 100,000 person-years on drug versus 3.8 on comparators, from 15 probable events. Labels will advise prompt ophthalmology referral and consideration of stopping the drug. FDA says benefits still outweigh risks.
Read-through: Read: an absolute excess of 2.6 per 100,000 person-years will not change prescribing. Class labeling does mean every pipeline GLP-1, including GPCR's aleniglipron and KLRA's ribupatide, inherits the warning and will be asked about eye events in Phase 3 safety databases.

One level deeper

What a review clock is made of, and why losing a voucher moves a launch by more than the review time
An NDA has three clocks stacked on each other, and the CMPS story touches all of them. First, FDA has 60 days from submission to decide whether to file the application; the Day-74 letter then tells the sponsor the goal date and whether review is priority or standard. Second, the PDUFA goal for a new molecular entity runs from that 60-day filing date, not from submission: 6 months for priority, 10 for standard, so the true elapsed times are about 8 and 12 months. The national priority voucher sat outside this statute. It was a commissioner's pilot promising a 1 to 2 month review through a special council, with no user-fee commitment behind it, which is why it can lapse without a rule change and why FDA can say work is ongoing while six sources say it is over. Breakthrough designation, which COMP360 has, makes priority review likely but does not guarantee it and does not shorten the 6-month goal. Third, for a Schedule I substance there is a clock after approval: DEA must reschedule before a single prescription ships, and the statute gives it 90 days from the later of approval or HHS's recommendation to issue an interim final rule. So a psilocybin launch date is submission plus filing plus review plus up to 90 days. Run that from Nov 9 on a priority clock and you get approval near July 2027 and first sales in the autumn, against company guidance of 1H27 and sell-side dates in 1Q27. The lesson for any name: when a model's approval date rests on a discretionary program, ask what date the statute alone would give you, and treat the gap between the two as the risk you are being paid, or not paid, to carry.
