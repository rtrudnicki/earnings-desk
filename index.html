The read

The swing. Oil: Brent at $114 after the Saudi shipping attacks is doing more to the long end than any print today, and a slide back under $110 would extend Thursday's bond rally instead of reversing it.

  • US flash composite 57.6 matched the one consensus available, manufacturing beat at a 53-month high 56.0 and services was in line at 57.9, so there is no growth-scare in the US to pair with Thursday's AI capex selloff.
  • Selling prices in the US survey rose at the fastest pace since August 2022 and services inflation hit a 26-month high on fuel, wages and insurance, which is the line the Fed will read during blackout.
  • Michigan inflation expectations were confirmed at 4.7% one-year and 3.5% long-run, both the highest since May and rising for a second month, with no upward revision; sentiment itself revised up to 48.0.
  • Europe missed (eurozone composite 51.9 vs 52.7, France 48.4) while the UK beat at 53.1, which keeps the dollar bid at about 101.7 on the index despite the Treasury rally.
  • Fed futures at 10:25 ET: 10% for a hike on October 28, about 75% for at least one hike by December 9 and 7.5% for two; the 10-year is back near 4.97-5.00% after closing at 4.952% Thursday.

Pay attention to

  1. The 10-year into the close: it finished Thursday at 4.952% and is back near 4.97-5.00% after the prints, so a close under 5% means the AI-capex bid for bonds is outlasting the inflation data.
  2. Brent against $110 and $114: the services survey blamed fuel first for the fastest selling-price rise since 2022, so oil is the swing for December pricing, not the next data point.
  3. December pricing on Fed Rate Monitor after the US close: 67.6% one hike and 7.5% two at 10:25 ET; the two-hike tail is what should move if the prices-charged detail gets traded.
  4. Whether the Nasdaq extends Thursday's 2.6% drop; a second down day would re-run the growth-scare trade and pull yields lower against the data.
  5. Next week: the FOMC holds October 27-28 (no projections) with the market at 10% for a hike, and third-quarter GDP, PCE and payrolls follow, so today's prints set the tone but not the level.
