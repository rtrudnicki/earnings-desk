Energy
Gulf oil flows cut to 8.5 mb/d as tanker attacks hit 18 in two weeks; Brent back above $110
Kpler data cited by Reuters counts at least 18 tanker attacks in two weeks and puts daily oil flow out of the Persian Gulf at 8.5 million barrels, down from more than 20 million two weeks earlier. Brent printed $109.68 to $111.38 overnight, its highest since May; WTI $98.55 to $100.62. US Energy Secretary Wright separately said flows have recovered to about 80% of pre-conflict levels, which conflicts with the Kpler read. The cost to ship Middle East crude to China hit a record $1.4 million a day.
So what: Read: the two flow numbers cannot both be right, and the market is pricing the Kpler one. A $110 Brent with a 5.1% 10Y is the stagflation pair the Fed cannot cut into, which is why Trump waived the Graham Act Russia sanctions on Oct 16 to get Russian diesel moving before the midterms. Winners: tanker owners, US refiners with distillate exposure, domestic gas-weighted E&Ps. Losers: airlines (United reports tonight), European industrials, anything that needs the 10Y below 5%.

Energy

Take: Crude is bid on supply, not demand: WTI November settled $101.41 Friday, up 10.4% on the week, Brent $109.86, and both held above those marks overnight on the Hormuz flow data covered above. Washington is responding on the product side (SPR exchange, Russian diesel waiver) rather than on crude, which keeps the diesel crack the pressure point.
Products
Trump waives Graham Act Russia measures to free diesel; SPR offers a 17 million barrel exchange
On Oct 16 Trump signed an order suspending every operative Russia section of the Graham Act two days before it took effect, including the proposed 500% and 100% tariffs on Russian energy and its top buyers, with no expiry date; Bloomberg ties it to diesel prices before the midterms and a Kremlin agreement to release millions of tons of diesel. The same day DOE issued an RFP to exchange up to 17 million barrels from an SPR at about 283 million barrels, part of 172 million committed under the IEA's 400 million barrel action; a June offer with premiums up to 24% drew only about 500,000 barrels.
So what: Read: both moves target distillate, which says the administration sees the diesel crack, not headline crude, as the political problem. A near-empty June SPR exchange shows refiners do not want sour crude at these premiums; the binding constraint is refining and shipping, which favors US Gulf Coast refiners and product tankers over E&Ps.

Data centers

Take: Two tracks are diverging: debt-funded neoclouds are being repriced (CoreWeave at 15.8% yields) while balance-sheet hyperscalers keep signing utility-scale power deals and groundbreakings. Power procurement has moved to structures where the tenant funds the generation outright, and behind-the-meter gas orders are sold out through 2028.
Power deal
NiSource signs 20-year Microsoft deal: about 1,100 MW of new generation plus a 600 MW battery in northwest Indiana, service from 2028
Announced Oct 16: NiSource adds about 1,100 MW to its GenCo pool for a Microsoft campus (likely La Porte, Indiana) and contracts a 600 MW battery under a tolling agreement built by an AGP Group affiliate. Microsoft funds the generation and transmission under a new large-load rate class; NiSource projects about $1.75 billion of customer savings over the contract (up from $1.4 billion) and will give detail with Q3 results on Oct 28. Generation mix and capital cost were not disclosed.
So what: Read: this is the template regulators want, the tenant pays for the iron and ratepayers get a credit. It favors regulated utilities with spare interconnect in the Midwest over merchant generators, and it means hyperscaler capex now includes power plants, which inflates the headline capex numbers Meta reports Wednesday without adding a single GPU.
