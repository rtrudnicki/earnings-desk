One level deeper

Why a hepatitis delta 'win' can read 44% or 56% for the same drug class: composite endpoints and timepoints
Both HDV Phase 3s this weekend used the FDA-preferred composite of HDV RNA undetectable (or a 2-log drop, depending on the protocol) plus ALT normalization, because regulators want proof of both viral and biochemical benefit before a lifelong therapy is approved. The two halves do not move together. Viral RNA falls within weeks of starting an entry inhibitor or an siRNA plus antibody; ALT normalizes only after hepatocyte injury stops and the enzyme clears, and in cirrhotic or steatotic livers it may never reach the lab's upper limit. That is why Vir's ECLIPSE 1 shows 79% undetectable but 44% composite at Week 48, and why bulevirtide's MYR301 (Wedemeyer, NEJM 2023) also sat near 45% to 48% composite with far higher virologic rates. Mirum's AZURE-1 measured at Week 24 and reported 56% on one dose; a Week 24 composite can look better or worse than Week 48 depending on how many responders have had time to normalize ALT versus how many lose suppression later. So never line up composite rates across trials without matching the timepoint, the virologic definition (TND vs 2-log), the cirrhosis share (46 of 83 treated patients in ECLIPSE 1 were cirrhotic, 85% TND in that group), and the control (deferred treatment, which is 0% by construction). The number that compares most cleanly across programs is HDV RNA TND at a fixed week, and the number that will decide labels is the composite. When ECLIPSE 3 reports head-to-head against bulevirtide in 1Q 2027, the first thing to check is whether both arms are scored on the same composite at the same week; that is the only comparison that settles the class question, and it is the one neither company could have shown this weekend.
