Credit
Banks and Blackstone arrange $63bn to finance Broadcom chips leased to Anthropic
A special purpose vehicle buys Broadcom XPU accelerators and networking gear and leases them to Anthropic. Bank of America, Citi and Morgan Stanley arrange about $43.5bn (69%) of first-lien debt; Blackstone is sole second-lien provider; Broadcom gives residual-value support equal to the first-lien amount. Deliveries start calendar 2027. It follows a $35bn Apollo/Blackstone facility for Anthropic with a Broadcom backstop up to $29bn. The Wall Street Journal reported some lenders considered pulling out over pricing before the deal closed.
So what: Chip capex is migrating off hyperscaler balance sheets into lease vehicles with vendor backstops, so reported capex understates the build and the residual-value risk sits with Broadcom, not the lender. Semis rallied Tuesday on this. The 'lenders got nervous' detail is the part to keep: the marginal dollar of AI financing now needs a vendor guarantee to clear, which is how the late innings of a capex cycle usually look. Read, not fact.

No consensus for Thursday's number found; late-September analyst view was end-of-season storage near 3.84 Tcf on October 31, a three-year low.
So what: National storage is fine, but the Gulf Coast salt deficit is where LNG feedgas draws from, so a cold snap in Texas would tighten the exporters' supply even with the Midwest full. That is why cash trades below futures in Louisiana while the December contract holds above $3: the curve is paying for the salt gap, not for heating demand that the forecast says is not coming.
