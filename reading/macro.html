Overnight: One stock is the tape. The WSJ reported Nvidia's board is preparing to remove Jensen Huang, the shares fell 8 to 9% after hours, and Nasdaq futures gave back about half of Wednesday's CoreWeave-led rally while Russell futures sat flat. That split says single-name governance shock, not a macro repricing. Asia sold off on the same story (Hang Seng, Nikkei, Kospi all red), and crude slipped on Iran and Ukraine talk headlines plus a 9M bbl API build. Data flag: the snapshot's 10Y and 30Y rows (0.50, 0.54) look like a feed error; CNBC had the 10Y near 4.99% and the 30Y near 5.34% after Tuesday's hot CPI.

Day ahead

Lower · low confidence
My read: US equities open lower and stay heavy in tech, with the damage concentrated in Nvidia and its supply chain. I am anchoring on Nasdaq futures down about twice the S&P while Russell is flat, and on AMD and Broadcom trading up more than 3% after hours, which points to money rotating inside AI and not leaving it. Index downside looks contained unless the board news gets worse; a clean succession announcement could erase the gap fast, which is why confidence is low.
• Nvidia down 6 to 9% premarket with no board decision announced, five days before its Nov 17 print
• Rates backdrop is hostile: CME FedWatch at about 88% for a 25 bp December hike after October core CPI of 0.3%, 10Y near 5%, and five or six Fed speakers today
• Cisco and Applied Materials report after the close, so AI-hardware longs have a reason to wait and not buy the dip before 15:00 CT
Swing factor: An Nvidia board statement. Huang retained, or removed with a named interim CEO (Semafor says some directors favor CFO Colette Kress), pushes tech higher. A removal with no successor, or any widening of the foreign-intelligence probe, pushes the Nasdaq lower.

What mattered

Tech
Nvidia board met to decide whether to remove Jensen Huang; no outcome announced
The WSJ reported the board is preparing to oust Huang after reports of extramarital relationships, one with a woman suspected of spying for China. Shares traded near $159 after hours against a $173.73 close (down 8 to 9%); CBS puts the market-value loss since the Nov 8 probe announcement above $450B. Reuters, via one source, says the board met late Wednesday; Nvidia has not commented. Bloomberg reports an outside search firm is engaged.
So what: Read: the earnings power did not change overnight, the discount rate on the franchise did. Three second-order effects matter more than the stock move. First, Washington now has a national-security pretext to tighten China export rules (see next item). Second, AMD and Broadcom gained more than 3% after hours, so custom silicon and the second source get the bid. Third, the Nov 17 print turns into a governance call, and any interim CEO inherits supply commitments one aggregator puts at $279B. A fast, clean succession is the bullish outcome; a drawn-out probe is the bearish one.

Fed
Hot October CPI has a December hike priced at about 88%, with a 50 bp tail
October CPI rose 0.4% m/m vs 0.3% expected; core rose 0.3% vs 0.2% (released Nov 10; no y/y figure because October 2025 prices were never collected). CME FedWatch shows about 88.8% odds of a 25 bp hike at the Dec 8-9 meeting, up from about 72% a week earlier, and roughly 10% odds of 50 bp. Waller said before the print that 50 bp should be considered if core hit 0.3%.
So what: Read: cash bonds were shut Wednesday for Veterans Day, so today is the first full session where Treasuries and Fed speakers both react. With the 10Y near 5%, long-duration equities carry the risk, and Wednesday's rally ignored it. Any speaker endorsing Waller's 50 bp framing reprices the front end and hits small caps and housing first. Hikes into $92 Brent also mean the Fed is tightening against an energy-driven impulse it cannot fix, which is the stagflation setup the market is not pricing in equities.

Geopolitics
Commerce reportedly finalizing curbs on Chinese remote access to Blackwell-class chips
The Information reports Commerce may issue rules this month restricting Chinese remote access to Blackwell and newer chips, with licensing extended to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, Norway and South Africa, plus formal repeal of the AI Diffusion Rule. The WSJ reported Nvidia customer Megaspeed shipped restricted chips into China; Alibaba fell as much as 7.1% in Hong Kong. Sourcing: single-outlet reports relayed through an overnight aggregator, not a published rule.
So what: Read: the Huang scandal converts export control from a trade-negotiation chip into a security file, which makes it harder for the administration to soften. Losers are offshore neoclouds in the named countries that rent capacity to Chinese labs, and Chinese hyperscalers that depend on that rented compute. Winners are domestic Chinese accelerator vendors. A Xi and Trump meeting is slated for next week, so the timing of any rule is itself a signal.

Natural gas

Take: Henry Hub is breaking higher into the first real cold of the season with speculators at a record short. The EIA storage print moves to Friday 09:30 CT this week; I found no consensus estimate for it.
Curve
December gas settles at $3.296, highest close since Sept 24, against a record short
December Henry Hub settled up 7.8 cents at $3.296 on Nov 10 after forecasts turned colder. Money managers hold a record 636,576 short-only positions. A nor'easter is forecast late this week, with up to about 45 cm of snow in interior New England and New York from Thursday through Saturday.
So what: Read: positioning, not fundamentals, drives the next leg. Storage is still 54 Bcf above the five-year average (3,801 Bcf as of Oct 30) and EIA just cut its November to March Henry Hub forecast to $2.87, so the shorts have the data on their side and the weather against them. A second cold model run forces covering; a warm revision under a strong El Nino sends it back below $3.

LNG
LNG feedgas at a record 19.3 Bcf/d; TTF near EUR 87/MWh
Feedgas to US export terminals is averaging a record 19.3 Bcf/d, and BloombergNEF counted a record 21.6 Bcf/d of net flows on Saturday. Lower 48 output slipped to 112.1 Bcf/d in November. Europe's TTF rose 2.7% to about EUR 87/MWh, roughly $28/MMBtu; Asian LNG is near $29.
So what: Read: exports now take about 17% of US output, and a $25 spread to Europe means every available molecule leaves. That structurally tightens the domestic balance going into winter and raises the floor under Henry Hub even with a storage surplus. Exporters with uncontracted volumes capture the spread; gas-fired power buyers and on-site-generation data centers pay for it.

Data centers

Take: Direction is unchanged and still up: capex guides keep rising and new campuses are being designed around their own gas plants because the grid cannot deliver. The new friction is local, with zoning lawsuits and withdrawn projects showing up beside the announcements.
New build
SpaceX asks Starbase, Texas to approve a $24B gas plant and compute campus
A City of Starbase hearing notice posted Nov 6 describes a gas-fired plant of about 748 MW (35 turbines in the initial configuration) and a compute facility on about 444 acres, with an initial-phase investment near $24B. The tax-incentive hearing is reported for Dec 9. Figures come from secondary coverage of the notice, not the notice itself.
So what: Read: behind-the-meter gas is now the default design for anyone who needs power before 2029. That shifts the scarce asset from grid interconnects to turbines, pipeline laterals and air permits, and it adds firm gas demand in South Texas at the same time LNG feedgas is at a record.

FY26 capex guide is now $37B to $39B, with $14.5B spent in Q3 alone against $1.85B a year earlier.
