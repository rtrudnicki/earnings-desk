Sector pulse

FDA
FDA grants accelerated approval to Inovio's Voceryq (INO-3107) for adult RRP; shares up roughly 200% after hours
The FDA approved Voceryq on its Oct 30 PDUFA date for adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, Inovio's first approved product (Reuters). The BLA rested on the single arm Phase 1/2 RRP-001 study of 32 adults who had at least two surgeries in the prior 12 months, with surgery frequency as the endpoint. At filing acceptance in Dec 2025 the FDA had flagged a preliminary view that Inovio had not yet shown it qualified for accelerated approval; the agency approved anyway. Reuters put the after hours move at about 215%.
Read-through: Read: a plasmid DNA immunotherapy delivered by electroporation is now a commercial product, which revalidates a modality the market wrote off after COVID. The direct comp is Precigen's Papzimeos (approved Aug 2025, adenoviral vector), so RRP becomes a two product rare disease market and the launch fight is about dosing convenience and surgery reduction. Broader lesson for SMID: a stated FDA 'review issue' in a filing letter is a risk flag, not a verdict.

Readout
Acumen's sabirnetug misses in Phase 2 ALTITUDE-AD; company ends development, stock down more than 80%
Acumen said Oct 30 that neither the 35 mg/kg nor the 50 mg/kg arm beat placebo on iADRS at 18 months, and no key secondary endpoint hit. ARIA-E was seen in about 10% of treated patients. Sabirnetug was the company's only clinical asset; Acumen will stop the open label extension and review strategic options including its Enhanced Brain Delivery platform. Shares fell to about $0.33, down roughly 85%.
Read-through: Read: the oligomer selective amyloid hypothesis just lost its cleanest test. Amyloid plaque antibodies (Leqembi, Kisunla) work by clearing plaque; an antibody tuned to soluble oligomers with less plaque removal delivered nothing on cognition, which argues the clinical signal tracks plaque burden, not oligomer binding. Negative read for other oligomer or non-plaque approaches at the Phase 2 stage and a reminder that an Alzheimer's Phase 2 powered on a composite is a coin flip.

One level deeper

Post hoc subgroups cannot rescue a single arm trial: the Iovance KRAS pivot
Iovance's IOV-LUN-202 update is a clean case of a trap you will see every quarter. The prespecified population delivered 24.6% ORR (17 of 69), essentially the same as the 25.6% seen at 39 patients, so more enrollment confirmed the signal rather than growing it. The company then split the same 69 patients by KRAS status and found 43.3% (13 of 30) in wild type versus 11.4% (4 of 35) in mutant, and announced a biomarker enriched registrational cohort. The problem is that a split chosen after seeing the data has already consumed its luck: with half a dozen plausible biomarkers (KRAS, PD-L1, STK11, KEAP1, prior IO duration, tumor burden) you expect one to show a two to four fold gap by chance, and a 13 of 30 estimate carries a 95% confidence interval of roughly 25% to 62%. The only fix is what Iovance is doing, a new prospectively enriched cohort, but that means the current data are hypothesis generating and the clock restarts by about a year, which is why the stock gave back 40% over two sessions on an unchanged headline. When you model this, haircut the subgroup rate toward the overall rate (a shrinkage estimate near 30% to 35% is defensible), and ask what the comparator arm would have done: docetaxel plus ramucirumab runs about 20% to 23% ORR in second line nonsquamous NSCLC, so a single arm filing needs both a wide ORR margin and durable responses to clear accelerated approval. Note the asymmetry with Inovio the same day: the FDA accepted a 32 patient single arm study for a rare disease because the endpoint (surgeries avoided against each patient's own prior year) has no post hoc degrees of freedom. Single arm works when the endpoint is anchored before the data arrive, not after.
