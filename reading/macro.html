Nvidia falls 14% on reported SEC accounting inquiry; semis lose 10.7% in a day

ANT trading 7% above its IPO price two days in removes the newest-issue bid from the group. Skeptical note: an inquiry is not a finding and many close without action.

Policy
Trump threatens a 'tremendous tax' on US firms building data centers abroad
On Friday Trump said he would stop US companies from building data centers outside the country or tax those that do, named a Google project in Finland, and gave no legal mechanism; CNBC reported the statement surprised his own staff. It followed the Commerce interim final rule of Nov 16 that requires licenses for advanced chips going to overseas data centers serving Country Group D:5 or Macau customers above 12 million aggregate TPP, with a 90-day window for existing sites.
So what: Read: no authority is identified, so treat the tax as a threat with a low near-term chance of becoming law, yet it reprices where the next gigawatt gets built. Losers if it sticks: Nordic, Gulf and Southeast Asian campuses that depend on US hyperscaler tenants, and Malaysia most under the Commerce rule. Winners: US sites with power in hand, and domestic gas turbine, transformer and switchgear suppliers, because demand that cannot go abroad lands on a US grid that is already the binding constraint. It also raises hyperscaler cost per MW, which feeds the capex-cut worry already in the tape.

Data centers

Take: Sentiment on the build-out turned last week faster than the spending did. No hyperscaler has cut capex, and UBS still projects $1.009T for 2026, but a BofA November survey shows 52% of fund managers expect a cut inside six months (30% in October), power-equipment names sold off with chips (Bloom Energy fell 15.3% Friday), and Washington is now trying to dictate where campuses get built.
Power deal
Fermi signs 600 MW with a hyperscaler at Project Matador; TensorWave lease dies
Fermi reported a 600 MW power arrangement with an investment-grade hyperscaler at its Project Matador campus in Texas, with the customer funding $15M through an early construction and site development agreement. In the same announcement it confirmed the TensorWave lease of up to 650 MW, underwritten by AMD, is terminated. Reported in the past week; exact date not confirmed.
So what: Read: an investment-grade tenant replaced a neocloud tenant on the same megawatts, which is the pattern to expect if GPU financing tightens. Second-tier AI clouds lose sites and the hyperscalers pick up powered land at better terms. $15M of committed cash against 600 MW is an option, not a lease, so the headline overstates the commitment.
