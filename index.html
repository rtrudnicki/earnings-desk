The read

The swing. The ten-year yield, sitting near 5.11% to 5.13% and up a few basis points on the day: a push back through last week's highs around 5.20% turns the tech bounce into another sale, while a bid after the NAHB miss and the 10:30 bill auctions lets it run.

  • Builder sentiment fell to 29, the lowest since June 2012, with buyer traffic at 18 and 43% of builders cutting prices; the first hard sign the 5% ten-year and 7.40% mortgage are hitting activity.
  • Canada's September CPI came in at 3.1% y/y, under the 3.2% consensus, with core measures easing to 1.8% to 1.9%; the energy shock is not yet spreading into core north of the border.
  • Brent is down about 1.7% to around $108 and WTI near $100.50 as Middle East supply holds near pre-war levels despite three tankers damaged in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Chips and AI names are bouncing after the Nasdaq lost about 4.5% last week and semis posted their longest losing streak since 2008; Intel is up near 5% on a JPMorgan upgrade.
  • Fed blackout through October 29 after Chair Warsh gave no steer on the October 28 decision; markets price roughly an 83% hold next week and a 75% to 90% chance of a December hike depending on the source.

Pay attention to

  1. The 10:30 CT 13-week and 26-week bill stops: last week's 4.085% and 4.200% embed a December hike, so watch whether the 26-week stop drifts lower after the housing miss.
  2. The ten-year around 5.11% to 5.13%: the tech bounce only survives the afternoon if the long end stays under last week's highs near 5.20%.
  3. Homebuilders and building products after the NAHB print: a weak close in that group with the broad tape up confirms the market is reading it as a sector problem, not a macro turn.
  4. Oil near $108 Brent with three tankers damaged in the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend: any renewed spike reverses today's bid in bonds and tech.
  5. Tomorrow's set-up: Netflix, GE Aerospace, GM and Capital One report, the Atlanta Fed GDPNow updates from 3.6% at 10:00 CT, and no Fed speakers until the October 28 decision.
