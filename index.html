The read

The swing. Fed Chair Warsh's opening remarks at 11:00 CT at the San Francisco Fed productivity conference: a hint that 50 bp is off the table for December would lift stocks; Newsquawk expects little guidance, which leaves the chip selling in charge.

  • Near 09:45 CT (Yahoo ticker): S&P 500 7,475.85 (-1.13%), Nasdaq Composite 24,692.52 (-1.77%), Dow -0.56%, Russell 2000 -1.19%, VIX 21.42 (+11%); the cash session is weaker than futures were at 04:30 CT (S&P -0.3%, Nasdaq 100 -0.9%).
  • Empire State index 7.8 against 18 expected and 25.5 in October; new orders 3.0 from 23.2, prices paid 45.9 (down fourteen points), employment 18.1, the highest since June 2022.
  • Canada October CPI 3.1% y/y against 3.2% expected and 0.2% m/m against 0.4%; CPI-trim 1.8%.
  • Rates and oil eased: 10-year Treasury about 5.12% (down 4 bp from Friday's 5.15% close) and December crude 86.67 (-2.1%) on the Yahoo ticker, so the stock decline today is not a yields story.
  • Chips lead the decline again with Nvidia lower a day before its Tuesday report (Newsquawk US equity open); Kansas City Fed President Schmid said inflation is running too hot.

Pay attention to

  1. Warsh at 11:00 CT: his first words since the hot October CPI; the market wants any hint on 25 bp versus 50 bp in December, and Newsquawk expects him to avoid guidance.
  2. Whether the S&P 500 holds its morning low near 7,475: yields and oil are both lower, so if stocks keep falling the selling is about chips and positioning ahead of Nvidia on Tuesday.
  3. The 10-year near 5.12%: the Empire prices paid drop to 45.9 and the cooler Canadian CPI pulled it off Friday's 5.15%; a move back up after Warsh would undo the one support stocks have.
  4. Hammack at 13:15 CT (Saxo listing): she dissented for a hike in October, so her tone marks the hawkish edge of the committee.
  5. Tuesday 07:30 CT retail sales (0.2% expected after 0.8%, Finviz): after today's Empire miss a second soft activity number would turn a chip selloff into a growth worry.
