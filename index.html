The read

The swing. The 12:00 CT 5-year auction: a tail above 5.05% on the day before a Fed that is expected to hold but signal December would reprice the whole curve and knock equities.

  • Consumer confidence jumped 7.5 points to 91.7 against an 81 consensus, with September revised up to 84.2; the drop in gasoline did it, so the consumer is not cracking the way the September print suggested.
  • Durable goods beat on aircraft (+1.8% vs +1.5%) but core capex rose only 0.5% vs 0.7% and Richmond Fed new orders fell to -8; manufacturing is flat, not booming.
  • New home sales 676k beat 650k, yet August was revised down and median prices are down 3.9% y/y with 8.4 months of supply; housing is stable, not recovering.
  • The ADP weekly pulse slowed to 16.75k jobs a week with the prior week revised down; hiring is the one soft spot the Fed can point to if it wants to hold in December.
  • Fed funds futures price a 90.6% hold for Wednesday; December hike odds sit around 71-73% on CME and 76% on Polymarket, and today's data gave no reason to cut those.

Pay attention to

  1. The 12:00 CT 5-year auction tail and indirect takedown; last month's 5.033% stop is the reference and a weak auction is the one thing that could break the bond calm before the Fed.
  2. Whether the ten-year can hold below 5.00% into the close now that the consumer confidence and durables beats are priced; a break back above 5% would weigh on the Nasdaq into the megacap prints.
  3. December hike pricing on CME FedWatch after today's data; it opened the day near 71-73% and a move above 80% means the market is front-running Wednesday's statement.
  4. Oil after the 15:30 CT API inventory number with WTI at $101 and Brent near $115; another draw feeds the inflation-expectation line the Fed is watching.
  5. Tonight's China open and the tech selloff there; it has been the overnight driver for US futures this week more than any US data.
