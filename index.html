The read

The swing. Tokyo core CPI at 17:30 CT (3.0% expected from 2.6%): a hot number with the yen at 159.31 lifts global yields into Friday, and it lands on top of an ECB account that already showed a large group ready to hike.

  • Nothing was released in the US at 07:30 or 09:00 CT: Thanksgiving, with claims and the income and outlays report pulled forward to Wednesday.
  • Tariffs: the White House proposals cover 14 major trading partners including China, Japan and the EU, will not stack on the July tariffs and are unlikely to be imposed until February or March (Reuters).
  • S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures rose about 0.5% each before the halt, against Wednesday's 7,182.78 close; STOXX +1.6%, Nikkei about +1.6%, Kospi about +1.8%.
  • The ECB account of the Oct 28-29 meeting: unanimous hold at 2.50%, inflation risks to the upside, and a large number of members could have supported a hike then.
  • Fed pricing did not move: a 25 bp hike on Dec 9 is 98% priced and talk of 50 bp has faded; Brent $95.1 (+0.5%), US crude $84.51 (+1%), dollar index 103.78, gold $3,565 (-0.4%).

Pay attention to

  1. Tokyo core CPI at 17:30 CT (3.0% expected, 2.6% prior): the only number left today, and it lands with the yen at 159.31, a step from the 160 intervention line.
  2. Retaliation headlines on the tariff proposal: China has reserved the right to respond, and the calm rests on the duties not stacking and not arriving before February or March.
  3. Friday's half session (stocks close 12:00 CT, bonds 13:00 CT): whether S&P 500 futures hold 7,200 when they reopen this evening, after rising about 0.5% to roughly 7,225 before the halt.
  4. Oil into the weekend OPEC+ meeting: Brent is back at $95.1, and the decision lands while markets are closed, so it is gap risk for Monday.
  5. The December pair: Fed on Dec 9 (98% priced for 25 bp) and ECB on Dec 17 (fully priced for 25 bp), with the Dec 4 jobs report the last big test before both.
