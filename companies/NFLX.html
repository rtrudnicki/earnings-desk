Company overview

Tue Oct 20

Netflix runs a paid streaming entertainment service: series, films, live events, games and now video podcasts and creator shows, reaching an audience it says is approaching 1B people. Paid memberships were above 325M at Q1 2026 (it no longer reports them quarterly)

How it makes money. Monthly membership fees across tiers, including an ad plan, are most of revenue; advertising is guided to about $3B of the $51.0B to $51.4B 2026 revenue (about 6%). Q2 2026 revenue by region: US and Canada $5.43B, EMEA $4.03B, LATAM $1.58B, APAC $1.51B. The main cost is content amortization (up about 10% in 2026) on about $20B of cash content spend

Bull case

Analysis: at about 18x 2027 EPS (Deutsche Bank cites about 40x at the 2025 peak) the stock prices a growth stall that the numbers do not yet show: revenue still +12% guided for Q3, ads doubling to about $3B, US price rise landing as planned, operating margin up 200 bps to 31.5% in 2026, FCF about $12.5B funding record buybacks ($4.7B in Q2, $27.1B authorization left)

Bear case

Analysis: growth has slowed four quarters running (17.6% in Q4 2025 to 11.7% guided for Q3 2026), engagement is losing time to YouTube and short-form video (HSBC, Wells Fargo, Pivotal), Netflix cut its view-hours report to once a year, the Warner Bros deal is gone, and live sports adds cost faster than viewing (just over 5% of content spend for about 1% of hours). Street 2027 EPS growth is only about 6%
