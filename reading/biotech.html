One level deeper

What a PDUFA date is worth: major amendments, three-month extensions and missed goals
A PDUFA date is a performance goal the FDA agreed to in exchange for user fees, not a legal deadline, and this week showed all three ways it bends. Vertex is the standard case: the agency sends an information request late in the cycle, the sponsor's answer is large enough to be classed a major amendment, and the goal moves by three months. That extension can be used only once per review cycle, which is why Savara is the more worrying case: it used its extension in April, so when the FDA could not finish by Nov 22 there was no mechanism left and the agency simply said it would miss, with no new date and no penalty. Praxis is the benign version, where sensitivity analyses of existing data triggered the same three-month move from Sep 27 to Dec 27. The analytical trap is treating an extension as neutral. It tells you nothing about approval odds by itself, but the trigger matters: a request for new analyses of efficacy data late in review means a reviewer is unconvinced on something, while a CMC or labeling request usually means the clinical review is done. Sponsors rarely say which, and Vertex did not. For position sizing, the practical rule is that the goal date is the earliest likely decision, not the expected one, so options expiring the week of a PDUFA carry timing risk on top of outcome risk. For MLTX, the same capacity constraint shows up earlier in the process: Priority Review is discretionary, and an agency that is extending and missing goals on filed applications has little reason to volunteer for a six-month clock.
