Trend
Local permitting is now a coin flip: Oklahoma City approves 6-3 with a water cap, DeSoto County FL denies 2-2, Oro Valley AZ hears a 20 MW ban, Chatham NC loses in court to a 750 MW project
Oklahoma City council approved zoning for Beltline Energy's first mega-data center 6-3 with a 75,000-gallon daily water cap and a 55-decibel noise limit; Beltline funds a new OG&E substation. DeSoto County, Florida, deadlocked 2-2 on DCIP Group's gas-powered hyperscale campus on about 826 acres. Oro Valley, Arizona, held a hearing October 28 on banning facilities of 20 MW or more. In Chatham County, North Carolina, a Superior Court judge ruled Eco TIP West's 750 MW Moncure project can proceed despite the county moratorium and ordered the county to pay damages; the developer had spent more than $11M.
So what: Entitled, power-committed sites are becoming the scarce asset as counties close the door after the first projects. The Chatham ruling is the first clear precedent that a moratorium cannot stop a vested project and costs the county money, which weakens every pending moratorium in the state. Read: the next wave of deals is land with LOAs and vested rights, not raw acreage.

Opportunity radar

Entitled, power-committed data center land in counties that are closing the door
Option or buy parcels that already hold data center zoning, a utility letter of authorization for a substation or large-load interconnect, and vested rights, in jurisdictions that are now passing moratoria or bans. The buyer is a hyperscaler or a developer like DCIP, Beltline or Eco TIP that needs a site the county can no longer block; Google's West Creek diligence shows the hyperscalers pay for the grid commitment, not the dirt.
Why now: In 48 hours: Oklahoma City approved its first mega-campus with a water cap, DeSoto County Florida deadlocked on a gas-powered campus, Oro Valley Arizona heard a 20 MW ban, and a North Carolina judge ruled Chatham County's moratorium cannot stop a vested 750 MW project and owes the developer damages. Supply of permittable sites is shrinking while Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon run $45B to $61B a quarter of capex each. Iran's Rezaei naming US data centers as targets adds a premium for inland, off-coast sites.
Upside: The spread between agricultural-zoned land and a site with zoning plus a signed interconnect. No sourced per-acre figures found overnight, so the prize is unsized here; the only hard number is the $11M Eco TIP West had sunk before the court protected its rights, which is the floor of what a vested site is worth to its holder.
First step: Pull the EEI large-load projects and tariffs list (October 2026) and the Dominion West Creek SCC docket to map which named loads hold LOAs but no public site; then screen counties with moratoria under litigation or drafting rules (Chatham NC, Hutchinson KS, Rochelle IL, Fayetteville NC) for parcels with applications filed before the moratorium date, which the Chatham ruling now treats as vested.
