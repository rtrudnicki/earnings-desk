One level deeper

Reading the FDA review clock: Day 60, Day 74, and the three-month major amendment (MLTX, Agios, Cogent)
MoonLake's two December dates are not two events, they are two letters on one clock. After a BLA is submitted FDA has 60 days to decide whether to file it (acceptance or refuse-to-file), then sends a Day 74 letter that states the review designation and the goal date. A BLA for a new molecular entity gets 10 months from the Day 60 filing date under standard review (about 12 months from submission) or 6 months from filing under priority (about 8 months), which is why MoonLake frames the Day 74 letter as the thing that decides a 1H27 versus 3Q-4Q27 launch. Agios shows the other lever: a goal date can move after the fact. If the sponsor submits a 'major amendment' inside the last three months of review, including a response to an FDA information request that the division classifies as major, the goal date extends by exactly three months once per cycle; that is how Nov 1 became Feb 1, and it is a labeling negotiation, not a verdict. The trap is reading an extension as a soft CRL or reading a priority grant as a view on efficacy; priority is about unmet need and is decided before the data are reviewed in depth. Cogent's three dates (Nov 30, Dec 30, Jun 29) show the last rule: each application or supplement runs its own clock, so one molecule can carry three independent binaries. When you model a PDUFA, write down the submission date, the implied Day 60 and Day 74, whether the goal is standard or priority, and whether there is room for a major amendment; most 'surprise' date changes are visible in that arithmetic.
