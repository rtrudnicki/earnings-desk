What mattered

Fed
Hot October CPI puts a December hike near certain; Waller floats 50bp
October CPI rose 0.4% m/m against 0.3% expected, with core at +0.3%, also above forecasts; energy was the stated driver. CME FedWatch shows about 91% odds of a 25bp hike at the Dec 8-9 meeting, up from 84% Monday, and 10% to 13% odds of 50bp. Governor Waller said he would back a December hike even if inflation cooled and that 50bp should be considered if price pressure worsens; Dallas Fed's Logan said at least two more quarter-point hikes are needed.
So what: Read: the debate has moved from whether to how much, and the 10Y at 4.98% sits one bad print from 5%. That is a headwind for long-duration equities with no earnings (XBI fell 1.2% Tuesday), for housing (30-year mortgage rate already 7.3%), and for levered small caps. Friday's PPI is the next input; Brent off 25% from its late-October peak argues the energy impulse in CPI fades in November, which the market is not crediting yet.

Tech
Nvidia down 5.5% for a second day on CEO and China-shipment reports
Nvidia fell 5.5% Tuesday to a market value near $4.07T, its lowest since late March. Coverage ties the drop to reports about CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese spy and reports of an internal probe into alleged chip shipments to China. These are press reports; I found no company filing or government action.
So what: Read: this is a governance and export-control risk, not a demand problem, which is why CoreWeave and the memory names can rally while Nvidia lags. The tail to watch is Washington: a Commerce or congressional response on China shipments would hit Nvidia's revenue mix directly and would reach the whole AI trade. Absent that, the stock is the cheapest way for funds to cut AI exposure and it will keep absorbing that selling.

Crude
Brent settles $91.49 after a sub-$90 print on Iran de-escalation talk
Brent settled Tuesday at $91.49 (-1.17%) with a low of $89.16; WTI settled $86.04 (-0.93%). Trump said Monday the nearly nine-month war is "going to be ending fairly soon" and reports say he is not planning new attacks. Hormuz crude flows have largely recovered, but the US carried out what Trump called minor strikes after missiles hit Qeshm Island.
So what: Read: the market is pricing a peace deal that is not signed, with a naval blockade still in place and both sides disputing control of the Strait. Today's rebound in Brent, larger than WTI's, says some of that premium is going back in. Lower crude is the one thing working against the Fed-hike story; a reversal would hit stocks and bonds together.

Natural gas

Take: Henry Hub is holding near $3.30 with no price event overnight. EIA's November outlook, out Tuesday, says a warm start to the month is pressuring prices and lifts its end-November storage estimate; the news with numbers is on the LNG side.
Storage
EIA raises end-November storage forecast to 3,840 Bcf
The November Short-Term Energy Outlook (Nov 10) expects 3,840 Bcf in storage at the end of November, 67 Bcf more than last month's forecast, and cites a warm start to November as the pressure on prices. I did not find this week's storage consensus or confirmation of the release day in a holiday week.
So what: Read: a fuller starting point for withdrawal season caps the front of the curve unless cold arrives in December. The bull case for gas stays structural (LNG feedgas, power burn for data centers), not seasonal.
https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_full.pdf
LNG
Orlen cancels a QatarEnergy long-term contract; Venture Global targets the top export spot
Poland's Orlen became the first long-term QatarEnergy buyer to cancel an LNG contract as force majeure continues; Hormuz LNG transits were 31 tankers in October, up from 27 in September and the highest since February. Venture Global says CP2 starts up in July 2027, ahead of schedule, and targets more than 100 million tonnes a year of capacity by 2030; its Q3 net income was $2.55B against $429M a year earlier.
So what: Read: a buyer walking away from Qatar on reliability grounds is the opening US exporters want, and it supports long-dated Henry Hub demand more than any weather forecast. If other Qatari buyers follow, the contracts go to the US Gulf Coast, and feedgas demand rises on a 2027 to 2030 schedule.

Data centers

Take: Capex direction is still up: CoreWeave raised its revenue guide and holds about $9B of customer prepayments, and Siemens Energy's record quarter shows the power-equipment orders behind the build. I found no named groundbreaking or completion dated Nov 10 or 11; the one new item is a gas-powered supply plan.
Power deal
Venture Global plans 5 GW of gas-fired data center power at its LNG campuses by 2031
Venture Global plans to turn excess land and gas-fired generation at its Louisiana LNG sites (Calcasieu Pass, CP2, Plaquemines) into hyperscaler data center hubs: 5 GW available by 2031, rising to 10 GW by 2034. It is expanding turbine and transformer contracts with GE Vernova and Baker Hughes. No customer is named and no dollar figure is given.
So what: Read: this is a plan, not a signed lease, and the company has an interest in talking it up after a 73% run in the stock. The logic is real, though: an LNG exporter already owns gas supply, turbines, land and an islanded grid, which removes the interconnection queue that stalls most projects. It also competes for the same GE Vernova turbine slots every other developer is waiting on, which tightens that bottleneck further.
