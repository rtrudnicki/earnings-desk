The read

The swing. The 12:00 CT 5-year TIPS auction: a strong bid at a 2.4%-plus real yield extends the bond rally and steadies stocks; a tail sends the 10-year back toward 5.1% and pulls tech down further.

  • Initial claims 194k vs 200k expected, lowest in three months, but continuing claims rose 25k to 1,728k, the highest in six weeks.
  • LEI flat in September with the six-month rate at +0.6%; the Conference Board says no recession signal and sees 2.2% real GDP growth in 2026.
  • Brent above $111 and WTI near $100 after the New York Times reported US and Israeli plans for renewed strikes on Iran after the November 3 midterms, plus attacks on Saudi energy sites.
  • Tesla fell more than 3% at the open despite an EPS beat on a small revenue miss; Meta fell more than 3% despite a beat and a 2-for-1 split, dragging the Nasdaq down about 1.4%.
  • CME FedWatch odds of an October 28 hike fell to 8% from 16% a day earlier and 28% a week earlier; the 10-year yield fell 13 bp to about 5.02%, a one-month low, and the 2-year to 4.718%.
  • European stress: euro area consumer confidence -17.9 vs -17.0 expected, French 10-year OAT at 5.126% (highest since 2002), UK September CPI 3.5% with core 2.8%.

Pay attention to

  1. The 12:00 CT 5-year TIPS auction bid-to-cover and the stop versus the when-issued real yield near 2.5%: it is the cleanest read on whether the term-premium demand that pushed the 10-year above 5% is fading.
  2. Kansas City Fed prices paid and prices received at 10:00 CT: the September finished-goods price index was at a July 2022 high, and a repeat argues against the market's 8% hike odds.
  3. Whether the 10-year holds below 5.05% into the close with Brent above $110; the bond rally and the oil spike cannot both be right for long.
  4. Tesla and Meta intraday: both beat and both fell more than 3% at the open, which says positioning, not fundamentals, is driving tech into next week's remaining megacap prints.
  5. Japan core CPI at 18:30 CT for Friday's open: consensus 2.1% from 1.7%, and a print above 2% with the yen near 157.5 revives Bank of Japan hike talk.
