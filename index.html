The read

The swing. Oil on the Iran headlines: a confirmed de-escalation knocks WTI back under $95 and lets stocks rally through the hot data, while a fresh strike puts the 10-year back at 5%.

  • JOLTS openings 7.523M vs 7.10M consensus with August revised up 111k; the openings rate rose to 4.5% and layoffs fell to 1.64M, nothing in it for the slowdown camp.
  • Quits jumped 321k to 3.365M (rate 2.1%), a wage-pressure signal that sits badly with a Fed already hiking into oil-driven inflation.
  • Factory orders +1.8% and ex-transportation +1.3% vs +0.4% consensus, stacked on Monday's strong ISM; the manufacturing rebound is real and broad.
  • Redbook same-store sales accelerated to +10.4% y/y, so the consumer is not cracking either.
  • 10-year up 4 bp to 4.935% and 2-year up 2 bp to 4.608% before the data; December hike odds were 77% on CME FedWatch this morning per Schwab and the data only push them higher.
  • Midterm Election Day plus Apple's 45-46% gross margin guide: Dow futures opened down 323 points while the Nasdaq held, so sector rotation rather than a directional tape.

Pay attention to

  1. The 2-year yield and December hike odds after the JOLTS and factory orders beats: anything that moves pricing toward a fully priced December hike shows the data, not oil, is driving the front end today.
  2. WTI around the $95 to $97 band: a close back above $97 on Iran retaliation reverses the morning's bond relief and puts the 10-year back at 5%.
  3. The 6-week bill auction at 10:30 CT and Wednesday's refunding announcement for any sign of bill-heavy financing with the 10-year near 5%.
  4. Total vehicle sales through the afternoon against the 16.0M prior; a hold above 16M would make four hot consumer datapoints in two days (ISM, Redbook, JOLTS quits, autos).
  5. Midterm results tonight: a Democratic House with a Senate toss-up is Schwab's base case, and a 'buy on the news' bounce needs no surprise there.
  6. Thursday's weekly claims and Friday's October payrolls (Reuters consensus +65k, unemployment 4.2%) are what decide whether the JOLTS jump is a trend or a low-response-rate artifact.
