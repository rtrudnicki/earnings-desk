Data centers
Texas elects a governor who pledged a data center moratorium
AP called the Texas governor's race for Gina Hinojosa (D) at 11:17 p.m. ET Nov 3, the first Democratic governor since 1990. She campaigned on a moratorium on new data centers until the Legislature reconvenes, plus stricter water and power rules and an end to tax breaks. Jefferies estimates 13.3 GW of announced Texas projects are at risk; Texas is ~25-26% of planned US capacity through 2030. Data-center and AI-linked names fell premarket.
So what: Read: the governor cannot impose a statewide moratorium alone, and the regular session does not convene until January 2027, so near-term the risk is permitting delay through agencies, not cancellations. Abbott already paused new grid connections pending a PUC/ERCOT audit in August, so Texas sites with signed interconnection gain scarcity value; unpermitted queue positions lose it. Expect capacity to shift toward Oklahoma, Louisiana and New Mexico gas-served sites. Watch for operators (Oracle/OpenAI Abilene, Crusoe, CoreWeave) addressing Texas exposure on calls.

Permitted, gas-served data center land just outside Texas

Why now: Texas has an existing grid-connection pause (Abbott, Aug 2026) and now an incoming governor pledging a moratorium; Jefferies puts 13.3 GW of announced Texas projects at risk. Upside: Read: entitled, powered sites have traded at multiples of raw land value; a single 200 MW package with a utility commitment can be worth tens of millions over its option cost. The size depends on how long Texas stays closed, so treat it as a 6-18 month window.
