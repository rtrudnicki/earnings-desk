One level deeper

Why a 0.76 hazard ratio got a CRL: alpha, not effect size
HARMONi had two primary endpoints, PFS and OS, and the statistical plan split the 5% type I error between them and across interim looks. By the time of the primary OS analysis, the threshold left for OS was p<0.0448. The result was HR 0.79 with p=0.057, so the trial failed on OS, full stop. Summit then showed longer follow-up with HR 0.78 at nominal p=0.0332 and later 0.76, but a nominal p-value is one computed after the pre-specified test has already been spent; it carries no error control, because with enough later looks some cut will cross 0.05 by chance. FDA's letter says exactly that: PFS without a statistically significant OS gain does not establish effectiveness here. The second problem is applicability: most patients were enrolled in China, and the western subgroup's interval (0.52 to 1.10) crosses 1, so the agency cannot tell whether US patients benefit. The trap for you as an analyst is anchoring on the point estimate. When a company leads with 'consistent hazard ratio' and 'nominal p', find the pre-specified boundary and ask whether the test that counted was passed. Apply the same check to any pivotal with dual primaries or interim looks on your list, including what a late-cut OS claim from HARMONi-3 would and would not prove.
