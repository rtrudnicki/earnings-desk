What mattered

Tech
Commerce puts overseas AI data centers under license, five countries named
BIS issued an interim final rule on Nov 16 requiring licenses to export advanced AI chips and servers to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and to any overseas data center serving companies headquartered in China or other arms-embargoed countries. Existing operators get 90 days from the effective date to obtain licenses. The rule also rescinds the January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule; reports differ on the effective date (Nov 19 or on Federal Register publication).
So what: Read: the hit lands on Southeast Asian colocation operators and the GPU-rental firms that sell compute to Chinese customers, and on Nvidia's sell-through to that channel right as it reports without Huang. Demand does not vanish; some of it relocates to US and Gulf capacity, which helps domestic data center landlords and power suppliers. Recommendation engines for e-commerce and social media are exempt, so the big US platforms' own overseas fleets are less exposed than the neoclouds.

Fed
Warsh warns the AI boom could disappoint; December hike stays fully priced

Energy

Take: Crude is flat today after a large November slide: Reuters has Brent down 22% and WTI down 18% this month as Gulf exports recover. The market is trading a 2027 surplus against tanker attacks in Hormuz, and the surplus is winning.
Crude
Goldman calls a 2 million b/d surplus for 2027, Brent to $78 by year-end
Goldman's outlook, reported Nov 16, has supply exceeding demand by 2 million b/d in 2027, Brent averaging $88 next year and ending 2027 at $78. Hormuz shipments are reported near pre-war levels. Goldman keeps near-term risk to the upside from shipping attacks and infrastructure damage; the IEA's reported 2027 surplus estimate is larger, about 3.8 million b/d.
So what: Read: with Brent near $92, the curve already sits close to Goldman's 2027 average, so the forecast confirms the move more than it extends it. Lower crude is the one disinflation input the Fed has into Dec 8-9. Losers are high-cost shale and service names; refiners and airlines gain if product cracks hold. An OPEC+ signal of restraint before its Nov 29 meeting is the upside risk.
