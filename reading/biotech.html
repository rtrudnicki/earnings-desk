Artiva ends AlloNK after two deaths, cuts 94% of staff, explores sale or liquidation
Artiva (ARTV) halted all AlloNK (AB-101) development including the planned Phase 3, cut about 94% of staff and will weigh a sale, merger or dissolution. Longer-term data showed durability of response below the company's own threshold. Death one: leukoencephalopathy in the Phase 2a basket trial, assessed by the investigator and an independent review as likely fludarabine neurotoxicity from the protocol lymphodepletion. Death two: sudden death about two months after dosing, cause undetermined, investigator-assessed as unrelated. The FDA asked Artiva to pause enrollment and dosing after the second death. Shares fell about 30% premarket.
Read-through: Third autoimmune cell therapy program with fatal events after Novartis and BMS, and the second allogeneic shutdown this month after Caribou (October 7). Read it as a durability failure first and a safety story second: without durable remission off drug, an NK product cannot carry any treatment-related mortality in lupus or myositis. Watch how Kyverna and Cabaletta report lymphodepletion-attributable events from here. On your list, IMMX's NXC-201 uses flu/cy lymphodepletion in frail AL amyloidosis patients; fludarabine neurotoxicity is now a question every CAR-T management team will get.

One level deeper

Reading a death that is 'unrelated to the drug' in an autoimmune cell therapy trial
Artiva's two deaths were attributed to fludarabine neurotoxicity and to an undetermined sudden death, and both were assessed as unrelated to AlloNK itself. That attribution is correct and also beside the point, and the distinction is the lesson. FDA licenses a regimen, not a molecule: lymphodepletion with fludarabine and cyclophosphamide is written into the protocol and would be written into the label, so a fludarabine death is a treatment death for benefit-risk purposes. The acceptable treatment mortality is set by the comparator population, and in lupus or myositis the comparator is a chronic disease managed with drugs that rarely kill in a two-month window, so the tolerable number is close to zero, unlike relapsed lymphoma where the same event would be absorbed. That is why the FDA asked for a pause after the second death even though the investigator called it unrelated. The second trap is durability: Artiva's own threshold for continuing was durable response off drug, and it missed, which means the program could not earn the risk it was already carrying; a cell therapy in autoimmunity is only worth its lymphodepletion if it produces treatment-free remission, otherwise a B-cell depleting antibody delivers the same thing without the inpatient week. When you screen the remaining names (Kyverna, Cabaletta, Cartesian's mRNA CAR-T that skips lymphodepletion by design), ask three things: what lymphodepletion the protocol uses and at what fludarabine dose, whether lymphodepletion-attributable SAEs are reported separately from product-attributable ones, and what fraction of responders are off all immunosuppression at 12 months. A company that answers the third question with CR rates at month three has not answered it. Finally, note the funding signal: Caribou could not raise for a Phase 3 in lymphoma three weeks ago and Artiva could not justify one in autoimmunity today, so allogeneic cell therapy is being repriced on cost of capital as much as on data.
