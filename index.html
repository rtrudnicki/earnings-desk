The read

The swing. Nvidia: it opened up about 2% premarket and reversed to down more than 4% after an HSBC downgrade to hold; a recovery in that one stock is what would turn the indexes back up.

  • PPI: final demand 0.3% m/m against 0.4% expected and 6.5% y/y against 6.6%, but September revised up to 1.4% and the measure without foods, energy and trade services rose 0.5% against 0.3% expected.
  • Stocks: futures added to gains right after the print (S&P 500 ETF +0.6% at 07:35 CT), then reversed after the open; CNBC at 09:40 CT had the Nasdaq -1.7%, the S&P 500 -1% and the Dow -0.1% (about 70 points).
  • Nvidia: down more than 4% Friday after more than 4% Thursday, HSBC cut it to hold, and earnings are due Tuesday under an interim CEO.
  • Rates: the 10-year Treasury yield rose nearly 4 basis points to 5.14% after the report, above Thursday's 5.13%; the dollar index is at its highest since April 2025.
  • Oil: Brent $96.80 (+1.0%) and WTI $89.21 (+0.5%) at about 09:07 CT, still rising after the President's Iran post.
  • Fed: a December 9 hike stays fully priced (about 87% for a quarter point, about 13% for a half point as of the morning reading); Bank of America now expects quarter-point hikes in December, January and March to a 4.50% to 4.75% peak.

Pay attention to

  1. Nvidia through the afternoon: it is down more than 4% for a second day and the Nasdaq is following it, so whether it holds Thursday's intraday low decides if the index loss stays near 1% or widens.
  2. The 10-year yield against 5.14%: a further rise with stocks already falling would say the bond market is trading the hot 0.5% core reading, not the soft headline.
  3. Fed speakers still to come (Daly late morning, Jefferson at 18:00 CT): the market prices only about 13% for a half-point hike, so any open mention of one moves rates.
  4. Brent near $97 and the dollar at its highest since April 2025: an Iran headline or a Japanese move on the yen past 160 would hit rates and stocks before anything left on the calendar.
  5. Friday positioning: the S&P 500 came in up about 2.9% for the week, so a weak last hour would show funds cutting exposure ahead of Tuesday's Nvidia earnings and next week's retail sales.
