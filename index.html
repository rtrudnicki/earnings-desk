The read

Higher low confidence

A relief bounce after the worst week since June, with S&P futures up about 0.7% and Nasdaq futures up about 1.3% on the China beat and Brent off 1.5% to 2%, but no US data to anchor it, so the chip tape and oil decide whether it holds.

Swing: Oil: Brent near $107 after a tanker hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz and the White House waived some Russia sanctions; a move back above $110 reverses the bounce and puts the 10-year back toward 5.3%.

  • Friday's selloff was AI financing, not macro: CoreWeave fell 12% with more than $51bn of debt, the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell an eighth straight day, the Nasdaq lost 4.5% on the week.
  • Rates: the 10-year sits near 5.07% to 5.09% after spiking to 5.33% on Wednesday's CPI; Treasuries extended their rally overnight as oil slipped.
  • Fed: blackout started Saturday ahead of the October 27-28 meeting; FedWatch prices about a 16-17% chance of an October hike and roughly 89-90% for December from the current 3.75-4.00% range.
  • China: Q3 GDP 4.5% and retail sales 1.1% both beat, the yuan hit 6.68, its strongest since July 2022, supportive for risk at the margin.
  • The US calendar is empty of hard data all week; the only scheduled items are housing (NAHB today, starts tomorrow), claims Thursday and flash PMIs Friday, so earnings carry the tape.

Pay attention to

  1. Brent against $110 at the US open: it drove Friday's late rally in energy and the overnight bond bid, and a reversal higher is the fastest way to lose the futures gain.
  2. Semiconductors and CoreWeave at the open: the Philadelphia semiconductor index has fallen eight sessions running and whether it bounces with futures tells you if Friday was capitulation or a pause.
  3. The 10-year around 5.07%: a close back under 5.05% with oil soft would confirm the Treasury rally; the $13bn 20-year reopening Wednesday is the real test.
  4. NAHB at 09:00 CT: the only US print; a sub-30 reading would be the lowest since 2012 and feed the growth-scare side of the stagflation trade.
  5. Bank earnings today (Fifth Third, Zions, Western Alliance) for credit and deposit cost commentary after a 25 bp hike cycle restart; Netflix Tuesday and Meta, Tesla, Texas Instruments and Lam Wednesday are where AI capex gets re-priced.
