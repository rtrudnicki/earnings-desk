One level deeper

When the high dose loses to the mid dose: reading Kymera's 64 / 72 / 61
KT-621 posted EASI reductions of 64%, 72% and 61% at low, mid and high dose against 27% on placebo. The instinct is to quote the mid dose, and several sell-side notes did. That is the wrong number to carry. A degrader's effect depends on how much target protein is removed, and Kymera's Phase 1 work showed STAT6 knockdown above 90% across its tested dose range (from the earlier program disclosures, not rechecked today). If all three Phase 2b doses sit on the plateau of that curve, the true dose-response is flat, and the three arms are three draws from the same distribution. The spread between them then measures sampling noise, and with arm sizes undisclosed you should assume it is large. Picking the best of three arms after the fact is a winner's-curse estimate: on replication it regresses toward the pooled mean, which is why Wells Fargo's pooled 31-point placebo-adjusted EASI-75 against dupilumab's roughly 34 is the honest comparison, not Oppenheimer's 39 against 28. The alternative reading is a true inverted-U, which heterobifunctional degraders can show through the hook effect when excess drug saturates target and ligase separately; that would be worse news, since it narrows the Phase 3 dose window, and management's own cross-check (high-dose patients tracking the mid dose in the open-label extension) argues against it. The practical rule: when a Phase 2b has no monotonic dose-response, value the program on the pooled active arms, expect the Phase 3 effect to land below the best arm, and ask which dose goes forward and why before paying for the headline number.
