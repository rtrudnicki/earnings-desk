The read

The swing. Whether the 10-year holds below 4.90% through Bowman's 11:15 CT remarks; a break higher turns the rate shock into an equity problem for the second day running.

  • October payrolls +165k against +65k consensus with +84k of upward revisions; unemployment down to 4.1% on a growing labor force.
  • Average hourly earnings +0.4% m/m and +3.1% y/y, both above forecast, removing the real-wage argument against a December hike.
  • Fed funds futures price about 77-80% odds of a quarter-point hike at the December 8-9 meeting, up from about 66-75% before the print.
  • Michigan sentiment beat at 51.0 with year-ahead inflation expectations falling to 4.3%, the only dovish print of the morning.
  • U-6 rose 0.3 point to 7.9% on more involuntary part-time work, the one line the dove camp can cite.

Pay attention to

  1. The 10-year yield against 4.90%: it rose to about 4.85-4.87% after the print and a further leg higher is what would knock equities a second day.
  2. Bowman at 11:15 CT (University of Mississippi, supervision and regulation topic): any aside on December is the only scheduled Fed voice today after a print that changed the odds.
  3. Nasdaq futures were up about 1.5% premarket while Dow futures fell about 0.9%; whether the AI dip-buy holds into the close tells you if Thursday's rout was a one-day event.
  4. Front-end repricing: traders pulled the expected final hike forward to April from June, so watch 2-year yields and the shape of the December-to-March strip.
  5. Consumer credit at 14:00 CT and the Baker Hughes rig count at 12:00 CT are the only remaining prints; neither is likely to move the tape.
