One level deeper

Why conjunctivitis in Kymera's KT-621 data is both the best and the worst line in the release
KT-621 degrades STAT6, the transcription factor that IL-4 and IL-13 signal through, so it should copy dupilumab's biology from inside the cell. Dupilumab's signature side effect in atopic dermatitis is conjunctivitis, thought to come from IL-13 blockade thinning conjunctival goblet cells and mucin. BROADEN2 showed conjunctivitis at 19.7%, 13.1% and 11.1% across low, mid and high dose, with one analysis of the slides counting 0 of 59 placebo patients on the two reported terms. Bull reading: an on-target class effect is pharmacodynamic proof that an oral pill is hitting the pathway as hard as an antibody. Bear reading: the pitch that a degrader would be dupilumab's efficacy without its baggage just lost a leg, and the Phase 3 safety database will be read for eye events first. Note the dose order runs backwards (highest rate at the lowest dose). With about 60 patients per arm, a 9-point spread is three or four patients, so do not build a dose-response story in either direction. The same arithmetic applies to the efficacy comparison: 62% EASI-75 against 22% placebo is a 40-point gap from a Phase 2b, while dupilumab's Phase 3 gaps ran in the low-to-mid 30s (my recall of SOLO 1 and 2, check the label), and Phase 2b effect sizes shrink in Phase 3 more often than they hold. The habit to build: when a safety signal matches the mechanism, treat it as real at any sample size and ask what it does to the commercial claim; when a dose trend contradicts the mechanism, treat it as noise until a bigger trial repeats it.
