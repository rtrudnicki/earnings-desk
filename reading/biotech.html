GSK's Hibsago (bepirovirsen) approved for chronic hepatitis B: the first functional-cure label sets the bar for ABUS and ASMB
Announced Oct 20, 19:58 ET, under Priority Review and Breakthrough. Label: adults without cirrhosis, on NrtI therapy for at least 6 months with suppressed HBV DNA, and HBsAg at or below 3,000 IU/mL; a 6-month course added to NrtI. Pooled B-Well 1/2: functional cure 19% (233/1,220) vs 0/614 on placebo; 26% (200/768) in the HBsAg at or below 1,000 IU/mL subgroup. Boxed warnings for on-treatment hepatic flares (ALT >20-40x ULN) and thrombocytopenia; contraindicated with platelets <100 x10^9/L. Already approved in Japan.
Read-through: Read: this is the comparator every HBV functional-cure program, including Arbutus's imdusiran (siRNA) combination and Assembly's portfolio, now gets measured against: about 1 in 5 overall, 1 in 4 in low-HBsAg patients, with a boxed warning. A program that only works in the low-HBsAg subgroup now competes with an approved drug in exactly that subgroup. A clean safety profile is the opening, because a boxed hepatic-flare warning limits uptake in community hepatology. Ionis gets the royalty. Watch AASLD (Nov 5-9) for how competitors frame their numbers against 19% and 26%.

The draft was published Oct 16. One level deeper

HBV functional cure: why the HBsAg baseline cutoff decides every cross-trial comparison
Functional cure means HBsAg loss with undetectable HBV DNA that lasts off all therapy, usually measured 24 weeks after treatment stops. It is rare because the virus keeps a reservoir (cccDNA plus integrated DNA) that NrtIs suppress but do not clear. The single best predictor of whether any drug achieves it is baseline HBsAg. Hibsago shows this inside one program: 19% at HBsAg at or below 3,000 IU/mL and 26% at or below 1,000. The low-HBsAg group is about 45% of diagnosed patients, per GSK. So when a small biotech reports, say, 25% functional cure, the first question is what the HBsAg ceiling was. A Phase 2 enrolled at below 1,000, or below 100, is a different population from B-Well, and its rate should be compared with 26% or higher, not with 19%. The second question is durability: how many weeks off treatment, and whether NrtIs were stopped at all. The third is safety, now that the approved standard carries boxed warnings for ALT flares and thrombocytopenia. Some flares are on-target (immune clearance of infected hepatocytes), so a 'cleaner' competitor may also be a weaker one. Apply this to ABUS and ASMB data at AASLD: rebuild the comparison on matched HBsAg strata before reading any headline percentage.
