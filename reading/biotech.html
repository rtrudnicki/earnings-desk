Capricor: second CRL for deramiocel, and FDA publicly alleges data integrity failures in HOPE-3

One level deeper

Why a BIMO finding is worse than a failed endpoint
Every BLA review has two tracks: the statistical review of the data and the Bioresearch Monitoring (BIMO) inspection of how the data were generated. Investors model the first and ignore the second. Capricor shows why that is a mistake. FDA's statement lists three findings in HOPE-3: changes to the statistical analysis plan that were not authorized, blinding violations, and protocol violations. Each one attacks the same thing, the guarantee that the analysis was fixed before anyone could see which arm was winning. If the SAP changed after unblinding was possible, the reported p-value no longer controls type 1 error, and no reanalysis can restore it, which is what 'incapable of post hoc remediation' means. That explains how the company can say the trial met its primary endpoint while FDA says it failed the prespecified one: they are describing two different analysis plans. The tell was available early. When a sponsor announces a win on an endpoint that differs from the registry's original primary, or amends the SAP late in a small rare-disease trial, pull the ClinicalTrials.gov history tab and compare versions before you trust the topline. Apply that check to every single-pivotal filing you cover; with 8 patients in IRD's LCA5 trial, one protocol deviation is 12.5% of the dataset.
