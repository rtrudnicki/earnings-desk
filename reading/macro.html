What mattered

Tech
Midterm data-center backlash, not a print, drove the AI rout
Thursday Nov 5 the S&P 500 fell more than 4%, its worst day since April 2025, the SOX fell 12%, Nvidia 9%, AMD and Micron about 17% each. Voters sided against data centers in 40 of 41 ballot measures across 10 states (21 in Ohio). Sanders posted 'National data center moratorium now'; Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa pledged a moratorium on all approvals until the legislature acts; Ohio's Amy Acton wants operators to cover 100% of utility costs. Wells Fargo puts 27% of planned US capacity in Texas.
So what: The budgets (roughly $720 to 740B of 2026 hyperscaler capex) are intact; the fight moved to siting and power cost allocation. 2027 projects are largely under way, so the hit lands on 2028+ additions: UBS estimates about 30% of expected 2028 capacity additions are at risk. Losers are neoclouds, power equipment and Texas-heavy developers; winners are entitled sites in states without a moratorium pledge and utilities that get rate recovery written into law. My read: outright bans are unlikely at the federal level; cost-shifting rules are the realistic outcome and they compress developer returns rather than halt the build.

Energy

Take: Crude's weekly fall of 12% or more is a deal-hope trade against a tape where tankers are still being hit. OPEC+ is a non-event until Nov 29.
OPEC
Seven OPEC+ members hold December output flat; 2027 quotas decided Nov 29
Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman kept December targets at September levels in a Nov 1 online meeting. The UAE left OPEC in May. Most members pump well below target because Gulf exports remain disrupted by the Iran war. The full 21-country group meets Nov 29 on 2027 quotas.
So what: Quotas are not binding when physical exports are blocked, so OPEC+ cannot cushion a post-deal price drop by cutting; the Nov 29 meeting is the first real test of Saudi willingness to defend $90 Brent once Hormuz flows normalize. Energy equities (XLE down 11% on the week) are pricing that outcome before it exists.

Data centers

Take: The budgets did not move this week; the permission to spend them did. Texas, with 27% of planned US capacity, now has a governor-elect pledging an approvals moratorium, and 40 of 41 local ballot measures went against data centers. Hyperscalers kept buying sites anyway.
New build
Google buys Crusoe's stalled Cheyenne site, commits $17B to a 2.7 GW campus
Announced Nov 6. Google's subsidiary Jupiter Star Holdings takes over the 716-acre south Laramie County site Crusoe began with Tallgrass in 2025 (formerly Project Jade, now Project Tembo). Four buildings, 2.7 GW design capacity including reserves, initial operations 2H 2028, full completion 2031. Crusoe cited construction delays and cost.
So what: A hyperscaler absorbing a developer's stalled project is the pattern to expect: balance-sheet buyers win entitled, power-ready land as developers' financing tightens. Wyoming did not vote on moratoria, which is part of why the site is worth $17B to Google now.
https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/google-is-customer-behind-27gw-data-center-camp
Trend
Sell-side sizes the moratorium risk: 30% of 2028 additions, 165 GW in pledge states
UBS estimates about 30% of expected 2028 US capacity additions are at risk from post-midterm pauses, with Texas alone 18 GW, against a 2028 need of 76 GW and 50 GW of visible supply. Modo Energy counts 165 GW of the 633 GW it tracks (26%) in states whose incoming governor backs an immediate moratorium. TrendForce notes most 2027 projects are already under construction.
So what: If 2028 supply is cut while demand estimates stand, scarcity value shifts to permitted, interconnected sites in permissive states and to existing operators' leases (colo pricing up). The estimates are not comparable to each other, and all three assume pledges become law.
https://finimize.com/content/ubs-flags-a-post-midterms-speed-bump-for-data-centers
New build
Google adds a $15B Malaysia AI campus with more than 1 GW of renewables
Announced Nov 4; Google will add more than 1 GW of local renewable generation alongside the campus. Coverage from the region notes growing protests over water and power use in Southeast Asia.
So what: Capacity the US fights over gets sited abroad; expect more Gulf and Southeast Asia announcements as a hedge against state-level US delays. The backlash is following the build offshore too.

Opportunity radar

Entitled, interconnected data-center sites in non-moratorium states just got scarcer
Acquire or option land that already holds a will-serve letter or signed interconnection agreement plus local zoning in states whose incoming governor made no moratorium pledge (Wyoming, Oklahoma, Louisiana, Indiana, the Dakotas), including developer-orphaned projects like the Casper, WY gigawatt site Prometheus Hyperscale walked away from this week. The buyer is a hyperscaler or neocloud that needs 2028 capacity it can no longer count on in Texas or Ohio; Google just paid $17B into exactly this kind of stalled site in Cheyenne.
Why now: Nov 3 moved 2028 supply risk from a forecast to a vote: UBS says about 30% of expected 2028 additions are at risk, 40 of 41 local measures went against data centers, and the Texas and Ohio governors-elect pledged moratoria. The permitted-site inventory outside those states did not grow overnight, so the spread between raw land and entitled land should widen.
Upside: The prize is the entitlement spread, not the data center. Site-level markups from raw land to power-ready entitled land have run from a few million to tens of millions of dollars per 100 MW block in recent trades; the relevant number is what a buyer who has lost a Texas site pays to keep a 2028 delivery date.
First step: Pull the Modo Energy and SemiAnalysis state exposure lists, cross them against your SiteScore parcel screen (Canadian County OK is already live), and call the Casper, WY landowner who said they intend to continue without Prometheus to learn what the interconnection queue position and water rights actually are.
