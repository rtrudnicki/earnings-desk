REPORT (anonymous sources, disputed by HHS): FDA has shelved the priority voucher programme that COMP360's 1-2 month review depends on
Reuters reported on Nov 12 that the FDA has shelved the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot: no voucher awarded since Makary left in May, the political-appointee council that ran it dormant, and one source saying companies were told the agency is not moving forward and submitted applications do not advance. Per the same report, 22 drugs received vouchers, 12 are approved, and for the 9 still in the programme career FDA scientists have resumed oversight of review timelines. The status of the three psychedelic voucher holders (psilocybin for TRD, psilocybin for MDD, methylone for PTSD) is not specified. HHS spokesperson Emily Hilliard said on the record that the pilot is ongoing and the FDA is reviewing feedback from its June 4 hearing. Compass completed its rolling NDA on Nov 9. I read the Reuters piece secondhand through Pharmacy Times and STAT, and found no statement from Compass.
The voucher is a pilot with no statutory or PDUFA commitment behind it, so nothing binds the FDA to a 1-2 month clock. If COMP360 falls back to a Breakthrough-backed priority review, the goal date is roughly 8 months from the Nov 9 submission (60-day filing period plus 6 months), about July 2027, and DEA rescheduling of a Schedule I drug adds up to 90 days after approval. That arithmetic is mine, not the company's. Sourcing quality: Reuters exclusive, anonymous sources, contradicted on the record by HHS. Treat it as probable drift in the timeline, not a confirmed change.
Thesis read: Your thesis pays roughly fair value for the readout plus rolling NDA optionality, and the company's 1H27 launch guide assumes voucher speed. This report does nothing to the approval odds; it attacks timing. A slip from 1H27 to late 2027 costs about two quarters of discounting on the rNPV and brings a financing ahead of launch into play. It is not a thesis break. What you need next is Compass's own words on the review clock, or a Day-74 letter with a goal date. Until then, do not model the 1-2 month review as base case.

One level deeper

What a priority voucher is worth when nothing binds the FDA to it: rebuilding the COMP360 clock from statute
Separate the review commitments that are law or negotiated PDUFA goals from those that are agency pilots. Breakthrough Therapy designation is statutory: it gives rolling submission and makes priority review likely, and COMP360 keeps it whatever happens to the voucher. Priority review for a new molecular entity under PDUFA means a goal date 6 months after the 60-day filing date, so about 8 months from the day the last module lands; for a Nov 9 submission that is roughly July 9, 2027. The Commissioner's National Priority Voucher promised 1 to 2 months, but it was created by announcement in June 2025, run by a council of political appointees, and appears in no user-fee agreement, so a sponsor has no goal date to hold the agency to and no recourse when the council stops meeting. Then add the step equity models often skip for a Schedule I drug: after approval the DEA must issue an interim final rule rescheduling the product within 90 days, and states then follow on their own calendars, so first commercial sale trails approval by up to a quarter. Stack those and a non-voucher path reads approval mid-2027 and launch in 2H 2027, against company guidance of a 1H 2027 launch that only works at voucher speed. The lesson generalises: when a guided date rests on a discretionary programme, haircut it to the statutory clock and treat the faster date as upside. The same test applies to any accelerated-approval or pilot-pathway name: ask which document commits the FDA to the date, and if the answer is a press release, the date is a hope.
